\chapter{Mott localisation in the normal phase}\label{chap:normal}

In this chapter, we study the normal phase, namely the phase characterised by the absence of any spontaneous symmetry breaking mechanism.

We show that the non-local pairing induces a renormalisation of the hopping amplitude, leading to a Mott localisation effect. The chapter is organised as follows. In Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-phase-theory} we discuss the symmetries of the normal phase. In Secs.~\ref{sec:normal-hf}, \ref{sec:normal-hf-hamiltonian} we derive the HF approximation to the EHM in the normal phase. In Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-sceq-analysis} we analyse the self-consistency equations and derive constraints on the self-consistent parameters identified by the Hartree-Fock method. In Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-numeric} we present the result of numeric simulations.

\section{The normal phase and its  symmetries}\label{sec:normal-phase-theory}

In the normal phase no symmetry is broken, as summarised in Tab.~\ref{tab:symmetry-groups}. Therefore, the HF approximation to the hamiltonian must exhibit the full $\mathrm{U}(2) \otimes \mathrm{C}_4 \otimes \mathrm{T}_1^{(x)} \otimes \mathrm{T}_1^{(y)}$ symmetry discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:ehm-syms}.

We anticipate here that we will actually allow for $\mathrm{C}_4 \SSB \mathrm{C}_2$, namely, breaking of planar rotational symmetry. As will result from our discussion, the normal phase does not break $\mathrm{C}_4$ even if we allow it to. Nevertheless, we carry out this general derivation because breaking of planar rotational symmetry will be observed in Chap.~\ref{chap:sc} in the context of superconductivity, and the computations carried out for the normal phase will be re-used.
 
Translational invariance implies the following result:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-<cc>}
	\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \rangle = \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}'} \delta_{\sigma=\sigma'} \langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle \, .
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}]
	
	Consider the generic EV on the left side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}
	\[
		\big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \big\rangle \, ,
	\]
	for two momenta $\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{k}'$. Moving to real space through an $\mathcal{F}$-transform, we get
	\[
		\big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \big\rangle \Feq \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} \sum_{\mathbf{r}' \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \left(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{k}' \cdot \mathbf{r}' \right)} \big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma'} \big\rangle \, ,
	\]
	where we used the convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier-convention}. Let now $\Delta \mathbf{r} \equiv \mathbf{r}' - \mathbf{r}$. Translational invariance implies that the function
	\[
		g(\Delta \mathbf{r}, \sigma, \sigma') = \big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\Delta\mathbf{r}\sigma'} \big\rangle
	\]
	is independent of $\mathbf{r}$. Thus we have:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \big\rangle &\Feq \sum_{\Delta\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \mathbf{k}' \cdot \Delta\mathbf{r}} g(\Delta \mathbf{r}, \sigma, \sigma') \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \left(\mathbf{k} - \mathbf{k}'\right) \cdot \mathbf{r}} \\
		&= \sum_{\Delta\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \mathbf{k}' \cdot \Delta\mathbf{r}} g(\Delta \mathbf{r}, \sigma, \sigma') \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}'} \, ,
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where we used the identity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-delta}. Then the EV of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>} vanishes for $\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k}'$. 
	
	Recall now Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-HV-wick-int1}. Upon substituting $\bm{\delta} \to \Delta \mathbf{r}$, we obtain that $g(\Delta \mathbf{r},\sigma,\overline{\sigma})=0$ for a phase that is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-symmetric. Then, the EV of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>} is non-zero only if $\sigma=\sigma'$. This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}.
\end{tabproof}

Translational invariance and spin rotational symmetry imply that the site occupation is independent of site and spin indices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-<n_i>}
	\ev{\hat n_{i\sigma}} = n \, ,
\end{equation}
with $n \in [0,1]$.

We define the single-state occupation operator in reciprocal space:
\[
\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\]
and its EV
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}
	u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\rangle \, ,
	\qquad
	u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^* \, .
\end{equation}
The relation on the right follows from the fact that $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is hermitian by construction. Note that $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is not the Fourier-transform of $\hat n_{i\sigma}$. Finally, using the functions $\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}$ of Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}, we define the harmonics
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics}
	u^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \equiv \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\rangle \, .
\end{equation}
These harmonics are the HFPs of the normal phase, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics} is their self-consistency equation.

\section{Application of Hartree-Fock methods to the normal phase}\label{sec:normal-hf}

Applying the HF methods to the interactions $\hat H_U$ and $\hat H_V$, from Tabs.~\ref{tab:HU-sel-rules} and \ref{tab:HV-sel-rules}, we know that in the normal phase the allowed contractions are:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\hat H_U 
	&&\text{Hartree contractions,} \\
	&\hat H_V \text{ (o.s. sector)}
	&&\text{Hartree contractions,} \\
	&\hat H_V \text{ (s.s. sector)}
	&&\text{Hartree and Fock contractions.} \\
\end{aligned}	
\]
As we will see in detail and is summarised in Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-RWCs}, Hartree contractions give a chemical potential shift, while the Fock contractions renormalise the bare bands.

\subsection{Effects of the Hartree terms: chemical potential renormalisation}\label{subsec:normal-hartree-mu}

In this section we show that the Hartree channels of $\hat H_U$ and $\hat H_V$ reduce to multiples of the number operator. Applying HF methods, we get the decomposition of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU}, \eqref{eq:mft-HV}. Specifically, the Hartree channels are given by
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_U &\HFsimeq U \sum_i \left(
		\hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\langle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} \big\rangle + \big\langle \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\rangle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} - \big\langle \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\rangle  \big\langle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} \big\rangle 
	\right) \, , \\
	\hat H_V &\HFsimeq -V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_{\sigma,\sigma'} \left(
		\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\langle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} \big\rangle + \big\langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\rangle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} - \big\langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\rangle  \big\langle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} \big\rangle
	\right) + (\text{Fock channel}) \, .
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing the two terms, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HU+HV-hartree-approx}
	\hat H_U + \hat H_V \HFsimeq -n(2zV-U) \hat N - \left(E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})} + E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})}\right) + (\text{Fock channel}) \, ,
\end{equation}
where we introduces the ``Hartree contraction energy'', namely a shift to energy coming from the scalar terms of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU}, \eqref{eq:mft-HV}:
\begin{align}
	E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= - U \sum_{i \in\mathcal{L}} \big\langle \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\rangle  \big\langle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} \big\rangle \, , \label{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit} \\
	E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_{\sigma,\sigma'} \big\langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\rangle  \big\langle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} \big\rangle \, . \label{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec-implicit}
\end{align}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU+HV-hartree-approx}, we see that the chemical potential is shifted as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-mu-shift}
	\tilde{\mu} \equiv \mu + n(2zV-U) \, ,
\end{equation}
as is reported in Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-RWCs}. This result is valid for all the other phases discussed in this thesis.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU+HV-hartree-approx}: HF approximation of $\hat H_U + \hat H_V$ in Hartree channel]
	
	We analyse $\hat H_U$ and $\hat H_V$ separately.

	\paragraph{Local repulsion.}
	
	In the normal phase, the local repulsion $\hat H_U$ decomposes in the Hartree channel as is specified in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU},
	\[
		\hat H_U \HFsimeq
		U \sum_{i} \left(
			\langle 
				\hat n_{i\uparrow}
			\rangle \hat n_{i\downarrow} + \hat n_{i\uparrow} \langle 
				\hat n_{i\downarrow}
			\rangle - \langle 
				\hat n_{i\downarrow}
			\rangle \langle 
				\hat n_{i\uparrow}
				\rangle
		\right) \, .
	\]
	Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<n_i>}, we obtain:
	\begin{align}
		\hat H_U &\HFsimeq
		nU \sum_i \left(
			 \hat n_{i\uparrow} + \hat n_{i\downarrow}
		\right)
		- E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})}\\
		&= nU \times \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, . \label{eq:normal-HU-hartree-approx}
	\end{align}
	
	\paragraph{Non-local attraction.}
	
	The non-local attraction is divided in two sectors, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-HV(ss)+HV(os)}. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HV}, its Hartree-like terms are:
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq 
		\overbrace{
			-V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
				\langle 
				\hat n_{i\sigma}
				\rangle \hat n_{j\sigma} + \hat n_{i\sigma} \langle 
				\hat n_{j\sigma}
				\rangle - \langle 
				\hat n_{i\sigma}
				\rangle \langle 
				\hat n_{j\sigma}
				\rangle
			\right)
		}^{\mathrm{s.s.}} \\
		\underbrace{
			-V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
				\langle
				\hat n_{i\sigma}
				\rangle \hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{i\sigma} \langle 
				\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
				\rangle
				- \langle 
				\hat n_{i\sigma}
				\rangle \langle 
				\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
				\rangle
			\right)
		}_{\mathrm{o.s.}} \, +\, 
		(\text{Fock channel}) \, . \label{eq:normal-hartree-HV}
	\end{multline}
	Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<n_i>}, we get
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-hartree-HV-int1}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq  \underbrace{
			-nV \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
			\hat n_{j\sigma} + \hat n_{i\sigma}
			\right)
		}_{\mathrm{s.s.}}
		\underbrace{
			-nV \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
			\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{i\sigma}
			\right)
		}_{\mathrm{o.s.}} 
		- E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
		+ (\text{Fock channel}) \, ,
	\end{equation}
	Both sums give a number operator. Note that we sum over NNs: each site is connected to $z=4$ sites. Including this combinatorial factor, we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-hartree-approx}
		\hat H_V \simeq -2nzV \times \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} + 
		(\text{Fock channel}) \, .
	\end{equation}
	Summing Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-approx} and \eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-approx}, we obtain Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU+HV-hartree-approx}.
\end{tabproof}

The contraction energies of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit}, \eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec-implicit} read
\begin{align}
	E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= -n^2 U \label{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec} \\
	E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
	&= L_x L_y \times 2zn^2 V \, . \label{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec}
\end{align}
where $z=4$ is the lattice coordination number.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec}, \eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec}: Hartree contraction energies]
	
	Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<n_i>} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit}, we obtain
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= -U \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} n \\
		&= -n^2 U \, ,
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec}. Doing the same in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec-implicit}, we get
	\[
	\begin{aligned}	
		E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\langle 
		\hat n_{i\sigma}
		\rangle \langle 
		\hat n_{j\sigma}
		\rangle
		+
		\langle 
		\hat n_{i\sigma}
		\rangle \langle 
		\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
		\rangle
		\right) \\
		&= 2V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma n^2 \\
		&= L_x L_y \times 2zn^2 V \, , 
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where in last line we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:square-NN-ghost-sum}. We obtained Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec}.
\end{tabproof}

\subsection{Effects of the Fock terms: bare bands renormalisation}\label{subsec:normal-fock-bands}
 
From the Wick's decomposition of $\hat H_V$ (see Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HV}), the only Fock term allowed in the normal phase comes from the same-spin sector. See Tab.~\ref{tab:HV-sel-rules} for a summary. Explicitly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-fock}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + 
	V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\langle
		\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
		\rangle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} + 
		\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
		\langle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} \rangle - \langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
		\rangle \langle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} \rangle
	\right) \, .
\end{equation}
We notice that the \textit{plus} sign in front of the displayed term comes from the \textit{minus} sign in Fock term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hf-wick-hfc}, applied to the interaction $-V$.

Using the Fourier transform of $\hat H_V$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:HV-ss-fourier}, we obtain
\begin{multline}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) \\
	+ \frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\cos \left(
	\delta k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	\delta k_y
	\right)	
	\right]
	\langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, . \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int1}
\end{multline}
Here, the $2$ prefactor comes from the fact that the first two operators in square brackets in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock} are equal in reciprocal space. The contraction energy shift is given by
\begin{align}\label{eq:normal-V-fock-double-counting}
	E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \equiv \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\cos \left(
			\delta k_x
		\right)	+ \cos \left(
			\delta k_y
		\right)	
	\right]
	\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma}
	\rangle \langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} 
	\rangle \, .
\end{align}

By recalling the definition of $u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \langle \hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle$, the non local attraction is approximated, in Fock channel, by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + 
	V
	\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma
	\left(
		u^{(s^*)}_\sigma
		\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)*} + u^{(d)}_\sigma
		\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)*}
	\right)
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, ,
\end{equation}
where $u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}$ are the harmonics of the occupation $u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$, defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics}.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}: HF approximation of $\hat H_V$ in Fock channel]
	
	Consider Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int1}. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}, it becomes:
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel})\\ + 
		\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma \left( \cos 2 k_x + \cos 2 k_y	
		\right)
		\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}
		\rangle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, . \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int2}
	\end{multline}
	Let now $\mathbf{q}\equiv\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{q}'\equiv\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}$. A representation of this variables change is given in Fig.~\ref{fig:Kk-qq'}. Being (for $\ell=x,y$)
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\delta q_\ell &\equiv q_\ell - q_\ell' \\
		&= (K_\ell+k_\ell) - (K_\ell-k_\ell) \\
		&= 2k_\ell \, ,
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	we reduce Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int1} to
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) \\
		+ \frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
			\cos \left(
				\delta q_x
			\right)	+ \cos \left(
				\delta q_y
			\right)	
		\right]	
		\underbrace{\langle
			\hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger 
			\hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
		\rangle}_{u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, , \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int3}
	\end{multline}
	where we recognised $u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}$, as was defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:coscos-symmetries-decomposition}, we obtain
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) \\
		+ \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left(
			\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_\mathbf{q\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
			u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
		\right) \times V
		\sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}}
		\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, , \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int4}
	\end{multline}
	where we used the functions defined in Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}. We recognise the harmonics $u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics},
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-V-fock-int5}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + V \sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}
		\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, ,
	\end{equation}
	which is almost Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}. 
	
	To complete the proof, we recognise that in the normal phase inversion symmetry is not broken, thus
	\[
		u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} = u_{-\mathbf{q}\sigma} \, .
	\]
	This follows from the fact that in the normal phase the EV of the operator $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ must be inversion-symmetric. It follows that antisymmetric harmonics are zero,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-up=0}
		u_\sigma^{(p_\ell)} = 0
		\qq{for}
		\ell\in\lbrace x,y \rbrace \, .
	\end{equation}
	Using this observation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int5}, we obtain Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}.
\end{tabproof}

Bringing together Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU+HV-hartree-approx} and \eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}, we obtain that the quadratic HF approximation to the EHM, for the normal phase, depends on the renormalised chemical potential $\tilde{\mu}$, and the occupation harmonics $u^{(s^*)}$ and $u^{(d)}$. The chemical potential $\tilde{\mu}$ is fixed by density and determined during computations. To find the ground-state, we must determine self-consistently the two HFPs $u_\sigma^{(s^*)}$, $u_\sigma^{(d)}$ through the self-consistency Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics}, which is reported symmetry-resolved in Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-ehm-ugamma-self-consistencies}.

The operator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx} has the same algebraic structure of the kinetic operator $\hat H_t$ in reciprocal space, given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ht-Fourier}. We can collapse the two into one effective kinetic operator with renormalised bands. Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation}
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + u^{(s^*)}_\sigma V (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) + u^{(d)}_\sigma V (\cos k_x - \cos k_y) \, .
\end{equation}
In Fock channel, we have:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-H~t}
	\hat H_t + \hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + \underbrace{
		\sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	}_{\hat H_{\tilde{t}}} - E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, ,
\end{equation}
where we defined the effective kinetic operator, $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$. 

The renormalised bands of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation} are a sum of $s^*$ and $d$-wave components. Instead, bare bands as defined Eq.~\eqref{eq:bare-bands} are purely $s^*$-symmetric functions, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bands-vphi}
	\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = -2t (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) = -4t \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} \, .
\end{equation}
We define the symmetry-resolved hoppings,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-tS-td}
	\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}_\sigma \equiv t - \frac{u_\sigma^{(s^*)} V}{2}
	\qq{and}
	\tilde{t}^{(d)}_\sigma \equiv - \frac{u_\sigma^{(d)} V}{2} \, ,
\end{equation}
so that the bands renormalisation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation} can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation-vphi}
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv -4\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} -4\tilde{t}^{(d)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)} \, .
\end{equation}
We notice the formal similarity with Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-vphi}, showing how bands renormalisation reduces to a renormalisation of the hopping parameter $t$,
\[
	\text{$t$ ($s^*$-wave)}
	\quad\to\quad
	\text{$\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}_\sigma$ ($s^*$-wave) and $\tilde{t}^{(d)}_\sigma$ ($d$-wave)} \, .
\]
As we will discuss in Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-d-wave-hopping}, the presence of $\tilde{t}^{(d)} \neq 0$ implies a nematic state that breaks $\mathrm{C}_4$.

We conclude this section by proving that the Fock contraction energy is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-fock-Ec}
	E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} = L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\big|
			u_\sigma^{(s^*)}
		\big|^2 + \big|
			u_\sigma^{(d)}
		\big|^2
	\right) \, .
\end{equation}
Later, using spin degeneracy, we will omit the sum on $\sigma$ and the $1/2$ prefactor.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-Ec}: non-local Fock contraction energy]

	Recall Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-double-counting}. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}, it reduces to:
	\[
		E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} = \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{ \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\cos \left(
		\delta q_x
		\right)	+ \cos \left(
		\delta q_y
		\right)	
		\right]	
		\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{q} \sigma}
		\rangle \langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}' \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{q}' \sigma}
		\rangle \, .
	\]
	Inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}, we get:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left(
		\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)} u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
		\right) \left(
		\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*} u_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^*
		\right) \\
		&= L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma}
		\big|
			u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}
		\big|^2 \, ,
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	which, using the fact that $u_\sigma^{(p_\ell)}=0$, proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-Ec}.
\end{tabproof}

\section{The HF hamiltonian and its properties}\label{sec:normal-hf-hamiltonian}

Collecting all the various terms, the HF approximation to the EHM hamiltonian in the normal phase is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-Hhf}
	\hat H - \mu \hat N \HFsimeq \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \tilde{\mu} \hat N + E_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{N})} \, ,
\end{equation}
where in first line we included the all contraction energies from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec}, \eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec} and \eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-Ec} in $E_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{N})}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-Ec}
	E_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{N})} \equiv
	-\left(
	E_{\mathrm{H},U}^{(\mathrm{N})} + E_{\mathrm{H},V}^{(\mathrm{N})} +  E_{\mathrm{F},V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
	\right) \, .
\end{equation}
All dependence on the HFPs is inside the renormalised bands of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation} and the contributions to contraction energy.

Apart from energy shifts, this hamiltonian describes a perfectly degenerate Fermi gas whose bare bands are $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation}, and whose chemical potential is $\tilde{\mu}$ as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-mu-shift}. The latter is fixed self-consistently by setting the density; the former must be determined using self-consistency equations. The thermal state of the approximated hamiltonian obeys Fermi-Dirac statistics,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-<cc>-FD}
	\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \rangle = \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}'} \delta_{\sigma=\sigma'} f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \, ,
\end{equation}
being $f$ the Fermi-Dirac distribution,
\[
	f(\epsilon;\beta,\mu) \equiv \frac{1}{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1} \, .
\]

In next subsections, we discuss in detail the renormalisation of the hopping parameter in real space, with a focus on the meaning of the $d$-wave component, and derive the free energy and entropy densities for the ground-state.

\subsection{Nematicity: $d$-wave bands and anisotropic hopping}\label{subsec:normal-d-wave-hopping}

In Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-H~t} we defined $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$ in reciprocal space. In real space, $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$ takes the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_{\tilde{t}} &= \hat H_t + V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\langle
	\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
	\rangle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} + \hc
	\right] \\
	&= - \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma 
	\left[
	\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma} \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma} + \hc
	\right] \, .
\end{aligned}
\]
where we introduce the renormalised hopping $\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma}$,
\[
	\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma} \equiv
	\begin{cases}
		\displaystyle t - V \langle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \rangle
		\qquad
		&\text{if $i,j$ are NNs,} \\
		0 &\text{otherwise.}
	\end{cases}
\]
Due to translational invariance, the renormalised hopping $\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma}$ depends only on the difference $i-j$. Therefore we can use the decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-decomposition},
\[
	\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma} = \sum_\gamma \tilde{t}^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_{ij}^{(\gamma)} \, .
\]
The harmonics, given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-gamma}, are identified with $\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)}$, $\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}$ already introduced in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-tS-td}. From the same argument we know that $\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(p_x)}=\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(p_y)}=0$. Then, the renormalised hopping is explicitly given by
\begin{multline*}
	\tilde{t}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'\sigma} = \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)}}{2} \left[
		\delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}}
	\right] \\
	+ \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}}{2} \left[
		\delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}} - \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}} - \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}}
	\right] \, .
\end{multline*}
Let us define the direction-resolved renormalised hopping,
\begin{align}
	\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(x)} &\equiv \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)} + \tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}}{2} \, , \label{eq:normal-tx} \\
	\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(y)} &\equiv \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)} - \tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}}{2} \, . \label{eq:normal-ty}
\end{align}
This shows that, as anticipated, a finite $\tilde{t}^{(d)} \neq 0$ implies a nematic order characterised by the breaking of planar rotational symmetry, highlighted by the fact that kinetic operators in directions $x$ and $y$ differ. We can rewrite $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$ as:
\[
	\hat H_{\tilde{t}} = - \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(x)} \left(
			\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}\sigma} + \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}\sigma}
		\right) + \tilde{t}_\sigma^{(y)} \left(
			\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}\sigma} + \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}\sigma}
		\right)
	\right] \, .
\]
A schematics of anisotropic hopping is given in Fig.~\ref{fig:anisotropic-hopping}: for each bond linking two sites, the hopping parameter can differ, as the link direction is vertical or horizontal. We notice, however, that even in the presence of a non-zero $d$-wave hopping the density remains uniform.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\input{\GraphicsFolder/anisotropic-hopping}
	\caption{Graphic rendition of anisotropic hopping, with a $s^*$-wave component (symmetric by $\pi/2$ rotations) and a $d$-wave component (antisymmetric).}
	\label{fig:anisotropic-hopping}
\end{figure}

\begin{tabproof}[A $d$-wave hopping is compatible with uniform density]

	Consider the on-site occupation operator $\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} = \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}$. Applying a Fourier transform as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier-convention}, we obtain
	\[
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \stackrel{\mathcal{F}}{=} \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} e^{i (\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}') \cdot\mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \, .
	\]
	We compute the expectation value of the above operator and apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>-FD},
	\begin{align}
		\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}} &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} e^{i (\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}') \cdot\mathbf{r}} \langle
			\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma}
		\rangle \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \, . \label{eq:normal-<nr>}
	\end{align}
	The right-hand side of last line is independent of $\mathbf{r}$, therefore also $\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}}$ is.
	
	Note that we never used the actual symmetry of the renormalised bands $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}$, but only the fact that the thermal state obeys the Fermi-Dirac distribution. A perfectly degenerate Fermi gas is always translational invariant and thus cannot exhibit non-uniform charge density.
\end{tabproof}

From now on, we will omit the spin index $\sigma$ from both the renormalised bands and the renormalised hopping,
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \to \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}
	\qquad
	\forall \alpha
	\quad\colon\quad
	\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(\alpha)} \to \tilde{t}^{(\alpha)} \, .
\]
The normal phase does not break spin rotational invariance. Therefore the renormalised bands $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}$ must be independent of the spin index. Inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-tS-td}, we can
express $\tilde{t}^{(x)}$ and $\tilde{t}^{(y)}$ in terms of the HFPs $u^{(s^*)}$ and $u^{(d)}$
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\tilde{t}^{(x)} &\equiv t - \frac{u^{(s^*)} + u^{(d)}}{2} V \, , \\
	\tilde{t}^{(y)} &\equiv t - \frac{u^{(s^*)} - u^{(d)}}{2} V \, .
\end{aligned}
\]
Inverting these relations we obtain:
\begin{align}
	u^{(s^*)} &= \frac{
		\left(
		t-\tilde{t}^{(x)}
		\right) + \left(
		t-\tilde{t}^{(y)}
		\right)
	}{V} \, , \label{eq:normal-us*(tx,ty)}
	\\
	u^{(d)} &= \frac{\tilde{t}^{(x)} - \tilde{t}^{(y)}}{V} \, . \label{eq:normal-ud(tx,ty)}
\end{align}
It follows that if $u^{(d)}>0$, the effective hopping parameter is larger in direction $x$ with respect to direction $y$. If $u^{(d)}<0$, roles are inverted.

\subsection{The entropy density of the normal ground-state}\label{subsec:normal-s}

In this section, we compute the density of entropy of the ground state in the normal phase. Entropy density is given by
\[
	s^{(\mathrm{N})} = \frac{S^{(\mathrm{N})}}{L_x L_y} \, ,
\]
being $S^{(\mathrm{N})}$ the total entropy.

The HF hamiltonian of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-Hhf} describes a non-interacting Fermi gas. The entropy density of a free Fermi gas in its ground state \cite{grosso2014solid} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-s}
	s^{(\mathrm{N})} = -\frac{2k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \left[
		\left(
			1- f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right) \ln \left(
			1-f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right) + f(
			\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
			\ln f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\vphantom{A^A}
	\right] \, .
\end{equation}
The $2$ prefactor was included to account for spin degeneracy. The above equation can be written in another way:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-s-variant}
		s^{(\mathrm{N})} = -\frac{2k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \left[ \ln \left(
		1-f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right)
		- \beta  (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) 
	\right] \, .
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s-variant}: entropy density for the normal phase]

	The following identity holds,
	\begin{align}
		\ln \left(
		1-\frac{1}{e^x+1}
		\right) &= \ln \left(
		\frac{e^x}{e^x+1}
		\right) \nonumber \\
		&= x+\ln \left(
		\frac{1}{e^x+1}
		\right) \, . \label{eq:ln-x-id}
	\end{align}
	Substituting $x=\beta (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu})$and using Eq.~\eqref{eq:ln-x-id} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s} we obtain
	\begin{multline*}
		s^{(\mathrm{N})} = -\frac{2k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \Big[ \ln \left(
		1- f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) + f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \ln f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		- \beta (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \ln f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\Big] \, .
	\end{multline*}
	The second and the fourth terms cancel out. The remaining parts give Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s-variant}.
\end{tabproof}

\subsection{The free energy density of the normal ground-state}\label{subsec:normal-f}

In this section we compute the free energy density of the ground state, for the normal phase. The density of free energy is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-fMFT-def}
	f^{(\mathrm{N})} \equiv \frac{F^{(\mathrm{N})}}{L_x L_y} \, ,
\end{equation}
being $F^{(\mathrm{N})}$ the total free energy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-FMFT-def}
	F^{(\mathrm{N})} = E_\mathrm{HF}^{(\mathrm{N})} - \frac{S^{(\mathrm{N})}}{k_\mathrm{B} \beta} \, .
\end{equation}
Here $E_\mathrm{HF}^{(\mathrm{N})}$ is the ground-state energy for the HF hamiltonian. In the normal phase, we compute $f^{(\mathrm{N})}$ using the following formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-fMFT}
	f^{(\mathrm{N})} = \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \ln \left(1-f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})\right) - V \left( 
		\big|
			u^{(s^*)}
		\big|^2 + \big|
			u^{(d)}
		\big|^2
	\right) + \tilde{\mu} n  \, .
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT}: free energy density for the normal phase]
	
	Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT-def}, \eqref{eq:normal-FMFT-def}, we have
	\[
		f^{(\mathrm{N})} = \frac{E_\mathrm{HF}^{(\mathrm{N})}}{L_x L_y} - \frac{s^{(\mathrm{N})}}{k_\mathrm{B} \beta} \, ,
	\]
	with $s^{(\mathrm{N})}$ the entropy density computed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s-variant}. Then, $f^{(\mathrm{N})}$ is obtained by summing the following terms:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item The free particles ground state energy density,
		\[
			e_\mathrm{g}^{(\mathrm{N})} = \frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu} ) \, .
		\]
		The prefactor $2$ accounts for spin degeneracy.
		\item The contraction energy density,
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			e_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{N})} &\equiv \frac{E_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{N})}}{L_x L_y} \\
			&= - n^2 U + V \left(
				2zn^2 - 
				\big|
					u^{(s^*)}
				\big|^2 - \big|
					u^{(d)}
				\big|^2
			\right) \, ,
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		where we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-Ec}.
		\item The entropic contribution to free energy
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			e_s^{(\mathrm{N})} &\equiv -\frac{s^{(\mathrm{N})}}{k_\mathrm{B} \beta} \\
			&= \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \ln \left(
			1- f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) - \frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \, .
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		We used Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s-variant}.
		\item The chemical potential correction
		\[
			e_n^{(\mathrm{N})} = \mu n \, .
		\]
		We explained at the end of Sec.~\ref{subsec:fixed-density-role-mu} why this correction is needed. 	Note here that we are using the ``physical'' value of $\mu$, not its RMP counterpart $\tilde{\mu}$.
	\end{enumerate}
	Thus, composing everything
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		f^{(\mathrm{N})} &\equiv e_\mathrm{g}^{(\mathrm{N})} + e_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{N})} + e_s^{(\mathrm{N})} + e_n^{(\mathrm{N})} \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \ln \left(
		1- f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) - V \left( 
			\big|
				u^{(s^*)}
			\big|^2 + \big|
				u^{(d)}
			\big|^2
		\right) + \left[
			\mu + n(2zV-U)
		\right] n \, .
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-mu-shift}, we recognise
	\[
		\mu + n(2zV-U) = \tilde{\mu} \, .
	\]
	Hence Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT} is proven.
\end{tabproof}

\section{Constraints on the self-consistent solution} \label{sec:normal-sceq-analysis}

In this section, we collect some constraints on the HFPs $u^{(s^*)}$, $u^{(d)}$, that follow from a formal analysis of the self-consistency problem for the normal phase. It is possible to prove that:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-us*-ud-constraint}
	0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
	\qq{and}
	u^{(d)} = 0 \, ,
\end{equation}
for any choice of $U,V>0$ and doping $\delta$. The proof is reported in App.~\ref{app:theoretical-constraints}. Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-ud-constraint} implies that in the normal phase there is no nematic effect, and the renormalised hopping parameter $\tilde{t}_{ij}$ is isotropic with an amplitude smaller than $t$,
\[
	0 < \tilde{t}^{(s^*)} < t \, .
\]
Here we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-tS-td}. Then, bands renormalisation
\[
	\epsilon_\mathbf{k}
	\quad\to\quad
	\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \, ,
\]
is actually a flattening. We denote this effect as ``bands shrinking'', and show a schematics in Fig.~\ref{fig:bands-shrinking}. The flattening of electronic bands is a signature of a Mott transition, as we discussed in Chap.~\ref{chap:theoretical-introduction}.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\input{\GraphicsFolder/bands-shrinking}
	\caption{Graphic representation of the shrinking of renormalised band $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} = -4t \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)}$, as the hopping parameter is decreased by renormalisation.}
	\label{fig:bands-shrinking}
\end{figure}

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-tS-td} we notice that, if the converged value of $u^{(s^*)}$ approaches its boundaries, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
	u^{(s^*)} &\to 0
	&\quad\implies\qquad \tilde{t}^{(s^*)} &\to t \, , \\
	u^{(s^*)} &\to \frac{2t}{V}
	&\quad\implies\qquad \tilde{t}^{(s^*)} &\to 0 \, .
\end{aligned}
\]
In the second line, we see that the upper boundary can be interpreted as a critical value for Mott transition. Hence we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-uS-upper}
	u^{(s^*)}_\mathrm{c}(V) \equiv \frac{2t}{V} \, .
\end{equation}
If $u^{(s^*)} \to u^{(s^*)}_\mathrm{c}(V)$ (from below), bands are completely flattened.

We conclude this section by proving some analytic properties of $u^{(s^*)}$. Its self-consistency equation, from Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-ehm-ugamma-self-consistencies}, reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-us*(beta,mu)}
	u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \, ,
\end{equation}
where we explicitly highlighted the dependence of $u^{(s^*)}$ on the chemical potential and the temperature. We also notice that the renormalised bands, inside the Fermi-Dirac factor on the right-hand side, depend on $u^{(s^*)}$. We now prove that in the low-temperature limit the above equation implies that $u^{(s^*)}$ is a monotonically descendent function of doping,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-pdv-uS}
	\pdv{}{\delta} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \le 0 \, ,
\end{equation}
with the equality being verified when bands are completely flattened. The fact that $u^{(s^*)}$ cannot increase as we increase $\delta$ implies that the bands shrinking effect must be suppressed as we move away from half-filling.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-pdv-uS}: $u^{(s^*)}$ decreases with doping at low temperature]
	
	Whatever the shape of the renormalised bands, the chemical potential cannot decrease with doping,
	\[
		\pdv{\tilde{\mu}}{\delta} \ge 0 \, ,
	\]
	the equality holding only when adding electrons the Fermi level does not rise -- namely, when the band is flat. Moreover,
	\[
		\pdv{}{\delta} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) = \pdv{\tilde{\mu}}{\delta}  \pdv{}{\tilde{\mu}} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \, .
	\]
	Then, if we shows that $\partial u^{(s^*)} / \partial \tilde{\mu} < 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-pdv-uS} is proven.
	
	Upon taking the derivative of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*(beta,mu)}, we obtain
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\pdv{}{\tilde{\mu}} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \pdv{}{\tilde{\mu}} f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		&= -\frac{\beta}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \frac{e^{\beta(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu})}}{\left[e^{\beta(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu})}+1\right]^2} < 0
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	which ends the proof.
\end{tabproof}

This is the first expected behaviour of $u^{(s^*)}$: if there exists a region of parametric space where the shrinking effect is not strong enough to flatten the band, a rise in the doping level must lower $u^{(s^*)}$. Such a region must exist: at least all the segment $(V=0,\delta)$ is included, since in that region of parametric space the HF hamiltonian reduces to the that of the free Fermi gas.

We conclude this section with the analytic computation of $u^{(s^*)}$ at half filling and unitary filling, both at low temperature:
\begin{align}
	\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}[\beta,0] &= \left( \frac{2}{\pi} \right)^2 \approx 0.405  \, , \label{eq:normal-uS-half-filling} \\
	\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}\left[
	\beta,2\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}
	\right] &= 0 \, , \label{eq:normal-uS-unitary-filling}
\end{align}
where, at unitary filling, we set the chemical potential equal to the maximum energy reached by a band of amplitude $4\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$. All the properties hereby discussed are independent of the sign of $\delta$. Namely, all we derived is symmetric as we add or remove electrons.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\input{\GraphicsFolder/MBZ}
	\caption{Representation of the MBZ. The dashed lines represent the boundaries of the BZ.}
	\label{fig:normal-MBZ}
\end{figure}

In order to prove Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uS-half-filling}, it is necessary to introduce the Magnetic Brillouin Zone (MBZ):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MBZ-def}
	\mathrm{MBZ} \equiv \left\lbrace
	\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}
	\mid 
	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} \ge 0
	\right\rbrace \, .
\end{equation}
In Chap.~\ref{chap:af} the reason for such a name will be clarified. The MBZ coincides with the set of points $(k_x,k_y)$ that satisfy the relation
\[
	\abs{k_x} + \abs{k_y} \le \pi \, .
\]
We draw the MBZ in Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-MBZ}. The renormalised bands satisfy
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} = - 4 \tilde{t}^{(s^*)} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} \, .
\]
Hence, $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}<0$ inside the MBZ and $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}>0$ outside. This is also visible in the top-left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}. Along the boundary $\partial \mathrm{MBZ}$ we have $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}=0$.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:normal-uS-half-filling}, \eqref{eq:normal-uS-unitary-filling}]

	The two proofs are the following:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item At half-filling, the Fermi surface coincides with the boundary of the MBZ. Hence, being the Fermi-Dirac distribution a step-function at low temperatures, Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*(beta,mu)} reduces to
		\[
			\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,0) \simeq \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \, .
		\]
		In thermodynamic limit, performing integration on the MBZ shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-MBZ}, we obtain
		\[
		\begin{aligned}
			\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,0) &\simeq \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_y \int_{-\pi+\abs{k_y}}^{\pi-\abs{k_y}} \mathrm{d} k_x\, \cos k_x + \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_x \int_{-\pi+\abs{k_x}}^{\pi-\abs{k_x}} \mathrm{d} k_y\, \cos k_y \\
			&= \frac{1}{2\pi^2} \int_{-\pi}^{0} \mathrm{d} k_y \int_{-\pi-k_y}^{\pi+k_y} \mathrm{d} k_x\, \cos k_x + \frac{1}{2\pi^2} \int_{0}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_y \int_{-\pi+k_y}^{\pi-k_y} \mathrm{d} k_x\, \cos k_x \\
			&= \frac{1}{\pi^2} \int_{-\pi}^{0} \mathrm{d} k_y\, \sin (\pi+k_y) + \frac{1}{\pi^2} \int_{0}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_y\, \sin (\pi-k_y) \\ 
			&= \left( \frac{2}{\pi} \right)^2 \, ,
		\end{aligned}
		\]
		which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uS-half-filling}.
		\item At unitary filling we have
		\[
			\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}\left(
				\beta,2\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}
			\right) = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) = 0 \, ,
		\]
		which vanishes due to the structure factor periodicity by $\bm{\pi}$. This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uS-unitary-filling}.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{tabproof}

\section{Numeric results for the normal phase}\label{sec:normal-numeric}

In this section we present and discuss the numeric results we obtained for the normal phase. In the end we present a concrete scenario where careful tuning of the algorithm is necessary to ensure convergence. As is discussed above, in the normal phase the HFPs vector (defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}) has just one component:
\[
	\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
		u^{(s^*)}
	\end{bmatrix} \, .
\]
We have two RMPs,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\tilde{t}_{ij} &\equiv \left(
		t - \frac{u^{(s^*)}V}{2}
	\right) \varphi_{ij}^{(s^*)} \, , \\
	\tilde{\mu} &\equiv \mu + n (2zV-U) \, ,
\end{aligned}
\]
the second one being only dependent on the model parameters and not on HFPs. 
Finally, the phase free energy is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT}. The normal phase is algebraically independent of $U$. Therefore, we present the results varying the other parameters, keeping everywhere $U=0$. We ran simulations in the $(V, \delta)$ plane, with the setup:
\[
	U=0 \, ,
	\qquad
	\Delta n = 10^{-2} \, ,
	\qquad
	\Delta \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
		u^{(s^*)} \colon 5 \times 10^{-4}
	\end{bmatrix} \, ,
	\qquad
	\beta = 100.0 \, .
\]
Here $\Delta \mathbf{v}$ is the tolerance vector, defined as in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}. We have set $\beta=100.0$ as an (arbitrary) approximation of zero temperature. We measure temperature in adimensional units,
\[
	\frac{\beta}{\beta_0}
	\quad\to\quad
	\beta
\]
being $\beta_0 = 1/ (1\mathrm{K} \times k_\mathrm{B})$.

\subsection{Mott localisation}\label{subsec:normal-mott-numeric}

In Sec.~\ref{subsec:mott-cuprates} we introduced Mott insulation, and argued that the flattening of bands is a signature of a Mott transition. Interestingly, our results suggest that Mott localisation is induced by the non-local attraction $-V$, instead of the usual Mott localisation induced by the local repulsion $+U$. 

In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-uS-data-B256},we see that the converged value of $u^{(s^*)}$ is approximately horizontal up to the boundary $u^{(s^*)}_\mathrm{c}=2t/V$, represented as a dashed black line. Therefore, in the region where it is constant, we have
\[
	u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}(\delta)] \big|_V = u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}(\delta)] \big|_{V=0} \, .
\]
We define the critical Mott boundary as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-Vc}
	V_\mathrm{c}[\beta,\delta] \equiv \frac{2t}{u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}(\delta)] \big|_{V=0}} \, .
\end{equation}
When $V$ becomes larger than this critical value, due to the constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-ud-constraint}, $u^{(s^*)}$ is forced to decrease while $\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$ drops to $0$. In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-tS-data-B128} the converged values of $\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$, extracted from the same data of Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-uS-data-B128}, are plotted in parametric space.

The localisation effect is particularly effective at half-filling, where the kinetic energy vanishes for $V/t \approx 5$. This value needs to be compared with the critical value of the local repulsion $U_\mathrm{c}$ for Mott localisation. In DMFT, $U_\mathrm{c} \simeq 3W$, with $W$ half the bare bandwidth \cite{georges1996dynamical}. In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-tS-data-B128-mott} we report, superimposed, the convergence values of the RMP $\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$ and $V_\mathrm{c}[\beta=100,\delta]$ given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-Vc}. Our prediction of the critical value for $V$ approximates reasonably the localisation region.

To extract the value of $V_\mathrm{c}[\beta,\delta]$ is, in general, a non-trivial analytic problem due to the insurgence of elliptic integrals and the smearing effects of temperature. At low temperature, however, we can use Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uS-half-filling} and obtain the asymptotic critical boundary at half-filling
\begin{equation}
	V_\mathrm{c}[\beta \to \infty,0] = \frac{2t}{(2/\pi)^2} \approx 4.93 t \, , \label{eq:normal-Vc-half-filling}
\end{equation}
which we report in  Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-tS-data-B128-mott} as a tick of the vertical axis.

\subsection{Solution free energy and entropy densities}\label{subsec:normal-f-s}

In order to compare phases, we need an estimation of free energy of each. The plots of Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-f-data} report our findings on the normal phase free energy, computed based on Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT}. The free energy is intended in unities of $t$. For a non-flat band, doping increases energy because the band fills up and electrons populate higher energy levels. 

Looking at the data of Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-f-data-B128} at fixed $\delta$, free energy density remains approximately constant up to the Mott boundary. This is better appreciated in Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-f-data-B256}. Indeed, as long as a band with non-zero amplitude is present, the HFP correction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT} compensates the free energy increase due to shrinking of bands,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	- V \big| u^{(s^*)} \big|^2 &= - \frac{V u^{(s^*)*}}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \left( \epsilon_\mathbf{k} - \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \right)  f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \, ,
\end{aligned}
\]
having we used in the second passage
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} = \epsilon_\mathbf{k} + u^{(s^*)*} V (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \, .
\]
Recalling the first point of the proof given in Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-f}, to add the HFP correction to free energy is the same as removing the \textit{tilde} sign from single-state energies, thus using the free model ($V=0$) free energy density expression.

\subsection{Convergence problems and mixing fine-tuning}\label{subsec:normal-g-optimization}

In this subsection we describe some of the typical convergence problems arising in HF iterative schemes anticipated in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}. Bands flattening is a source of algorithmic instability: numerics can lead to values of $u^{(s^*)}$, during iterations, slightly out of bounds with respect to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-ud-constraint}. Even if the numeric effect is small, the consequence can be vast. To heal this effect and extract the actual convergence values, it is necessary to optimise the mixing parameter $g \in [0,1]$. 

\begin{figure}[t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../Project/src/labs/tracks/tracks.pdf}
	\caption{Comparison of three example tracks for the single-valued HFPs vector of the normal phase, simulated at a ``Mott-deep'' point.}
	\label{fig:normal-tracks-compared}
\end{figure}

We defined $g$ in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}, and operatively in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mix}. This parameter controls deterministically how, from an initialiser (the HFPs vector we plug in the algorithm) and an output (the HFPs vector we get back from the algorithm) the new initialiser for the following step is generated. We show in Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-tracks-compared} the evolutions at different mixing parameters of a simulation at
\[
	V = 6.0 \, ,
	\qquad
	\delta = 0.1 \, ,
	\qquad
	\beta = 100.0 \, .
\]
This point is inside the Mott region, as can be checked in Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-tS-data-B128-mott}. The HFP $u^{(s^*)}$ needs to converge at $2t/V=t/3$ in order to give complete localisation. The initialiser
\[
	\text{Step $1$}
	\quad\colon\quad
	u^{(s^*)} = 0.1 \, ,
\]
was arbitrarily chosen. In each panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-tracks-compared}, the red track represents the value of $u^{(s^*)}$ we plug in the self-consistency equations, the blue the result of the self-consistent equation, the green one the mixed value of the two based on different values of $g$. The gray dashed line is the theoretical convergence target. 

In the first two panels, there is an evident infinite loop occurring whenever the green line crosses the threshold $2t/V$. At those steps, the green point is out of bounds with respect to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-ud-constraint}. When this happens, the successive red point gives a band with inverted sign, a feature that ``breaks'' the self-consistency equation and generates a blue point ``far away''.

To overcome this problem it is necessary to move ``slowly''. As is shown in the right panel, the choice of a small enough mixing parameter ensures convergence. In the track, each blue dot is above the target and would produce a band with inverted sign. Mixing blue and red dots with a $95\%$ unbalance on the red ones, we ensure that the green track approaches the target from below.

This kind of problems can turn out to be pretty common for iHF schemes, and to understand how to knob the algorithmic parameters without sacrificing accurateness and precision, as well as avoiding computationally costly and frustrating infinite loops, was an essential part when designing this algorithm.
