\chapter{The normal phase}\label{chap:normal-phase}

In this chapter we apply the HF methods sketched in Sec.~\ref{sec:sketch-hf-method} to the ``normal phase''. We are studying the model in a state were no symmetry of those enumerated in Tab.~\ref{tab:symmetry-groups} is broken. The application of HF methods in this context will lead to a self-consistent renormalisation of the hopping parameter $t$, which will produce a Mott localisation effect.

This chapter is divided in three parts. First, we discuss the symmetries of the normal phase, following the general discussion of Chap.~\ref{chap:ehm-symmetries}. Second, we apply HF methods to derive the self-consistent approximated hamiltonian and impose some theoretical constraints on the solution. Lastly, we present numeric results.

\section{The normal phase and its  symmetries}\label{sec:normal-phase-theory}

In the normal phase no symmetry is broken. Thus, the HF wavefunction approximating the ground state must exhibit the full $\mathrm{U}(2) \otimes \mathrm{C}_4 \otimes \mathrm{T}_1^{(x)} \otimes \mathrm{T}_1^{(y)}$ symmetry discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:ehm-syms}. Translational invariance and spin rotational symmetry imply that the site occupation is independent of site and spin indices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-<n_i>}
	\ev{\hat n_{i\sigma}} = n
\end{equation}
with $n \in [0,1]$.

We define the single-state occupation operator in reciprocal space:
\[
	\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\]
and its EV
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}
	u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \langle
		\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\rangle
	\qquad
	u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^*
\end{equation}
The relation on the right follows from the fact that $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is hermitian by construction. Note that $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is not the Fourier-transform of $\hat n_{i\sigma}$. Translational invariance implies the following result:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-<cc>}
	\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \rangle = \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}'} \delta_{\sigma=\sigma'} \langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}]
	
	Consider the generic EV
	\[
		\big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \big\rangle
	\]
	for two momenta $\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{k}'$. Moving to real space via an $\mathcal{F}$-transform, we get
	\[
		\big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \big\rangle \Feq \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} \sum_{\mathbf{r}' \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \left(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{k}' \cdot \mathbf{r}' \right)} \big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma'}^\dagger \big\rangle
	\]
	where we used the convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier-convention}. Let now $\Delta \mathbf{r} \equiv \mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1$. Translational invariance implies that
	\[
		g(\Delta \mathbf{r}) = \big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\Delta\mathbf{r}\sigma'}^\dagger \big\rangle
	\]
	is independent of $\mathbf{r}$. Thus we have:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\big\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'}^\dagger \big\rangle &\Feq \sum_{\Delta\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \mathbf{k}' \cdot \Delta\mathbf{r}} g(\Delta \mathbf{r}) \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \left(\mathbf{k} - \mathbf{k}'\right) \cdot \mathbf{r}} \\
		&= \sum_{\Delta\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{-i \mathbf{k}' \cdot \Delta\mathbf{r}} g(\Delta \mathbf{r}) \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}'}
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where we used the identity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-delta}. Then the above EV vanishes for $\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k}'$. This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}.
\end{tabproof}

We anticipate here that we will actually allow for $\mathrm{C}_4 \SSB \mathrm{C}_2$, namely, breaking of planar rotational symmetry. As will result from our discussion, the normal phase does not break $\mathrm{C}_4$ even if we allow it to. Nevertheless we carry out this general derivation because breaking of planar rotational symmetry will be observed in Chap.~\ref{chap:sc} in the context of superconductivity, and the computations carried out for the normal phase will be re-used.

Applying the HF methods to the interactions $\hat H_U$ and $\hat H_V$, from Tabs.~\ref{tab:HU-sel-rules} and \ref{tab:HV-sel-rules}, we know that in the normal phase the allowed contractions are:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	&\hat H_U 
	&&\text{Hartree contractions} \\
	&\hat H_V \text{ (o.s. sector)}
	&&\text{Hartree contractions} \\
	&\hat H_V \text{ (s.s. sector)}
	&&\text{Hartree and Fock contractions} \\
\end{aligned}	
\]
As we will see in detail and is summarised in Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-RWCs}, Hartree contractions give a chemical potential shift, while the Fock contraction renormalises the bare bands.

In the normal phase, spin is a pure physical degeneracy, being the $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ sectors separated; moreover, $\hat H$ is $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant with respect to spin inversion, $\sigma \leftrightarrow \overline{\sigma}$. This implies that all physical quantities, as is for the normal phase, should not depend on the spin index. We will perform all computations leaving $\sigma$ explicit and using this consideration at the end.

\section{Application of HF methods to the normal phase}\label{sec:normal-hm}

The quartic part of $\hat H$ is given by the the local repulsion $\hat H_U$ and the non-local attraction $\hat H_V$. Applying HF methods, we get the decomposition of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU}, \eqref{eq:mft-HV}. In the next section we analyse the Hartree channel, which contains all Hartree-like terms. We will see that $\hat H_U$ and $\hat H_V$ are approximated, in this channel, by a multiple of the number operator. In the section that follows, we deal with Fock terms, and show how $\hat H_V$ is approximated, in that channel, by an operator that is formally equivalent to the kinetic operator $\hat H_t$.

\subsection{Hartree effects: chemical potential renormalisation}\label{subsec:normal-hartree-mu}

Consider the Hartree channel of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU}, \eqref{eq:mft-HV},
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_U &\HFsimeq U \sum_i \left(
		\hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\langle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} \big\rangle + \big\langle \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\rangle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} - \big\langle \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} \big\rangle  \big\langle \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow} \big\rangle 
	\right) + (\text{all the rest}) \\
	\hat H_V &\HFsimeq -V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_{\sigma,\sigma'} \left(
		\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\langle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} \big\rangle + \big\langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\rangle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} - \big\langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \big\rangle  \big\langle \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'} \big\rangle
	\right) + (\text{all the rest})
\end{aligned}
\]
Let us deal with those separately.

\paragraph{Local repulsion.}

In the normal phase, the local repulsion $\hat H_U$ decomposes in Hartree channel as specified in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU}
\[
	\hat H_U \HFsimeq
	U \sum_{i} \left(
		\langle 
			\hat n_{i\uparrow}
		\rangle \hat n_{i\downarrow} + \hat n_{i\uparrow} \langle 
			\hat n_{i\downarrow}
		\rangle - \langle 
			\hat n_{i\downarrow}
		\rangle \langle 
			\hat n_{i\uparrow}
			\rangle
	\right)
\]
For a translationally invariant phase such as the normal phase, it holds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-density-ansatz}
	\langle \hat n_{i\sigma} \rangle = n
\end{equation}
which gives
\begin{align}
	\hat H_U &\HFsimeq
	nU \sum_i \left(
		 \hat n_{i\uparrow} + \hat n_{i\downarrow}
	\right)
	- U \sum_i n^2 \nonumber \\
	&= nU \times \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{N})} \label{eq:normal-HU-hartree-approx}
\end{align}
being $\hat N$ the total number operator and $ E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{N})}$ the ``local Hartree contraction energy'' (also known as double counting term),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec}
	E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{N})} = L_x L_y \times n^2U 
\end{equation}
This energy contributes negatively to the ground-state energy, for $U>0$. The chemical potential is corrected by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-mu-hU}
	\mu \to \mu - nU
\end{equation}
The non-local attraction further corrects $\mu$.

\paragraph{Non-local attraction.}

The non-local attraction is divided in two sectors, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-HV(ss)+HV(os)}. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HV}, its Hartree-like terms are:
\begin{multline*}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq 
	\overbrace{
		-V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
			\langle 
			\hat n_{i\sigma}
			\rangle \hat n_{j\sigma} + \hat n_{i\sigma} \langle 
			\hat n_{j\sigma}
			\rangle - \langle 
			\hat n_{i\sigma}
			\rangle \langle 
			\hat n_{j\sigma}
			\rangle
		\right)
	}^{\mathrm{s.s.}} \\
	\underbrace{
		-V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
			\langle
			\hat n_{i\sigma}
			\rangle \hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{i\sigma} \langle 
			\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
			\rangle
			- \langle 
			\hat n_{i\sigma}
			\rangle \langle 
			\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
			\rangle
		\right)
	}_{\mathrm{o.s.}} \, +\, 
	(\text{all the rest})
\end{multline*}
where ``all the rest'' collects all non-Hartree terms. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-density-ansatz}, we get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-hartree-HV-int1}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq  \underbrace{
		-nV \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\hat n_{j\sigma} + \hat n_{i\sigma}
		\right)
	}_{\mathrm{s.s.}}
	\underbrace{
		-nV \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{i\sigma}
		\right)
	}_{\mathrm{o.s.}} 
	- E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
	+ (\text{all the rest})
\end{equation}
with $E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})}$ the normal state shift to energy brought by $\hat H_V$:
\begin{align}
	E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= - V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
	\langle 
	\hat n_{i\sigma}
	\rangle \langle 
	\hat n_{j\sigma}
	\rangle
	+
	\langle 
	\hat n_{i\sigma}
	\rangle \langle 
	\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
	\rangle
	\right) \nonumber \\
	&= -2V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma n^2 \nonumber \\
	&= - L_x L_y \times 2zn^2 V \label{eq:normal-HV-hartree-Ec}
\end{align}
Note the similarity with Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec}.

Getting back to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-hartree-HV-int1}, both sums give a number operator. Note that we sum over NNs: each site is connected to $z=4$ sites. Including this combinatorial factor, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-hartree-approx}
	\hat H_V \simeq -2nzV \times \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} + 
	(\text{all the rest})
\end{equation}
This accounts for the final Hartree shift of $\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:non-local-hartree-mu-shift}
	\mu \to \mu + 2znV
\end{equation}

Collecting everything, we get the HF approximation to the two interactions in the Hartree channel:
\[
	\hat H_U + \hat H_V \HFsimeq -n(2zV-U) \hat N 
\]
Considering the net shift to $\mu$ due to both interactions by combining Eqns.~\eqref{eq:normal-mu-hU}, \eqref{eq:non-local-hartree-mu-shift}, we get the final RMP anticipated in Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-RWCs},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-mu-shift}
	\tilde{\mu} \equiv \mu + n(2zV-U)
\end{equation}
This result will remain valid throughout all phases discussed in this text.

\subsection{Fock effects: bare bands renormalisation}\label{subsec:normal-fock-bands}
 
We move to Fock terms. From Wick's decomposition of $\hat H_V$, the unique allowed Fock term comes from the same-spin sector. This selection rules is implied by the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ symmetry of the normal phase and was stated in Tab.~\ref{tab:HV-sel-rules}. Then, from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HV}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-fock}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + 
	V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\langle
		\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
		\rangle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} + 
		\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
		\langle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} \rangle - \langle \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
		\rangle \langle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} \rangle
	\right)
\end{equation}
Note the plus sign in front of the displayed term: it comes from the minus sign in Fock term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hf-wick-hfc}.

Consider the Fourier Transform $\mathcal{F}$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:HV-ss-fourier}, which gives
\begin{multline}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) \\
	+ \frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \left(
	\cos \left(
	\delta k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	\delta k_y
	\right)	
	\right)
	\langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int1}
\end{multline}
Here, the $2$ prefactor comes from the fact that the first two operators in square brackets in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock} are equal in reciprocal space. The double counting energy shift is given by
\begin{align}\label{eq:normal-V-fock-double-counting}
	E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \equiv \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\cos \left(
			\delta k_x
		\right)	+ \cos \left(
			\delta k_y
		\right)	
	\right)
	\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma}
	\rangle \langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} 
	\rangle
\end{align}

Recall the definition of $u_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}. Using the functions of Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}, we define its harmonics:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics}
	u^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \equiv \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\rangle
\end{equation}
The non-local attraction $\hat H_V$ is approximated, in Fock channel, by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}
	\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + 
	V
	\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma
	\left(
		u^{(s^*)}_\sigma
		\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)*} + u^{(d)}_\sigma
		\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)*}
	\right)
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
\end{equation}
We provide a proof.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}]
	
	Consider Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int1}. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}, it becomes:
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel})\\ + 
		\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma \left( \cos 2 k_x + \cos 2 k_y	
		\right)
		\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}
		\rangle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int2}
	\end{multline}
	Let now $\mathbf{q}\equiv\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{q}'\equiv\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}$. A representation of this variables change is given in Fig.~\ref{fig:Kk-qq'}. Being (for $\ell=x,y$)
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\delta q_\ell &\equiv q_\ell - q_\ell' \\
		&= (K_\ell+k_\ell) - (K_\ell-k_\ell) \\
		&= 2k_\ell
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	we reduce Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int1} to
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) \\
		+ \frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
			\cos \left(
				\delta q_x
			\right)	+ \cos \left(
				\delta q_y
			\right)	
		\right]	
		\underbrace{\langle
			\hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger 
			\hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
		\rangle}_{u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int3}
	\end{multline}
	where we recognised $u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}$, as was defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:coscos-symmetries-decomposition}, we obtain
	\begin{multline}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) \\
		+ \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left(
			\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_\mathbf{q\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
			u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
		\right) \times V
		\sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}}
		\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} \label{eq:normal-V-fock-int4}
	\end{multline}
	where we used the functions defined in Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}. We recognise the harmonics $u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics},
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-V-fock-int5}
		\hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + V \sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}
		\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
	\end{equation}
	which is almost Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}. 
	
	To complete the proof, we recognise that in the normal phase inversion symmetry is not broken, thus
	\[
		u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} = u_{-\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\]
	This follows from the fact that in the normal phase the EV of the operator $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ must be inversion-symmetric. It follows that antisymmetric harmonics are zero,
	\[
		u_\sigma^{(p_\ell)} = 0
		\qq{for}
		\ell\in\lbrace x,y \rbrace
	\]
	Using this observation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int5}, we obtain Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx}.
	
\end{tabproof}

Composing Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx} with Eqns.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-approx}, \eqref{eq:normal-HV-hartree-approx} we obtain the quadratic HF approximation to the EHM, for the normal phase. The chemical potential $\tilde{\mu}$ is fixed by density and determined during computations. To find the ground-state, we must determine self-consistently the two HFPs $u_\sigma^{(s^*)}$, $u_\sigma^{(d)}$ through the self-consistency Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-ugamma-harmonics}. The operator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock-approx} has the same algebraic structure of the kinetic operator $\hat H_t$ in reciprocal space, given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ht-Fourier}. Hence, we can collapse the two into one effective kinetic operator. Let the renormalised bare bands be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation}
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + u^{(s^*)}_\sigma V (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) + u^{(d)}_\sigma V (\cos k_x - \cos k_y)
\end{equation}
In Fock channel, we have:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-H~t}
	\hat H_t + \hat H_V \HFsimeq (\text{Hartree channel}) + \underbrace{
		\sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	}_{\hat H_{\tilde{t}}} - E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
\end{equation}
where we defined the effective kinetic operator, $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$. 

The renormalised bands of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation} are a sum of $s^*$ and $d$-wave components. Instead, bare bands as defined Eq.~\eqref{eq:bare-bands} are purely $s^*$-symmetric functions, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bands-vphi}
	\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = -2t (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) = -4t \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)}
\end{equation}
We define:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-tS-td}
	\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}_\sigma \equiv t - \frac{u_\sigma^{(s^*)} V}{2}
	\qquad
	\tilde{t}^{(d)}_\sigma \equiv - \frac{u_\sigma^{(d)} V}{2}
\end{equation}
Hence, Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation} can be written as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation-vphi}
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv -4\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} -4\tilde{t}^{(d)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)}
\end{equation}
Note the formal similarity with Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-vphi}. Bands renormalisation is actually a renormalisation of the hopping parameter $t$,
\[
	\text{$t$ ($s^*$-wave)}
	\quad\to\quad
	\text{$\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}_\sigma$ ($s^*$-wave) and $\tilde{t}^{(d)}_\sigma$ ($d$-wave)}
\]
Therefore, $t$ is an RMP. In Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-d-wave-hopping} we will show how all of this is translated in real space.

We conclude this section by proving that the Fock contraction energy is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-HU-fock-Ec}
	E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} = L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_\sigma \left(
		\big|
			u_\sigma^{(s^*)}
		\big|^2 + \big|
			u_\sigma^{(d)}
		\big|^2
	\right)
\end{equation}
Later, using spin degeneracy, we will omit the sum on $\sigma$ and the $1/2$ prefactor.

\begin{tabproof}[Non-local Fock contraction energy.]

	Recall Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-double-counting}. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}, it reduces to:
	\[
		E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} = \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{ \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\cos \left(
		\delta q_x
		\right)	+ \cos \left(
		\delta q_y
		\right)	
		\right]	
		\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{q} \sigma}
		\rangle \langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{q}' \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{q}' \sigma}
		\rangle 
	\]
	Inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uqsigma-def}, we get:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} &= L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left(
		\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)} u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
		\right) \left(
		\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*} u_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^*
		\right) \\
		&= L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma}
		\big|
			u_\sigma^{(\gamma)}
		\big|^2
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	which, using the fact that $u_\sigma^{(p_\ell)}=0$, proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-fock-Ec}.
\end{tabproof}

\section{The HF hamiltonian and its properties}

In the normal phase, the HF approximation to the EHM hamiltonian is given by the following operator:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-Hhf}
	\hat H - \mu \hat N \HFsimeq \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \tilde{\mu} \hat N
	- \left(
		E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{N})} + E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})} + 		E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{N})}
	\right)
\end{equation}
Apart from energy shifts, this hamiltonian describes a perfectly degenerate Fermi gas whose bare bands are $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation}, and chemical potential $\tilde{\mu}$ as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-mu-shift}. The latter is completely determined by the model parameters; the former must be determined self-consistently. The ground state of the approximated hamiltonian obeys Fermi-Dirac statistics,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-<cc>-FD}
	\langle \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma'} \rangle = \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}'} \delta_{\sigma=\sigma'} f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
\end{equation}
being $f$ the Fermi-Dirac distribution,
\[
	f(\epsilon;\beta,\mu) \equiv \frac{1}{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1}
\]
Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>-FD} is the explicit version of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>}.

In next subsection we discuss in detail the renormalisation of the hopping parameter in real space, with a focus on the meaning of the $d$-wave component. In the subsection that follow, we derive the free energy and entropy densities for the ground-state

\subsection{Nematicity: $d$-wave bands and anisotropic hopping}\label{subsec:normal-d-wave-hopping}

In Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-H~t} we defined $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$ in reciprocal space. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HV-fock} we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_{\tilde{t}} &= \hat H_t + V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\langle
	\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
	\rangle \hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{i\sigma} + \hc
	\right] \\
	&= - \sum_{i,j} \sum_\sigma 
	\left[
	\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma} \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma} + \hc
	\right]
\end{aligned}
\]
Note that in second line we are not summing over NNs, but on two site indices $i,j \in \mathcal{L}$. We defined the effective hopping amplitude
\[
	\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma} \equiv \frac{1}{2} \left(
		t - V \langle
		\hat c_{j\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma} \rangle
	\right)
	\qquad
	(\text{for $i,j$ NNs})
\]
The $1/2$ prefactor was included to avoid double counting.

Due to translational invariance, the renormalised hopping $\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma}$ depends only on the difference $i-j$. Therefore we can use the decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-decomposition},
\[
	\tilde{t}_{ij\sigma} = \sum_\gamma \tilde{t}^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_{ij}^{(\gamma)}
\]
The harmonics, given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-gamma}, are identified with $\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)}$, $\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}$ already introduced in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-tS-td}. From the same argument we know that $\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(p_x)}=\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(p_y)}=0$. Then, the renormalised hopping is explicitly given by
\begin{multline*}
	\tilde{t}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'\sigma} = \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)}}{2} \left[
		\delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}}
	\right] \\
	+ \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}}{2} \left[
		\delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}} + \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}} - \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}} - \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}}
	\right]
\end{multline*}
Let us define:
\begin{align}
	\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(x)} &\equiv \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)} + \tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}}{2} \label{eq:normal-tx} \\
	\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(y)} &\equiv \frac{\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(s^*)} - \tilde{t}_\sigma^{(d)}}{2} \label{eq:normal-ty}
\end{align}
With this definition, we can rewrite $\hat H_{\tilde{t}}$ as:
\[
	\hat H_{\tilde{t}} = - \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(x)} \left(
			\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}\sigma} + \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}\sigma}
		\right) + \tilde{t}_\sigma^{(y)} \left(
			\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}\sigma} + \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}\sigma}
		\right) + \hc
	\right]
\]
This is what we intend for ``nematic effects'': the breaking of planar rotational symmetry, with the consequence that the kinetic operators in $x$ and $y$ directions differ. Breaking of planar rotational symmetry and uniform density are compatible.

\begin{tabproof}[A $d$-wave hopping is compatible with uniform density]

	Consider the on-site occupation operator $\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} = \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}$. Applying a Fourier transform as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier-convention}, we obtain
	\[
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \stackrel{\mathcal{F}}{=} \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} e^{i (\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}') \cdot\mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma}
	\]
	We compute the expectation value of the above operator and apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-<cc>-FD},
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}} &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} e^{i (\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}') \cdot\mathbf{r}} \langle
			\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma}
		\rangle \\
		&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	The right-hand side of last line is independent of $\mathbf{r}$, therefore $\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}}$ is.
\end{tabproof}
%
Note that we never used the actual topology of the renormalised bands $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}$, but only the fact that the ground-state obeys the Fermi-Dirac distribution. A perfectly degenerate Fermi gas is always translational invariant and thus cannot exhibit non-uniform symmetry.

	\centering
	\input{\GraphicsFolder/anisotropic-hopping}
	\caption{Graphic rendition of anisotropic hopping, with a $s^*$-wave component (symmetric by $\pi/$ rotations) and a $d$-wave component (antisymmetric).}
	\label{fig:anisotropic-hopping}
\end{figure}

A schematics of anisotropic hopping is given in Fig.~\ref{fig:anisotropic-hopping}: for each bond linking two sites, the hopping parameter can be a positive or negative combination of the two components, as the link direction is vertical or horizontal.

From now on, we will omit the spin index $\sigma$ from both the renormalised bands and the renormalised hopping,
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \to \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}
	\qquad
	\forall \alpha
	\quad\colon\quad
	\tilde{t}_\sigma^{(\alpha)} \to \tilde{t}^{(\alpha)}
\]
The normal phase does not break spin rotational invariance. Therefore the renormalised bands must be independent of the spin index. Inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-tS-td}, we can
express $\tilde{t}^{(x)}$ and $\tilde{t}^{(y)}$ in terms of the HFPs $u^{(s^*)}$ and $u^{(d)}$
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\tilde{t}^{(x)} &\equiv t - \frac{u^{(s^*)} + u^{(d)}}{2} V \\
	\tilde{t}^{(y)} &\equiv t - \frac{u^{(s^*)} - u^{(d)}}{2} V
\end{aligned}
\]
Inverting these relations we obtain:
\begin{align}
	u^{(s^*)} &= \frac{
		\left(
		t-\tilde{t}^{(x)}
		\right) + \left(
		t-\tilde{t}^{(y)}
		\right)
	}{V} \label{eq:normal-us*(tx,ty)}
	\\
	u^{(d)} &= \frac{\tilde{t}^{(x)} - \tilde{t}^{(y)}}{V} \label{eq:normal-ud(tx,ty)}
\end{align}
It follows that if $u^{(d)}>0$, the hopping in direction $x$ is larger than in direction $y$ is damped. If $u^{(d)}<0$, roles are inverted.

\subsection{The entropy density of the SC ground-state}\label{subsec:normal-s}

The HF hamiltonian of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-Hhf} describes a non-interacting Fermi gas. The entropy density of the ground state is given by \cite{grosso2014solid},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-s}
	s = -\frac{2k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \left[
		\left(
			1- f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right) \ln \left(
			1-f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right) + f(
			\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
			\ln f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\vphantom{A^A}
	\right]
\end{equation}
The $2$ prefactor was included to account for spin degeneracy. The above equation can be written in another way:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-s-variant}
		s = -\frac{k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \left[ \ln \left(
		1-f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right)
		- \beta  (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) 
	\right]
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s-variant}]

	The following identity
	\begin{align}
		\ln \left(
		1-\frac{1}{e^x+1}
		\right) &= \ln \left(
		\frac{e^x}{e^x+1}
		\right) \nonumber \\
		&= x+\ln \left(
		\frac{1}{e^x+1}
		\right) \label{eq:ln-x-id}
	\end{align}
	Substituting $x=\beta (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu})$and using Eq.~\eqref{eq:ln-x-id} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s} we obtain
	\begin{multline*}
		s = -\frac{k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \Big[ \ln \left(
		1- f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) + f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \ln f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		- \beta (\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \ln f( \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\Big]
	\end{multline*}
	Second and fourth terms cancel out. The remaining parts give Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-s-variant}.
	
\end{tabproof}

		\label{subfig:NBZ-sectors}
	}

The full green zone composes $\mathcal{A}$, the region where both the $s^*$-wave and $d$-wave structure factors are positive defined; its MBZ complementary white counterpart in the left panel gives $\mathcal{B}$, for which the $s^*$-wave structure factors is positive-definite while the $d$-wave is negative. Let us focus on the MBZ and the NBZ. Starting from the intersections of the two zones, both can be factored in two sectors of interest,
\[
	\mathrm{MBZ} = \mathcal{A} \cup \mathcal{B}
	\qquad
	\mathrm{NBZ} = \mathcal{A} \cup \mathcal{C}
\]
being, accordingly to Fig.~\ref{fig:BZ-sectors},
\[
	\mathcal{A} \equiv \mathcal{A}_1 \cup \mathcal{A}_2
	\qquad
	\mathcal{B} \equiv \mathcal{B}_1 \cup \mathcal{B}_2
	\qquad
	\mathcal{C} \equiv \mathcal{C}_1 \cup \mathcal{C}_2
\]
These domains satisfy:
\begin{align}
	\mathcal{A} &\equiv \left\lbrace
	\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}
	\mid 
	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} \ge 0 \land 	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)} \ge 0
	\right\rbrace \label{eq:BZ-A-def} \\
	&= \mathrm{MBZ} \cap \mathrm{NBZ} \nonumber\\[0.5em]
	\mathcal{B} &\equiv \left\lbrace
	\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}
	\mid 
	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} \ge 0 \land 	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)} \le 0
	\right\rbrace \label{eq:BZ-B-def} \\
	&= \mathrm{MBZ} \setminus \mathrm{NBZ} \nonumber\\[0.5em]
	\mathcal{C} &\equiv \left\lbrace
	\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}
	\mid 
	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(s^*)} \le 0 \land 	\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(d)} \ge 0
	\right\rbrace \label{eq:BZ-C-def} \\
	&= \mathrm{NBZ} \setminus \mathrm{MBZ} \nonumber
\end{align}
and will be used later on. Whatever the shape of the mixed band, the following equation always holds
\[
	\abs{k_x} = \frac{\pi}{2} \qq{and} \abs{k_y} = \frac{\pi}{2}
	\quad\implies\quad
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{(k_x,k_y)} = 0
\]
since only cosines are involved. This identifies four node points, represented as black dots in Fig.~\ref{fig:BZ-sectors}.

\subsection{Constraints in presence of a robust $\mathrm{C}_4$ symmetry and bands shrinking}\label{subsec:normal-bands-shrinking}

Consider the simpler situation where $\mathrm{C}_4$ symmetry is imposed. This implies that the renormalised bands are
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \equiv (u^{(s^*)} V - 2t) (\cos k_x + \cos k_y)
\]
with a single self-consistency equation
\[
	u^{(s^*)} = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
\]
The following chain of deductions leads us to a constraint on the possible values of $u^{(s^*)}$:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item For $t>0$, there is no self-consistent solution admitting $u^{(s^*)}\le0$. Let us prove this: suppose instead $u^{(s^*)}\le0$. For any function $g_\mathbf{k}$ defined on the BZ:
	\[
		\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} g_\mathbf{k} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
		g_\mathbf{k} + g_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}
		\right]
	\]
	where we defined the vector $\bm{\pi}\equiv(\pi,\pi)$. The self-consistency equation thus reads
	\begin{align}
		u^{(s^*)} &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \left[
			f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) + f(\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right] \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \left[
			f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right] \label{eq:normal-us*-constraint-int1}
	\end{align}
	having we used the antisymmetry of $s^*$-wave functions by $\bm{\pi}$ shifts. Now, if $u^{(s^*)}\le0$ the renormalised band $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}$ is negative inside the MBZ and positive outside, implying
	\[
		\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}
		\quad\colon\quad
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \ge f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\]
	the equality only being satisfied on the MBZ contour. Then, since inside the MBZ it holds by definition $\cos k_x + \cos k_y \ge 0$, the above equations imply
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0 
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(s^*)} \ge 0
	\]
	To exclude the possibility of $u^{(s^*)}=0$, it suffices to notice that from the argument above such a situation only arises when the MBZ contour degenerates to the entirety of the MBZ itself -- which is, when the band is flat. But that cannot be the case for $u^{(s^*)} = 0$, as long as $t>0$. Thus we have proven that
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} > 0
	\]
	\item For $t>0$, there is no self-consistent solution admitting $u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V$. We proceed exactly as did for the previous point, up to the passage:
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \left[
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right]
	\]
	Now, if $u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V$ this means the prefactor in front of the renormalised bands is positive. Then $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}$ is positive inside the MBZ and negative outside, implying
	\[
		\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}
		\quad\colon\quad
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \le f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\]
	thus we have
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0
	\]
	which is absurd. Thus
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
	\]
\end{enumerate}
Collecting the two points above, we have the constraint on $u^{(s^*)}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-us*-constraint}
	\mathrm{C}_4
	\quad\implies\quad
	0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
\end{equation}
The effective hopping is smaller than the original hopping. We can denominate this net effect ``bands shrinking''.

\subsection{Natural suppression of nematicity within $\mathrm{C}_2/\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry}\label{subsec:normal-bands-shrinking-C2}

Let us extend the arguments above to a weaker symmetry, allowing for
\[
	\mathrm{C}_4 \SSB \mathrm{C}_2/\mathbb{Z}_2
\]
%
\begin{figure}[t]
	\centering
	\subfloat[Same-sign mixed bands.]{
		\includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{../Project/src/labs/bands/normal-nematic-bands-pp.pdf}
		\label{subfig:normal-nematic-bands-detail-pp}
	}
	\hspace{0.5em}
	\subfloat[Opposite-sign mixed bands.]{
		\includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{../Project/src/labs/bands/normal-nematic-bands-mp.pdf}
		\label{subfig:normal-nematic-bands-detail-mp}
	}
	\caption{Theoretical example of bands with $u^{(s^*)} > 2t/V$, $u^{(d)}>0$ (top-left) and $u^{(s^*)} < 0$, $u^{(d)}>0$ (top right). Two example points are reported, connected by a $\pi/2$ rotation as given in Eqns.~\eqref{eq:normal-A=RB}, \eqref{eq:normal-B=RA}.}
	\label{fig:normal-nematic-bands-detail}
\end{figure}
%
We work with the full, anisotropic band of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation}. From the following chain of deductions we conclude that $\mathrm{C}_4$ is not broken:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Let for instance
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V > 0
		\qquad
		u^{(d)} > 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		\epsilon^{(s^*)} \ge 0
		\qquad
		\epsilon^{(d)} > 0
	\]
	In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-nematic-bands-detail-pp} an example band is shown for $\epsilon^{(s^*)}=1$, $\epsilon^{(d)}=1/2$. Note that if $u^{(s^*)}=2t/V$, the renormalised band is purely $d$-wave since $\epsilon^{(s^*)}=0$. 
	
	Recall Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-constraint-int1}, and separate in two sums over $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$:
	\begin{multline*}
		u^{(s^*)} = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{A}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \left[
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right] \\
		+ \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{B}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \left[
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right]
	\end{multline*}
	
	Consider the sum over $\mathcal{B}$. Looking at the bottom-left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-nematic-bands}, such a region contains nodal curves across which bands change sign.	
	Let $\mathcal{R}$ be the clockwise $\pi/2$ rotation: then, any point in $\mathcal{B}$ can be seen as the rotation of a point in $\mathcal{A}$
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-B=RA}
		\forall \mathbf{k}' \in \mathcal{B} 
		\quad\exists\quad \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{A} \mid \mathbf{k}' = \mathcal{R} \mathbf{k}
	\end{equation}
	A graphical rendition of this idea is given in Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-nematic-bands-detail-pp}. Then we can rewrite $u^{(s^*)}$ as a single sum over $\mathcal{A}$,
	\begin{multline}
		u^{(s^*)} = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{A}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \\
		\times \left[
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) + f(\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right]
		\label{eq:normal-us*-AR}
	\end{multline}
	
	From Eq.~\eqref{eq:BZ-A-def}, $\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{A}$ the form factors $\cos k_x \pm \cos k_y$ are positive by construction. By hypothesis $\epsilon^{(s^*)} \ge 0$ and $\epsilon^{(d)} > 0$. Hence:
	\[
	\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{A}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\begin{aligned}
		\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} &= \overbrace{\epsilon^{(s^*)} \left( \cos k_x + \cos k_y \right)}^{\ge 0} + \overbrace{\epsilon^{(d)}  \left( \cos k_x - \cos k_y \right) }^{\ge 0} \\
		\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}} &= \underbrace{\epsilon^{(s^*)} \left( \cos k_x + \cos k_y \right)}_{\ge 0} - \underbrace{\epsilon^{(d)}  \left( \cos k_x - \cos k_y \right) }_{\ge 0}
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	Summing the two equations above we have:
	\[
	\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{A}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} + \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}} \ge 0
	\]
	thus $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \ge -\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}}$.	The Fermi-Dirac distribution is monotonically descendent. Then:
	\[
	\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{A}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\begin{aligned}
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) &\le f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) &\ge f(\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) 
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	This condition, inserted in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-AR}, leads to an absurd:
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V \qquad
		u^{(d)} > 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0
	\]
	%
	\item An identical argument, exchanging the roles of $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$, can be carried out to show that
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V > 0
		\qquad
		u^{(d)} < 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0
	\]
	%
	\item The two points above imply that it is possible to have $u^{(s^*)} \ge 2t/V$ if and only if $u^{(d)}=0$. But from Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-bands-shrinking}, we conclude that the upper boundary $u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V$ always holds.
	%
	\item Let for instance
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0
		\qquad
		u^{(d)} > 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		\epsilon^{(s^*)} < 0
		\qquad
		\epsilon^{(d)} > 0
	\]
	In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-nematic-bands-detail-mp} an example band is shown for $\epsilon^{(s^*)}=-1$, $\epsilon^{(d)}=1/2$.
	
	Proceed as for the first point. Similarly to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-B=RA}, Any point in $\mathcal{A}$ can be obtained by rotating some point in $\mathcal{B}$,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-A=RB}
		\forall \mathbf{k}' \in \mathcal{A} 
		\quad\exists\quad \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B} \mid \mathbf{k}' = \mathcal{R} \mathbf{k}
	\end{equation}
	An equation similar to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-AR} holds, the sum being performed over $\mathcal{B}$
	\begin{multline}
		u^{(s^*)} = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{B}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) \\
		\times \left[
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) + f(\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right]
		\label{eq:normal-us*-BR}
	\end{multline}
	
	From Eq.~\eqref{eq:BZ-B-def}, $\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}$ the form factor $\cos k_x + \cos k_y$ is positive by construction; the form factor $\cos k_x - \cos k_y$ is negative by construction. By hypothesis $\epsilon^{(s^*)} < 0$ and $\epsilon^{(d)} > 0$. Hence:
	\[
		\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}
		\quad\colon\quad
		\begin{aligned}
			\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} &= \overbrace{\epsilon^{(s^*)} \left( \cos k_x + \cos k_y \right)}^{\le 0} + \overbrace{\epsilon^{(d)}  \left( \cos k_x - \cos k_y \right) }^{\le 0} \\
			\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}} &= \underbrace{\epsilon^{(s^*)} \left( \cos k_x + \cos k_y \right)}_{\le 0} - \underbrace{\epsilon^{(d)}  \left( \cos k_x - \cos k_y \right) }_{\le 0}
		\end{aligned}
	\]
		Summing the two equations above we have:
	\[
		\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}
		\quad\colon\quad
		\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} + \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}} \le 0
	\]
	thus $\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \le -\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}}$.	The Fermi-Dirac distribution is monotonically descendent. Then:
	\[
	\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\begin{aligned}
		f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) &\ge f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		f(-\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) &\le f(\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) 
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	This condition, inserted in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-AR}, leads to an absurd:
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0 \qquad
		u^{(d)} > 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(s^*)} > 0
	\]
	%
	\item can be carried out to show that
	\[
		u^{(s^*)} \le 0
		\qquad
		u^{(d)} < 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(s^*)} > 0
	\]
	%
	\item The two points above imply that it is possible to have $u^{(s^*)} \le 0$ if and only if $u^{(d)}=0$. But from Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-bands-shrinking}, we conclude that the lower boundary $u^{(s^*)} > 0$ always holds.
	\newcounter{enumTemp}
	\setcounter{enumTemp}{\theenumi}
\end{enumerate}
From the chain of derivations above, we can conclude self-consistently that also in the nematic structure the constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-constraint},
\[
	0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
\]
holds. In the $s^*$-wave channel, partial breaking of rotational symmetry is not enough to modify the main effect identified under $\mathrm{C}_4$ symmetry, ``bands shrinking''. What is missing is the behaviour of the $d$-wave channel when said effect takes place. Continuing the enumeration above,

\begin{enumerate}
	\setcounter{enumi}{\theenumTemp}
	\item Let $0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V$ and $u^{(d)} > 0$. Then $\epsilon^{(d)}>0$. The self-consistency equation for $u^{(d)}$ reads
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		u^{(d)} &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x - \cos k_y) f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{NBZ}} (\cos k_x - \cos k_y) \left[
			f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) - f(\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
		\right]
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where we recognised, as is shown in Fig.~\ref{subfig:NBZ-sectors}, that $\mathrm{BZ} \setminus \mathrm{NBZ} = \mathcal{R} \mathrm{NBZ}$. It holds:
	\[
		\forall \mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{NBZ}
		\quad\colon\quad
		\begin{aligned}
			\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} &= \epsilon^{(s^*)} \left( \cos k_x + \cos k_y \right) + \overbrace{\epsilon^{(d)}  \left( \cos k_x - \cos k_y \right) }^{\ge 0} \\
			\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}} &= \epsilon^{(s^*)} \left( \cos k_x + \cos k_y \right) - \underbrace{\epsilon^{(d)}  \left( \cos k_x - \cos k_y \right) }_{\ge 0}
		\end{aligned}
	\]
	thus $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{R}\mathbf{k}} < \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}$, and inserting this fact in the self-consistency equation above we get
	\[
		0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
		\qq{and}
		u^{(d)} > 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(d)} < 0
	\]
	which is absurd.
	%
	\item An identical argument, with inverted inequalities, can be carried out to show that
	\[
		0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
		\qq{and}
		u^{(d)} < 0
		\quad\implies\quad
		u^{(d)} > 0
	\]
	%
	\item The two points above imply that it is possible to satisfy the constraint $0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V$ if and only if $u^{(d)}=0$.
\end{enumerate}
All the deductions above indicate that $u^{(d)}=0$ everywhere. Nematic effects are always absent, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-us*-ud-constraint}
	0 < u^{(s^*)} < 2t/V
	\qq{and}
	u^{(d)} = 0
\end{equation}
This is physically expected but non trivial. The normal phase does not break $\mathrm{C}_4$ and we can ignore $u^{(d)}$ at all. In next section we identify the expected self-consistent value for $u^{(s^*)}$.

\subsection{Expected dependence on $\delta$ at low temperature}\label{subsec:normal-uS-doping}

We understood that $u^{(d)}=0$. Moreover, the unique HFP $u^{(s^*)}$ is independent of $U$, but in general it will depend in some way on the doping level. We work at low-temperature, $\beta \to \infty$. Consider the self-consistency equation of Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-ehm-ugamma-self-consistencies} as a function of temperature and chemical potential,
\[
	u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
\]
in the zero-temperature limit, as long as the band is not flat, it holds
\[
	\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}(\beta,\tilde{\mu}) = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\epsilon_\mathbf{k} \le \tilde{\mu}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y)
\]
Let $\mathrm{FS}(\delta)$ be the Fermi surface for a given doping level $\delta$. We denote with $\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{FS})$ be its internal part, the Fermi sea:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\mathrm{FS} = \left\lbrace \mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ} \mid \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} = \tilde{\mu} \right\rbrace \\
	\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{FS}) = \left\lbrace \mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ} \mid \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \le \tilde{\mu} \right\rbrace
\end{aligned}
\]

At half filling, the Fermi surface coincides with the boundary of the MBZ:
\[
	\delta = 0
	\quad\implies\quad
	\mathrm{FS} = \partial \mathrm{MBZ}
\]
This is implied by the fact that renormalised bands are antisymmetric by $\bm{\pi}$ shifts,
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = -\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
\]
The portion of an $s^*$-wave band outside the MBZ is mirrored with respect to the portion inside the MBZ. This implies that half the states lie in the MBZ, half out.

At $\delta>0$ the FS expands in the region $\mathrm{BZ}\setminus\mathrm{MBZ}$ where the form factor $\cos k_x + \cos k_y$ becomes negative. This gives the self-consistency equation
\[
	\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \simeq \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\epsilon_\mathbf{k} \le 0} \abs{\cos k_x + \cos k_y} - \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{0<\epsilon_\mathbf{k} \le \tilde{\mu}} \abs{\cos k_x + \cos k_y}
\]
which, being the sum of a positive and a negative term, is maximised whenever $\tilde{\mu}=0$, at half filling. This holds as long as the band is not flat. Indeed, if the effective hopping is not zero, to rise the doping level means to 
\[
	\delta_1 > \delta_2
	\quad\implies\quad
	\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{FS}_1) \subset \mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{FS}_2)
\]
with obvious meaning of the notation. Using this property we conclude
\[
	\tilde{t} > 0
	\qq{and}
	\delta_1 > \delta_2
	\quad\implies\quad
	\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \Big|_{\delta_1} < 
	\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \Big|_{\delta_2}
\]
This is the first expected behaviour of the self-consistent functional $u^{(s^*)}$: if there exists a region of parametric space where the shrinking effect is not strong enough to flatten the band, a rise in the doping level must lower $u^{(s^*)}$. Such a region must exist: at least all the segment $(V=0,\delta)$ is included, since on said region of parametric space the model is just the common free electronic model.

Following this line of reasoning, we can extract the two low-temperature limits of $u^{(s^*)}$ at half-filling and unitary filling.
\begin{enumerate}
	\item At half-filling, the self-consistency equation reduces to
	\[
		u^{(s^*)}[\beta,0] \big|_{\beta \gg 1} \simeq \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y)
	\]
	In thermodynamic limit, performing integration on the MBZ shown in the top-left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-nematic-bands}:
	\begin{align}
		\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}[\beta,0] &\simeq \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_y \int_{-\pi+\abs{k_y}}^{\pi-\abs{k_y}} \mathrm{d} k_x \cos k_x + \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_x \int_{-\pi+\abs{k_x}}^{\pi-\abs{k_x}} \mathrm{d} k_y \cos k_y \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{1}{2\pi^2} \int_{-\pi}^{0} \mathrm{d} k_y \int_{-\pi-k_y}^{\pi+k_y} \mathrm{d} k_x \cos k_x + \frac{1}{2\pi^2} \int_{0}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_y \int_{-\pi+k_y}^{\pi-k_y} \mathrm{d} k_x \cos k_x \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{1}{\pi^2} \int_{-\pi}^{0} \mathrm{d} k_y \sin (\pi+k_y) + \frac{1}{\pi^2} \int_{0}^{\pi} \mathrm{d} k_y \sin (\pi-k_y) \nonumber \\
		&= \left( \frac{2}{\pi} \right)^2 \approx 0.405 \label{eq:normal-uS-half-filling}
	\end{align}
	\item At unitary filling we have
	\[
		\lim_{\beta \to \infty} u^{(s^*)}\left[
			\beta,2\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}
		\right] = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) = 0
	\]
	which vanishes due to the structure factor periodicity by $\bm{\pi}$.
\end{enumerate}
To summarise: around $V=0$, $u^{(s^*)}$ is expected to descend monotonically from $4/\pi^2$ to $0$ at increasing doping.

\section{Discussion of numeric results}\label{sec:normal-numeric}

In this section we present and discuss the numeric results, the physical relevance of the found phenomena and the boundaries of the algorithm. Let us start by sketching the algorithm used to determine self-consistently the mean-field structure of the normal phase in parameter space. For the normal phase, the HFPs ``vector'' defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate} has just one component:
\[
	\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
		u^{(s^*)}
	\end{bmatrix}
\]
As before, we treat spin as a pure degeneracy. The only HFP is characterised by the first self-consistency equation of Tab.~\ref{tab:normal-ehm-ugamma-self-consistencies}. The resulting model has two RMPs:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\tilde{t}_{ij} &\equiv \left(
		t - \frac{u^{(s^*)}V}{2}
	\right) \varphi_{ij}^{(s^*)} \\
	\tilde{\mu} &\equiv \mu + n (2zV-U)
\end{aligned}
\]
the second one being only dependent on the model parameters and not on HFPs. 
Finally, the phase free energy is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT}. The normal phase is completely independent of $U$. Because of this we present the results varying the other parameters, keeping everywhere $U=0$. We ran simulations in the $(V, \delta)$ plane, with the setup:
\[
	U=0
	\qquad
	\Delta n = 10^{-2}
	\qquad
	\Delta \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
		u^{(s^*)} \colon 5 \times 10^{-4}
	\end{bmatrix}
\]
with $\Delta \mathbf{v}$ the tolerance vector defined as in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}.

When $V$ gets large enough,  $u^{(s^*)}$ starts decreasing because of its constraints. The net effect is the nullification of $\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$, which is, a total localisation effect.

\subsection{Mott localisation effect}\label{subsec:normal-mott-numeric}

The most interesting result we obtained for the normal phase is the possibility of localising electrons by tuning the $V/\delta$ ratio. In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-uS-data-B256}, at low $V$ the convergence value of $u^{(s^*)}$ is approximately $V$-independent at sufficiently low temperatures. Let $V_\mathrm{c}[\beta,\delta]$ be the critical value such that
\[
	0 \le V < V_\mathrm{c}[\beta,\delta]
	\quad\colon\quad
	\text{$u^{(s^*)}$ is $V$ independent.}
\]
Then within the entire segment $[0,V_\mathrm{c}]$ the convergence value is (approximately) the same obtained for $V=0$. We can define in first instance the critical $V$ as the value such that the hopping correction is maximized,
\[
	V_\mathrm{c}[\beta,\delta] \equiv \frac{2t}{u^{(s^*)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}(\delta)] \big|_{V=0}}
\]
When $V$ becomes larger than this critical value, the resulting RMP $\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$ drops to $0$ and due to the constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-constraint} the convergence value of $u^{(s^*)}$ is forced to decrease. Note that the constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-constraint} does not prevent $u^{(s^*)}$ from saturating to its upper boundary from below. In Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-tS-data-B128} the converged values of $\tilde{t}^{(s^*)}$, extracted from the same data of Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-uS-data-B128}, are plotted in parametric space.

\subsection{Solution free energy and entropy densities}\label{subsec:normal-f-s}

In order to compare phases, we need an estimation of free energy of each. The plots of Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-f-data} report our findings on the normal phase free energy, computed based on Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT}. The free energy is intended in unities of $t$. For a non-flat band, doping increases energy because the band fills up and electrons populate higher energy levels. 

Looking the data of Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-f-data-B128}, at fixed $\delta$ free energy density remains pretty much constant up to the Mott boundary. This is simply because as long as a band with non-zero amplitude is present, the HFP correction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-fMFT} compensates the free energy increase due to bands shrinking,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	- V \big| u^{(s^*)} \big|^2 &= - \frac{V u^{(s^*)*}}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \left( \epsilon_\mathbf{k} - \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} \right)  f(\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k};\beta,\tilde{\mu})
\end{aligned}
\]
having we used in the second passage
\[
	\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} = \tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k} + u^{(s^*)*} V (\cos k_x + \cos k_y)
\]
Recalling the first point of the derivation of Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-free-energy}, to add the HFP correction to free energy is the same as removing the \textit{tilde} sign from single-state energies, thus using the free model ($V=0$) free energy density expression.

\subsection{Convergence problems and mixing fine-tuning}\label{subsec:normal-g-optimization}

The normal phase is simple enough to describe some of the typical convergence problems arising in HF iterative schemes anticipated in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}. Bands flattening is a source of algorithmic instability: numerics can lead to values of $u^{(s^*)}$, during iterations, slightly out of bounds with respect to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-constraint}. Even if the numeric effect is small, the consequence can be vast. To heal this effect and extract the actual convergence values, it is necessary to optimise the mixing parameter $g \in [0,1]$. 

\begin{figure}[t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../Project/src/labs/tracks/tracks.pdf}
	\caption{Comparison of three example tracks for the single-valued HFPs vector of the normal phase, simulated at a ``Mott-deep'' point.}
	\label{fig:normal-tracks-compared}
\end{figure}

We defined $g$ in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate}, and operatively in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mix}. This parameter controls deterministically how, from an initialiser (the HFPs vector we plug in the algorithm) and an output (the HFPs vector we get back from the algorithm) the new initialiser for the following step is generated. We show in Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-tracks-compared} the evolutions at different mixing parameters of a simulation at
\[
	V = 6.0
	\qquad
	\delta = 0.1
	\qquad
	\beta = 100.0
\]
This point is inside the Mott region, as can be checked in Fig.~\ref{subfig:normal-uS-data-B128}. The HFP $u^{(s^*)}$ needs to converge at $2t/V=t/3$ in order to give complete localisation. The initialiser
\[
	\text{Step $1$}
	\quad\colon\quad
	u^{(s^*)} = 0.1
\]
was arbitrarily chosen. In each panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-tracks-compared}, the red track represents the value of $u^{(s^*)}$ we plug in the self-consistency equations, the blue the result of the self-consistent equation, the green one the mixed value of the two based on different $g$s. The gray dashed line is the theoretical convergence target. 

In the first two panels, there is an evident infinite loop occurring whenever the green line crosses the threshold $2t/V$. At those steps, the green point is out of bounds with respect to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-us*-constraint}. When this happens, the following red point gives a band with inverted sign, a feature that ``breaks'' the self-consistency equation and generates a blue point with no physical significance.

To overcome this problem it is necessary to move ``slowly''. As is shown in the right panel, the choice of a small enough mixing parameter ensures convergence. In the track, each blue dot is above the target and would produce a band with inverted sign. Mixing blue and red dots with a $95\%$ unbalance on the red ones, we ensure that the green track approaches the target from below. Note, however, that lowering $g$ cannot be reiterated too many times. Indeed, for $g \to 0$, any point of our map becomes a fixed point. 

This kind of problems can turn out to be pretty common for iHF schemes, and to understand how to knob the algorithmic parameters without sacrificing accurateness and precision, as well as avoiding computationally costly and frustrating infinite loops, was an essential part of this algorithm design.
