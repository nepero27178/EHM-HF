\chapter{Antiferromagnetic instability}\label{chap:af}

In this chapter we study the AF phase in the EHM. Among all possible configurations, we limit to the so-called ``commensurate antiferromagnetism''. Moreover, we will restrict to the half-filling condition, based on stability considerations. 

This phase is well known and studied on the square Hubbard lattice. Insertion of the non-local attraction $\hat H_V$ adds two main self-consistent effects. First, an enhancement of the antiferromagnetic gap, an effect we call ``gap complexification''. While this effect is allowed at HF level, we derive some theoretical constraint and show that is actually suppressed. Second, the shrinking of bare bands that lead to Mott localisation in the normal phase and, in the end, to a amplification of magnetisation.

This chapter is divided in four parts. In Sec.~\ref{sec:af-phase-theory} we present the commensurate AF phase and discuss its symmetries. \todo

\section{The AF phase and its symmetries}\label{sec:af-phase-theory}

Long-range antiferromagnetic ordering is not uniquely defined. In Fig.~\ref{fig:antiferromagnets} we show three (of many) antiferromagnets, with the blue arrows indicating an abundance of spin-up electrons on-site and the red arrows an abundance of spin-down electrons. For all three configurations, we have a staggered pattern of spin density with a given periodicity.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\input{\GraphicsFolder/antiferromagnets.tex}
	\caption{Three possible antiferromagnetic structures. Dots and arrows indicate spin abundance on a given site, while empty sites are intended as spin-balanced. Within this text we deal with the lowest-periodicity commensurate phase, the antiferromagnet on left panel.}
	\label{fig:antiferromagnets}
\end{figure}

The left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:antiferromagnets} shows an alternating structure with two-sites periodicity in both directions. We denote this configuration ``commensurate''. The central panel presents another configuration AF with doubled periodicity, where sites with a given polarisation are intermediated by spin-balanced sites (gray spots). The right panel shows a possible ``striped'' antiferromagnet, where AF is effectively established along one particular direction; the example we report is $2$ sites periodic in $y$ direction, but any other periodicity could work. Striped antiferromagnetism is studied in HM physics \cite{wietek2021stripes} as a possible long-range ordering energetically competing with anisotropic superconductivity. Nematic antiferromagnets, establishing long-range magnetic ordering along one particular direction, are interesting due to competition and interplay against superconducting ordering at finite doping \cite{qin2020absence}. Geometrically non-trivial magnetic ordering are at the core of SDW formation in copper oxides at finite doping \cite{zaanen1989charged,kato1990soliton}.

In this thesis, we focus on the commensurate configuration on the left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:antiferromagnets}. As we anticipated in Sec.~\ref{sec:ehm-sym-break} and summarised in Sec.~\ref{tab:symmetry-groups}, the AF phase is described by the breaking of global spin rotation and translational symmetries:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\mathrm{SU}(2) &\SSB \mathrm{U}^z(1) \\
	\mathrm{T}_1^{(x)} \otimes \mathrm{T}_1^{(y)} &\SSB \mathrm{T}_2^{(x)} \otimes \mathrm{T}_2^{(y)}
\end{aligned}
\]
As we did for the normal phase, we also allow for
\[
\mathrm{C}_4 \SSB \mathrm{C}_2
\]
We will show that the commensurate AF phase actually leaves $\mathrm{C}_4$ unbroken.

Commensurate antiferromagnetism is specified by the Ansatz:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ansatz}
	\langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \rangle = n - (-1)^{x+y+\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}} m
	\qquad
	n, m \in [0,1]
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{r}=(x,y) \in \mathcal{L}$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} represents the staggered distribution of spin populations shown in the left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:antiferromagnets}. At site $\mathbf{r}$ the two spin populations average to $n$ and differ by $2m$. A detailed justification for this Ansatz in the HM is given in next subsection. Note that $\langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \rangle \le 1$ due to Pauli exclusion principle: this argument is already sufficient to claim that doping disrupts commensurate antiferromagnetism.

\subsection{Symmetries of the commensurate AF Ansatz}

The Ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} breaks translational invariance in each spin sector, but preserves $\mathrm{U}^z(1)$ and $\mathrm{U}^\mathrm{c}(1)$ symmetries. Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} is formulated equivalently as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ansatz-2m}
	\langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \rangle = (-1)^{x+y} 2m
\end{equation}
Antiferromagnetism establishes when $m \neq 0$.

\begin{tabproof}[The Ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} is $\mathrm{U}^z(1)$-symmetric]
	We use the spinors $\hat \Psi_\mathbf{r}$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Psi-onsite} 
	\[
	\hat \Psi_\mathbf{r} = \begin{bmatrix}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \\ \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\]
	and the local spin operators of Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-S},
	\[
	\hat S_\mathbf{r}^+ = \frac{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}{2}
	\qquad
	\hat S_\mathbf{r}^- = \frac{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}}{2}
	\qquad
	\hat S_\mathbf{r}^z = \frac{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} + \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}{2}
	\]
	Consider Eq.~\eqref{eq:[c+,n]} and its conjugate,
	\[
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}} = -\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger
	\qquad
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}} = \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\]
	The operators $\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}$ and $\hat S_\mathbf{r}^\pm$ do not commutate. Indeed,
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\comm{\hat S_\mathbf{r}^+}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} &= \frac{1}{2} \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} - \frac{1}{2} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} \\
		&= - \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \\[0.5em]
		\comm{\hat S_\mathbf{r}^-}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} &= \frac{1}{2} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} - \frac{1}{2} \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \\
		&= \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	Instead, $\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}$ and $\hat S_\mathbf{r}^z$ commutate, because all on-site number operators commutate among themselves. Then, in order to have $m \neq 0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz-2m}, $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ must be broken while $\mathrm{U}^z(1)$ needs not. Therefore the commensurate AF phase breaks global spin rotational invariance, reducing it to the smaller symmetry of rotations around the magnetisation axis.
	
\end{tabproof}

\begin{tabproof}[The Ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} is $\mathrm{U}^\mathrm{c}(1)$-symmetric]
	
	From Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge-symmetry-c+c-} we know that on-site density operators are gauge-invariant separately in both spin sectors,
	\[
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\quad\to\quad
	e^{i(\eta - \eta)} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} = \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\]
	Then no selection rule applies to $\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}$: its EV can be non-zero without breaking $\mathrm{U}^z(1)$.
	
\end{tabproof}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} contains two physical observables: the average density $n$ and the staggered magnetisation $m$. These two quantities obey the equations:
\begin{align}
	n &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} + \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2} \label{eq:af-hm-n-self-consistency-implicit}\\
	m &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} - \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2} \label{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency-implicit}
\end{align}
Both are self-consistency equations, in the sense that the ground state that we use to compute expectation values depends on $n$ and $m$. However, $n$ is fixed, hence Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-n-self-consistency-implicit} is used to determine the chemical potential. Instead magnetisation must be determined, thus Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency-implicit} is the self-consistency equation for the HFP $m$.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-n-self-consistency-implicit} and \eqref{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency-implicit}]
	
	The on-site averaged density is given by:
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-n-def}
		\forall \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}
		\quad\colon\quad 
		n \equiv \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} + \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2}
	\end{equation}
	while on-site magnetisation is, inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz-2m},
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-m-def}
		\forall \mathbf{r} = (x,y) \in \mathcal{L}
		\quad\colon\quad 
		m \equiv (-1)^{x+y} \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} - \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2}
	\end{equation}		
	By construction, both $n$ and $m$ are translationally invariant quantities. Hence
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		n &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} + \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2} \\
		m &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} - \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2}
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	which ends the proof.
\end{tabproof}

\subsection{The AF Ansatz in reciprocal space}\label{subsec:af-ansatz-reciprocal}

The derivations that follow will be carried out in reciprocal space. It is then useful to give the reciprocal structure of the AF Ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz}. Let $\bm{\pi} \equiv (\pi,\pi)$ be the ``AF vector''. We define
\begin{align}
	\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} &\equiv \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \label{eq:af-ehm-n-def} \\
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} &\equiv \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \label{eq:af-ehm-psi-def}
\end{align}
In the AF phase, the order parameter describing the phase transition to a magnetised configuration is $m$. A finite magnetisation implies, at least for a subset of momenta $\lbrace \mathbf{k} \rbrace$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-psi>0}
	m \neq 0
	\quad\implies\quad
	\exists \mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\big| \langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle  \big| > 0
\end{equation}
The above statement expresses a feature of the AF ground-state: states separated by an amount $\bm{\pi}$ are connected. Removing one electron at $(\mathbf{k},\sigma)$ and creating another at $(\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi},\sigma)$ produces a state with a finite overlap with the ground-state.

\begin{tabproof}[If $m \neq 0$, for some $\mathbf{k}$ it must hold $\langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle \neq 0$]
	
	We assume $m \neq 0$. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} we get
	\begin{align}
		(-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} m &=  \overbrace{\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} n}^{=0} + \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} m \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \left(
		n + (-1)^{x+y+\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} m
		\right) \nonumber \\
		&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \rangle \label{eq:af-m-EV-int}
	\end{align}
	where we used $1 = \left((-1)^{x+y}\right)^2$. Fourie-transforming the operator and its sign factor inside the sum in last line, we get
	\begin{align}
		\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} &= \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \nonumber \\
		&\stackrel{\mathcal{F}}{=} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} e^{i \bm{\pi} \cdot \mathbf{r}}
		\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_x L_y}} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} e^{i \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_x L_y}} \sum_{\mathbf{k}' \in \mathrm{BZ}} e^{-i \mathbf{k}' \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \nonumber \\
		&=\sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_{\mathbf{k}' \in \mathrm{BZ}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} e^{-i [\mathbf{k}' - (\mathbf{k} + \bm{\pi}) ] \cdot \mathbf{r}} \nonumber \\
		&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma} \label{eq:af-<psi>-not0-int} \\
		&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \label{eq:af-<psi>-not0}
	\end{align}
	We insert the last line in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-m-EV-int},
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-m-EV}
		(-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} m = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle
	\end{equation}
	Since $m \neq 0$, at least for some momentum $\mathbf{k}$ it must hold $\langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle \neq 0$.	
	
\end{tabproof}

Note that from Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-m-EV} we have
\begin{align}
	m &= \frac{1}{L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow} \rangle \label{eq:af-m-EV-up}\\
	&= -\frac{1}{L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow} \rangle \label{eq:af-m-EV-down}
\end{align}
Composing the two lines, we obtain
\begin{equation}
	m = \frac{1}{2L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \langle \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow} - \psi_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow} \rangle \label{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency-reciprocal}
\end{equation}
which is the reciprocal version of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency-implicit}. We will use this version of the self-consistency equation in later calculations.

We conclude this subsection by proving a symmetry property that will be useful. Recall the functions $\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}$ defined in Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}. 
The operator $\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ satisfies the relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-vphi-psi-property}
	\varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)} \psi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger = - \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)} \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-vphi-psi-property}]
	The following identity holds
	\begin{align}
		\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger &= \left(
		\hat c_{\mathbf{k}+2\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
		\right)^\dagger \nonumber \\
		&= \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \nonumber \\
		&= \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \label{eq:af-(psi+pi)=psi}
	\end{align}
	Moreover, being combinations of sines and cosines, the functions $\varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)}$ are antisymmetric under $\bm{\pi}$ shifts,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-(phi+pi)=-phi}
		\varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)} = -\varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}
	\end{equation}
	Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-(psi+pi)=psi} and \eqref{eq:af-(phi+pi)=-phi}, we obtain Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-vphi-psi-property}.
\end{tabproof}

\section{The AF phase in the conventional HM}\label{sec:af-hm}

We dedicate this section to describe antiferromagnetism in the conventional HM, $\hat H_t + \hat H_U$. The two-body repulsive interaction is
\[
\hat H_U = U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
\]
We know from Tab.~\ref{tab:HU-sel-rules} that the only contribution to the HF hamiltonian comes from Hartree channel, while Fock and Cooper channel are identically zero in the AF phase. In next subsection we derive the analytic form of the approximated hamiltonian.

\subsection{Local Hartree effects: magnetisation and chemical potential renormalisation}\label{subsec:af-l-hartree}

In Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HU} we decomposed $\hat H_U$ through Wick's theorem. The Hartree channel reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-HU-hartree-approx-implicit}
	\hat H_U \HFsimeq U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \left[
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} + \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} - \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}
	\right]
\end{equation}
We define the MBZ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:MBZ-def}, as the set of points in reciprocal space that satisfy the inequality $\abs{k_x} + \abs{k_y} \le \pi$. We sketched it in Fig.~\ref{fig:normal-MBZ}. Applying the Ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz}, we obtain the HF approximation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-HU-hartree-approx}
	\hat H_U \HFsimeq -\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \left(
	\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^* \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\right) + nU \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
\end{equation}
where we defined the gap function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hm-Delta-def}
	\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} mU
\end{equation}
We also defined the energy shift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit}
	E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})} \equiv U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}
\end{equation}

We observe, in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-gap-psi}, the presence of a number operator. Hence, chemical potential is shifted in the Hartree channel by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-mu-hU}
	\mu \to \mu - nU
\end{equation}
Note that this shift coincides with that obtained in the normal phase for the local repulsion, given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-mu-hU}.

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-approx}]
	
	Consider Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-approx-implicit}. Using Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-n-self-consistency-implicit}, \eqref{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency-implicit} we can re-write
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} + \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		&= \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} + \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2} \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		\right) - \frac{\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} - \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}}{2} \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		\right) \\
		&= n \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		\right) - m \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		\right)
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	Inserting this decomposition and the expression for $E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-approx-implicit}, we obtain
	\begin{equation} \label{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit-int1}
		\hat H_U \HFsimeq nU \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		\right) - mU \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
		\right) - E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
	\end{equation}
	The first term gives a number operator,
	\[
	nU \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \left(
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
	\right) = nU \times \hat N
	\]
	
	Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-<psi>-not0-int}, the second term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit} becomes
	\[
	mU \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \left(
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
	\right) = mU \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\]
	Recall the definition of the MBZ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:MBZ-def}. The full BZ can be seen as the sum of two MBZ shifted by $\bm{\pi}$. Then, from Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-(psi+pi)=psi}, we get
	\[
	mU \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} (-1)^{x+y} \left(
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
	\right) = mU \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} \left(
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\right)
	\]
	Composing everything, Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit-int1} becomes
	\[
	\hat H_U \HFsimeq - mU \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} \left(
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\right) + nU \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
	\]
	By defining the gap function $\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-Delta-def}, we obtain the HF approximation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-approx}.
	
\end{tabproof}

The contraction energy, defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit}, is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hm-hartree-U-Ec}
	E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = L_x L_y \times U \left(
	n^2 - m^2
	\right)
\end{equation}
Compared with that for the normal phase given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-HU-hartree-Ec}, we observe the presence of an additional term dependent on $m^2$.

\begin{tabproof}[Local Hartree contraction energy.]
	
	Inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-hartree-Ec-implicit}, we obtain
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})} &\equiv U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} \\
		&= U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \left(
		n + (-1)^{x+y} m
		\right) \left(
		n - (-1)^{x+y} m
		\right) \\
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	Expanding the product above we get:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\left(
		n + (-1)^{x+y} m
		\right) \left(
		n - (-1)^{x+y} m
		\right) &= n^2 + \left(
		(-1)^{x+y} - (-1)^{x+y}
		\right)nm - \left((-1)^{x+y}\right)^2 m^2 \\
		&= n^2 - m^2
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	The sum over $\mathcal{L}$ gives $L_x L_y$ identical terms. Hence we obtain Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-hartree-U-Ec}.
	
\end{tabproof}

\subsection{Antiferromagnetism in the HM}\label{subsec:af-hm}

One of the aims of this projects is to identify, theoretically, what changes for each phase due to the presence of the non-local attraction $\hat H_V$ with respect to the conventional HM. In this section we give a description of antiferromagnetism in the conventional HM, and later we move to the EHM.

\paragraph{Matrix representation.}

The HM hamiltonian contains the kinetic operator and the local repulsion,
\[
\hat H_t + \hat H_U
\]
For the AF phase, the HF hamiltonian can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-HM-hk}
	\hat H_t + \hat H_U \simeq \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + nU \hat N -  E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
\end{equation}
where we defined the $2 \times 2$ matrix:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hk}
	h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & -\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^* \\
		-\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & - \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\begin{tabproof}[Proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HM-hk}]
	
	Let the reciprocal space spinors:
	\[
	\hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \\
		\hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma} 
	\end{bmatrix}
	\]
	Recall Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ht-Fourier}: the kinetic sector is represented in reciprocal space by
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\hat H_t &= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma \epsilon_\mathbf{k} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \\ 
		&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \epsilon_\mathbf{k} \left(\hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}\right) 
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	where we used antiperiodicity by $\bm{\pi}$ of the bare bands $\epsilon_\mathbf{k}$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bare-bands},
	\[
	\epsilon_\mathbf{k} = -\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}
	\]
	The kinetic operator can be written in terms of the spinors $\hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$,
	\[
	\hat H_t = \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \begin{bmatrix}
		\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & \\ & -\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\end{bmatrix} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\]
	The local repulsion $\hat H_U$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-gap-psi} can also be written in terms of these spinors:
	\[
	\hat H_U = \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in  \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \begin{bmatrix}
		& -\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^* \\  -\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & 
	\end{bmatrix} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}  + nU \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
	\]
	Summing $\hat H_t + \hat H_U$ and defining $h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hk}, we obtain the matricial representation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HM-hk}.
	
\end{tabproof}

\textbf{FROM HERE}

This is a parametric hamiltonian. We need to identify its ground state for each choice of the parameters $\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$. This is done by mapping the problem to the one of a set of spin subject to local magnetic fields: let us show this. 

\paragraph{Pseudospin representation.} The matrix $h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ can be written in term of the Pauli matrices $\tau^\alpha$ 
\[
h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^z - \Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^x
\]
Let us define the pseudospin operator $\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$
\[
\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\alpha \equiv \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^\alpha \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\qq{with}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \begin{bmatrix}
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^x \\
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y \\
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^z
\end{bmatrix}
\]
and the pseudofield $\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$:
\[
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
	-\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \\ 0 \\ \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} 
\end{bmatrix}
\]
These operators satisfy a $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ algebra up to a constant $C$,
\[
\comm{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\alpha}{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\beta} = C \times i\sum_\gamma \epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma} \hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\gamma
\]
The proof is conceptually identical to the one presented in Sec.~\ref{subsec:spin-charge-syms}. The actual value of $C$ is irrelevant, as we will see in next paragraphs. {\color{tabred}\textbf{The two set of spin operators are unrelated.}} Let us express the pseudospin operator in terms of the operators of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-n-def}, \eqref{eq:af-ehm-psi-def}:
\begin{align}
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^x &= \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \label{eq:af-hm-x-psi} \\
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y &= i \left(
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger 
	\right) \label{eq:af-hm-y-psi} \\
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^z &= \hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \hat n_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma} \label{eq:af-hm-z-psi} 
\end{align}
We report these relations also in Tab.~\ref{tab:af-pseudo-spin-map}.

Let also:
\begin{align}
	\hat \Id_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} &\equiv \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \Id_{2\times2} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \nonumber \\
	&= \hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma} \label{eq:af-hm-id-def}
\end{align}

In pseudospin representation we get:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hm-spin-h}
	\hat H_t + \hat H_U \simeq \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + nU \hat N - E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
\end{equation}
The hamiltonian represents a system of spins subject to local magnetic fields.

	\label{fig:pseudo-magnetic-field-hm}
\end{figure}

The field $\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is orthogonal to the $y$ plane, and is tilted in the $z$ plane by an angle $2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ with:
\[
\cos (2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}) = \frac{\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
\qquad
\sin (2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}) = \frac{\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
\qquad
E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv 	\sqrt{\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^2 + \abs{\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}^2}
\]
A sketch of these angles and the pseudofield is given in Fig.~\ref{fig:pseudo-magnetic-field-hm}.

\paragraph{Diagonalisation.}

To diagonalise the Hubbard hamiltonian $\hat H_t + \hat H_U$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-spin-h} means to perform some unitary operation on the $\hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ spinors which reduces the $2 \times 2$ matrix to a diagonal form. This means to diagonalise each $h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$. This is obtained through a rotation around the $y$ axis:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-WhW+-hm}
	d_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\qq{with}
	d_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & \\
		& - E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
The matrix $W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is unitary, and gives a counter-clockwise rotation by an angle $2 \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ around the $y$ axis:
\begin{align}
	W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} &\equiv e^{-i \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^y} \nonumber\\
	&= \cos \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} -i \sin \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^y \nonumber\\
	&= \begin{bmatrix}
		\cos \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & - \sin \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \\
		\sin \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & \cos \theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\end{bmatrix} \label{eq:af-hm-diagonalizer}
\end{align}
Its eigenvalues are $\pm E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-bands}
	E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \sqrt{
		\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^2 + \abs{\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}^2
	}
\end{equation}
In Fig.~\ref{fig:pseudo-magnetic-field-hm} we sketch the pseudo-magnetic field in the $xz$ plane, and the diagonalisation procedure. $W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is a rotation by an angle $2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ around the $y$ axis, and in the end aligns the original $z$ axis with the pseudofield axis.

Applying the rotation matrix to the spinors we obtain new spinors of $\gamma^{(\pm)}$ fermions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-Gamma=WPsi}
	\hat\Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \begin{bmatrix}
		\hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)} \\ \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
In the end we have the non-interacting hamiltonian:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_t + \hat H_U &\simeq \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + (\cdots) \\
	&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + (\cdots) \\
	&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger d_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + (\cdots) \\
	&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \sum_\sigma E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \left(
	\hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)\dagger} \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)} - \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)\dagger} \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}
	\right) + (\cdots) 
\end{aligned}
\]
where we intend $(\cdots) = nU \hat N -  E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})}$. The final non-interacting hamiltonian describes a free Fermi gas of $\gamma^{(\pm)}$ fermions, respectively on the bands $\pm E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$. Note that these two bands are separated by a gap $\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$.

We could redefine the pseudospin operators dividing them by an appropriate constant, in order to satisfy exactly the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ algebra, and at the same time multiply the pseudo-magnetic field by the same constant. The product $\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ would be unchanged, and the diagonalisation matrix $W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ would be the same. Because of this, the aforementioned constant $C$ is irrelevant.

\subsection{Features of the AF solution}\label{subsec:af-features}

This subsection is devoted to discussing briefly some features of the AF solution we found. It is organised as a series of unrelated paragraphs, each to derive a precise property or formula we use in the algorithmic computation. With these observations we conclude the part about the local repulsion and extend to full EHM.

\paragraph{Pseudospin EVs.}

The expectation value of the original pseudospin operators in the various directions can be expressed in terms of the tilted pseudospins $\hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^\alpha \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\langle \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^x \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &= -\sin 2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle \\
	\langle \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^y \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &= 0 \\
	\langle \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &= \cos 2\theta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used the angular relations of Fig.~\ref{fig:pseudo-magnetic-field-hm}. Moving to the tilted frame, the $z$ component of the pseudo-spin is just a thermal superposition of ``up'' and ``down'' states
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-<z>}
	\langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle =
	f\left(
	E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\tilde{\mu}
	\right) - f\left(
	-E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\tilde{\mu}
	\right)
\end{equation}
Let us define the thermal factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-Th}
	\mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(\pm)}[\beta,\mu] \equiv 
	f\left(
	-E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\mu
	\right) \pm f\left(
	E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\mu
	\right)
\end{equation}
which will often appear in computations. We have:
\begin{align}
	\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^x} &= \frac{\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} \mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-hm-sx}\\
	\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y} &= 0 \\
	\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^z} &=  -\frac{\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}  \mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-hm-sz}
\end{align}
Gauge invariance guarantees that we can rotate the pseudo-field on the $xy$ plane, thus obtaining $\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y} \neq 0$. From these EVs, we get the expression of the self-consistency equation for $m$, and the self-consistent formula to compute the density. Note that $E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is actually spin independent, and so is the thermal factor. We can neglect the spin index from both.

\paragraph{Magnetisation self-consistency equation.}

Using Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-m-EV-up}, \eqref{eq:af-m-EV-down} we get:
\[
m = \frac{1}{2L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \left\langle
\left(\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow} + \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^\dagger\right) - \left(\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow} + \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow}^\dagger\right)
\right\rangle
\]
From Tab.~\ref{tab:af-pseudo-spin-map}, we recognise a pseudospin EV in direction $x$. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-sx}
\begin{align}
	m &= \frac{1}{2L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\langle
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^x
	\rangle
	- 
	\langle
	\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow}^x
	\rangle
	\right] \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{1}{2L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Re\lbrace\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}\rbrace-\Re\lbrace\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow}\rbrace}{E_\mathbf{k}} \mathrm{Th}_\mathbf{k}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}}{E_\mathbf{k}} \mathrm{Th}_\mathbf{k}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency}
\end{align}
In last passage we used $\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow} = -\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow}$, which follows from the definition of the gap function. The last passage gives the operational self-consistency equation for the HFP $m$.

\paragraph{Density.}

Restricting the sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-n-EV} to the MBZ and extending to both spins, we have:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	n &= \frac{1}{2L_x L_y} \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \langle 
	\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
	\rangle \\
	&= \frac{1}{2L_x L_y} \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \left\langle 
	\Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} 
	\right\rangle \\
	&= \frac{1}{2L_x L_y} \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \left\langle 
	\Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} 
	\right\rangle \\
	&= \frac{1}{2L_x L_y} \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \left(
	\left\langle \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)\dagger} \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)} \right\rangle + 
	\left\langle \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)\dagger} \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)} \right\rangle
	\right) \\
	&= \frac{1}{2L_x L_y} \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)}[\beta,\mu]
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used unitarity of the $\hat \Psi \to \hat \Gamma$ transformation.

The thermal factor is spin-independent. Moreover, the Bogoliubov bands $E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ are MBZ periodic. Thus we get the two equivalent formulas for density:
\begin{align}
	n &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{(+)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-hm-n-MBZ-Th} \\
	&= \frac{1}{2L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{(+)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-hm-n-BZ-Th}
\end{align}
The two are perfectly equivalent, but the second one is handier to implement in numeric computations. In Fig.~\ref{fig:af-Ek-density} we sketch density as a function of the chemical potential. At low temperature, the gap plateau at zero energy is most visible. The lattice is half-filled for any chemical potential inside the plateau range.

Consider now the gran canonical hamiltonian $\hat K \equiv \hat H - \mu\hat N$ and use Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-id-def}. The diagonalising matrix remains the same, being
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-WkW+-hm}
	-\mu\hat N = \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \begin{bmatrix}
		-\mu & \\ & -\mu
	\end{bmatrix}
	\quad\implies\quad
	W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \left[ 
	h_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}-\mu \Id_{2\times2}
	\right] W_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger = d_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}-\mu \Id_{2\times2}
\end{equation}
thus for non-null chemical potential (i.e. finite doping) the eigenvalues are just shifted, $\pm E_\mathbf{k}-\mu$. These two bands have the same number of single-particle states. In Fig.~\ref{fig:af-Ek-bands} we sketch the bands $\pm E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$. Intense colour indicates an almost filled single-particle state, lighter gray colour an almost empty single-particle state. The gap is at zero energy, but at any finite doping $\tilde{\mu}$ crosses the upper band. 

\paragraph{Instability of the doped antiferromagnet.}

As we said at the beginning of this chapter, there exist several ways to realise an Hubbard antiferromagnet. For the conventional HM, it is known \cite{singh1990collective,singh1991doped} that the commensurate AF phase is unstable at any finite doping. This is discussed briefly in Sec.~\ref{appsubsec:af-instability}. The key takeaway is that insulating antiferromagnets are stable with respect to spin fluctuations, while metallic antiferromagnets are not. The commensurate AF phase is insulating only at half-filling, because the chemical potential lies within the gap.

The mere existence of a self-consistent solution does not guarantee stability: indeed, when in Sec.~\ref{subsec:hf-variational-solution} we derived the variational solution, we only required it to be a stationary point of the HF energy. In other words, there is no need for the solution to be a minimum. The commensurate AF phase is, in fact, a saddle point of HF energy \cite{singh1990collective} and any finite doping disrupts commensurate ordering towards uncommensurate phases.

These observations end the description of commensurate antiferromagnetism in the conventional HM. Some more details and observations are provided in App.~\ref{app:hm-af-ground-state}.

\section{Extension to the EHM}\label{subsec:af-ehm}

We now expand to the EHM, including the non-local interaction $\hat H_V$. Our strategy is to generalise the discussion of the section above. A formally identical solution with renormalised quantities,
\[
\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \to \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\qquad
E_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \to \tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\qquad
\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \to \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\qquad
\mu \to \tilde{\mu}
\]
is coherent by construction with the symmetry structure of the AF phase. Explicitly, the renormalised bands are $\pm \tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$, with
\[
\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \sqrt{\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^2 + |\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}|^2}
\]
Moreover, we allow $\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ to be a general complex function of $\mathbf{k}$ and $\sigma$,
\[
\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \Re \lbrace \Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rbrace + i \Im \lbrace \Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rbrace
\]

We define the renormalised angle $\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ through the relations:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-(~theta)}
	\sin 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \frac{|\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}|}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
	\qquad
	\cos 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \frac{\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
\end{equation}
and similarly the angle $\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-(~zeta)}
	\sin 2\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \frac{\Re\{\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\}}{|\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}|}
	\qquad
	\cos 2\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \frac{\Im\{\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\}}{|\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}|}
\end{equation}
A sketch of these angles is given in Fig.~\ref{fig:pseudo-magnetic-field-ehm}. For a complex gap function, the new diagonalisation matrix is given by:
\begin{align}
	\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} &\equiv e^{-i \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^y} e^{-i \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^z} \nonumber\\
	&= \left(\cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} -i \sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^y \right) \left(\cos \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} -i \sin \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \tau^z \right) \nonumber\\
	&= \begin{bmatrix}
		\cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & - \sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \\
		\sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} & \cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
		e^{-i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} & \\ 
		& e^{i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
	\end{bmatrix} \nonumber \\
	&= \begin{bmatrix}
		\cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} e^{-i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} & - \sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} e^{i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} \\
		\sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} e^{-i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} & \cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} e^{i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
	\end{bmatrix} \nonumber
\end{align}
The following relations hold (\textit{tilde} sign indicates the renormalised quantity):
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\langle \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^x \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &= -\sin 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \cos 2\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle \\
	\langle \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^y \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &= -\sin 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \sin 2\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle \\
	\langle \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &= \cos 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle
\end{aligned}
\]

Let us extend Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-<z>} and define the renormalised thermal factors,
\begin{align}
	\langle \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rangle &=
	f\left(
	\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\tilde{\mu}
	\right) - f\left(
	-\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\tilde{\mu}
	\right) \label{eq:af-<~z>}\\
	\tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(\pm)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] &\equiv 
	f\left(
	-\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\tilde{\mu}
	\right) \pm f\left(
	\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma};\beta,\tilde{\mu}
	\right) \label{eq:af-~Th}
\end{align}
which gives $\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^z} = -\tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu]$. Pseudospin expectation values are:
\begin{align}
	\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^x} &= \frac{\Re \lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rbrace}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-ehm-sx} \\
	\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y} &= \frac{\Im \lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \rbrace}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-ehm-sy}\\
	\ev{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^z} &=  -\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}  \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] \label{eq:af-ehm-sz}
\end{align}
These equations are an extension of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-sx}, \eqref{eq:af-ehm-sy}, \eqref{eq:af-ehm-sz} for the EHM. From now on, in the following sections, we deal with the problem of determining how the various quantities are renormalised.

	\label{fig:pseudo-magnetic-field-ehm}
\end{figure}

By renormalising all quantities, the self-consistency equation for magnetisation must stay formally the same of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-m-self-consistency}
	m = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{(-)}[\beta,\mu] 
\end{equation}
as well as the formula for density:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-n-MBZ-Th}
	n = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \mathrm{Th}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{(+)}[\beta,\mu]
\end{equation}
With this strategy, we get back to the application of HF methods to the full EHM, including the non-local attraction.

\section{Non-local Hartree effects: chemical potential renormalisation}\label{subsec:af-hartree-mu}

In last sections we obtained the pseudospin representation of the HM hamiltonian for the AF phase. The main results were Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-HU-gap-psi}, \eqref{eq:af-hm-spin-h}. Now we must approximate $\hat H_V$, reducing to a quadratic operator. We start from the Hartree channel.

The non-local attraction decomposes, in the non-local Hartree channel, in same-spin and opposite-spin sectors. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:mft-HV} we get:
\begin{multline*}
	\hat H_V \simeq
	\overbrace{
		-V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\langle
		\hat n_{i\sigma}
		\rangle \hat n_{j\sigma} + \hat n_{i\sigma} \langle
		\hat n_{j\sigma}
		\rangle - \langle
		\hat n_{i\sigma}
		\rangle \langle
		\hat n_{j\sigma}
		\rangle
		\right]
	}^{\mathrm{s.s.}} \\
	\underbrace{
		-V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\langle
		\hat n_{i\sigma}
		\rangle \hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{i\sigma} \langle
		\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
		\rangle
		- \langle
		\hat n_{i\sigma}
		\rangle \langle
		\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
		\rangle
		\right]
	}_{\mathrm{o.s.}} \, +\,
	(\text{all the rest})
\end{multline*}
Let $i \to \mathbf{r} = (x,y)$ and $j \to \mathbf{r}' = (x',y')$. We use Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz}, describing oscillatory density for each spin sector. This Ansatz is composed of two parts: the offset $n$, and the oscillatory part $m$. The algebraic derivation relative to $n$ part is, of course, the same as for the normal phase. We now treat separately the ``operatorial'' part of the braced terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-wick-decomposition},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-V-wick-decomposition-operatorial}
	-V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\rangle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma}
	\rangle
	\right] -V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\rangle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}}
	\rangle
	\right]
\end{equation}
as well as the its scalar counterpart
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-V-wick-decomposition-double-counting}
	V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma
	\langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \rangle \langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma} \rangle + V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma
	\langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \rangle \langle \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}} \rangle
\end{equation}
accounting for energy shift originated as ``leftovers'' of Wick's contractions.

\subsection{Non-local Hartree channel in the AF phase: analytic derivation}\label{subsec:af-nl-hartree-analytic}

Let us start from the operator of Expr.~\eqref{eq:af-V-wick-decomposition-operatorial}. By inserting the Ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ansatz}, we get
\begin{multline*}
	\overbrace{
		-nV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\right] + mV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}} \left[
		(-1)^{x+y} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma} + (-1)^{x'+y'} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\right]
	}^{\mathrm{s.s.}} \\
	\underbrace{
		-nV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\right] + mV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		(-1)^{x+y+\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}} + (-1)^{x'+y'+\delta_{\overline{\sigma}=\uparrow}} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\right]
	}_{\mathrm{o.s.}}
\end{multline*}
For a square lattice, if $\mathbf{r} = (x,y)$ and $\mathbf{r}' = (x',y')$ are NNs, both the following identities are true
\[
\begin{aligned}
	(-1)^{x'+y'} &= (-1)^{x+y+1} \\
	(-1)^{\delta_{\overline{\sigma}=\uparrow}} &= (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}+1}
\end{aligned}
\]
We obtain
\begin{multline}
	\overbrace{
		-nV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma}
		\right]
	}^{\text{s.s. (density)}} - \overbrace{
		mV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{x+y+\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}} \left[
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma}
		\right]
	}^{\text{s.s. (magnetisation)}} \\
	\underbrace{
		-nV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}}
		\right]
	}_{\text{o.s. (density)}} + \underbrace{
		mV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{x+y+\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}} \left[
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}}
		\right]
	}_{\text{o.s. (magnetisation)}}
	\label{eq:af-ehm-hartree-int-1}
\end{multline}
In the above expressions we separated in ``density'' contributions and ``magnetisation'' ones. Let us deal with them separately.

\paragraph{Density terms.}

Consider s.s. and o.s. (density) terms of Expr.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-hartree-int-1},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hartree-renormalisation-density}
	-nV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma}
	\right] -nV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}}
	\right]
\end{equation}
The discussion is identical to Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-hartree-analytic} for the normal phase, with renormalisation of $\mu$ being the same of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-mu-shift}.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-nl-mu}
	\tilde{\mu} \equiv \mu + n(2zV-U)
\end{equation}
We already included the shift to $\mu$ due to the local Hartree channel, anticipated in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-l-mu}.

\paragraph{Magnetization terms.}

The magnetisation $\mathrm{s.s.}$ and $\mathrm{o.s.}$ terms of Expr.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-hartree-int-1} perfectly cancel out:
\[
mV \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma (-1)^{x+y+\delta_{\sigma=\uparrow}} \left[
\left(
\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} - \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma}
\right) - \left(
\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}}
\right)
\right] = 0
\]
The Hartree channel only affects $\mu$.

\subsection{Non-local Hartree contraction energy}\label{subsec:af-nl-hartree-energy}

We now collect double counting terms originated by Wick's contraction in Hartree sector. Consider the scalar Expr.~\eqref{eq:af-V-wick-decomposition-double-counting}: it's the sum of two parts, the first coming from s.s. sector, the second from o.s. sector. In computing the contraction energy from Hartree channel, being the operatorial result identical to the one obtained for the normal phase, we expect the same to happen for double counting terms.

\paragraph{s.s. sector double counting energy.}

For s.s. terms we get
\begin{multline*}
	V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\rangle \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma}
	\rangle = \overbrace{
		V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \left[
		(
		n + (-1)^{x+y} m
		) (
		n + (-1)^{x'+y'} m
		)
		\right]
	}^{\sigma=\uparrow} \\
	+ \underbrace{
		V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \left[
		(
		n - (-1)^{x+y} m
		) (
		n - (-1)^{x'+y'} m
		)
		\right]
	}_{\sigma=\downarrow}
\end{multline*}
The mixed terms (those of order $\mathcal{O}(n)\times\mathcal{O}(m)$) cancel out, and since sign factors necessarily alternate on neighbouring sites, the remainder is just
\[
V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \langle
\hat n_{i\sigma}
\rangle \langle
\hat n_{j\sigma}
\rangle = V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} 2(
n^2-m^2
)
\]

\paragraph{o.s. sector double counting energy.}

Proceeding identically on the o.s. sector, we get
\begin{multline*}
	V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \sum_\sigma \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\rangle \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\overline{\sigma}}
	\rangle = \overbrace{
		V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \left[
		(
		n + (-1)^{x+y} m
		) (
		n - (-1)^{x'+y'} m
		)
		\right]
	}^{\sigma=\uparrow} \\
	+ \underbrace{
		V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} \left[
		(
		n - (-1)^{x+y} m
		) (
		n + (-1)^{x'+y'} m
		)
		\right]
	}_{\sigma=\downarrow}
\end{multline*}
which gives
\[
V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma \langle
\hat n_{i\sigma}
\rangle \langle
\hat n_{j\overline{\sigma}}
\rangle = V \sum_{\ev{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}} 2(
n^2+m^2
)
\]
Hence, the total ``contraction energy'', given by sum of both contributions, is identical to the normal one of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-hartree-V-contraction-energy} indeed:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hartree-V-contraction-energy}
	E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = - 2V \sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_\sigma n^2 = 4n^2 V \times \frac{z}{2} L_x L_y
\end{equation}
the minus sign coming from the fact that we are subtracting this energy shift.

We explored the Hartree channel. Let us now move to the effects originated by Fock contractions. We can use the very beginning of Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-fock-bands-renormalisation} derivation, up to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-int1},

\subsection{Fock channel in the AF phase: analytic derivation}\label{subsec:af-fock-analytic}

	(\cdots) \stackrel{\mathcal{F}}{=} \frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma \left[

	2k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(

\paragraph{Diagonal terms.}

The diagonal terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int2} are products of $\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ operators. Consider Fig.~\ref{subfig:Kk-qq'-generic}: $\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}$, with $\mathbf{q}\equiv\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}$, is coupled to $\ev{\hat n_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}}$ with $\mathbf{q}'\equiv\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}$. Apply to the diagonal part of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int2} this change of variables represented in Fig.~\ref{fig:Kk-qq'}
\begin{align}
	\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [
	\cos \left(
	2k_x
	\right)	+ &\cos \left(
	2k_y
	\right)	
	] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\sigma} \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\cos \left( \delta q_x \right) + \cos \left( \delta q_y \right)
	\right] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} 
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}
	\nonumber \\
	&= \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_\mathbf{q} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
	\right] \times V
	\sum_{\mathbf{q}'}
	\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} \label{eq:af-V-fock-int3-diag}
\end{align}
where in the last passage we substituted the decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:coscos-symmetries-decomposition}. Define as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-uqsigma-def} the EV
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-uqsigma-def}
	u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} \equiv \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
\end{equation}
The expression in square brackets of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int3-diag} is just its $\gamma$ component, and is expanded in
\begin{align}
	u^{(\gamma)}_\sigma &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle \nonumber\\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
	+
	\varphi_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
	\rangle
	\right] \nonumber\\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)} \left[
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
	-
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
	\rangle
	\right] \nonumber\\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \Psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^z \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle \nonumber\\
	&= -\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \label{eq:af-ugamma-chain}
\end{align}
where, in the last passage, Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-sz} has been used. We are not breaking inversion symmetry (and as a consequence, neither $\mathrm{C}_2$): this implies that, whatever the shape of the renormalised bands, it must be
\[
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} = \tilde{\epsilon}_{-\mathbf{q}\sigma}
\quad\implies\quad
u^{(p_\ell)}_\sigma = 0
\qq{with}
\ell\in\lbrace x,y \rbrace
\]
Renormalised bare bands need to be symmetric functions of the moment. Hence for $\gamma\in\lbrace p_x, p_y \rbrace$ the above sum vanishes. The remaining $s^*$ and $d$ symmetries give legitimate HFPs with relative self-consistency equations, reported in Tab.~\ref{tab:af-ehm-ugamma-self-consistencies} using spin degeneracy.

Recognising harmonics, Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int3-diag} becomes
\begin{multline}
	\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\cos \left(
	2k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	2k_y
	\right)
	\right] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\sigma} \nonumber \\
	= V \sum_{\mathbf{q},\sigma} \left[
	u^{(s^*)}_\sigma (\cos q_x + \cos q_y) + u^{(d)}_\sigma (\cos q_x - \cos q_y)
	\right] \hat c_{\mathbf{q} \sigma}^\dagger
	\hat c_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-bands-renormalisation}
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + u^{(s^*)}_\sigma V (\cos k_x + \cos k_y) + u^{(d)}_\sigma V (\cos k_x - \cos k_y)
\end{equation}
as was for the normal phase in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-bands-renormalisation}. These RMPs are, as before, controlled linearly by $V$ and in principle can lead to relevant changes in the kinetic sector of the hamiltonian.

\paragraph{Off-diagonal terms.}

Consider off-diagonal terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int2}. These contribute instead to full gap, being out of diagonal in the $2\times2$ hamiltonian matrix of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-HM-hk}. In pseudo-spin picture, they account for a transverse field. Define $\mathbf{q}$, $\mathbf{q}'$ as in Fig.~\ref{subfig:Kk-qq'-newvariables}, 
\[
\mathbf{q} \equiv \mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}
\qquad
\mathbf{q}' \equiv \mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}
\]
and rewrite as for Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int3-diag}
\begin{multline*}
	-\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [
	\cos \left(
	2k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	2k_y
	\right)	
	] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi} \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}-\bm{\pi} \sigma} \\
	= -\sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger
	\rangle
	\right] \times V
	\sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}}
	\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger
\end{multline*}
Now shift the first sum by an AF vector $\bm{\pi}$ defining $\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\equiv\mathbf{q}$, and use Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-vphi-psi-property} as well as reciprocal space periodicity by a BZ,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger
	\rangle &= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger
	\rangle \\
	&= - \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\rangle
\end{aligned}
\]
which finally gives
\begin{multline}
	-\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [

	= \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
	\right] \times V
	\sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}}
	\varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^\dagger \label{eq:af-V-fock-int3-offdiag}
\end{multline}

Proceed as done previously: define the expectation value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-vqsigma-def}
	v_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} \equiv i\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
\end{equation}
(which is just the AF order parameter with a complex factor) and its $\gamma$-wave component, with $\gamma\in\lbrace s^*,d,p_x,p_y\rbrace$:
\begin{align}
	v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma &= \frac{i}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{i}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
	+
	\varphi_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
	\rangle
	\right] \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{i}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)} \left[
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle
	-
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger
	\rangle
	\right] \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\langle
	\hat \Psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger \tau^y \hat \Psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)}
	\frac{\Im\lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \label{eq:af-vgamma-chain}
\end{align}
where we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-vphi-psi-property}, Tab.~\ref{tab:af-pseudo-spin-map} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-sy}. There is no particular reason to exclude one component or another. In Tab.~\ref{tab:af-ehm-vgamma-self-consistencies} the four derived self-consistency equations are reported, ignoring spin index.

\[
v^{(s^*)}_\sigma, v^{(d)}_\sigma \in \mathbb{R}
\qquad
v^{(p_x)}_\sigma, v^{(p_y)}_\sigma \in i\mathbb{R}
\]
Getting back to Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int3-offdiag} and inserting these components, we get
\begin{multline}
	-\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [
	\cos \left(
	2k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	2k_y
	\right)	
	] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi} \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}-\bm{\pi} \sigma} \\
	= -iV \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma
	\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}}
	\varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)*}
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger
\end{multline}
We can restrict to MBZ as
\begin{multline*}
	-\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [
	\cos \left(
	2k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	2k_y
	\right)	
	] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi} \sigma}
	\rangle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}-\bm{\pi} \sigma} \\
	= -iV\sum_{\gamma,\sigma}
	\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}
	\left[
	v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)*}
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger + v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)*}

	\right]

	-\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [
	\cos \left(
	2k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	2k_y
	\right)	
	] \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi} \sigma}
	\rangle

	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger
	\right]
	\label{eq:af-V-fock-int4-offdiag}
\end{multline}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-ivvphi-imag}
	iv^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)*} \in i\mathbb{R}
\end{equation}
and to conjugate a purely imaginary number it suffices to put a minus sign in front of it,
\[
-iv^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)*} = \left(
iv^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)*}
\right)^*
\]
Therefore, we conclude
\begin{multline}
	-\frac{2V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}} \sum_\sigma [
	\cos \left(
	2k_x

	2k_y
	\right)

	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi} \sigma}^\dagger

	\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}
	\left[
	iv^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{q}^{(\gamma)*}
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} + \hc
	\right]
	\label{eq:af-V-fock-int5-offdiag}
\end{multline}
which is no surprise, since hamiltonian are hermitian operators.

\subsection{Fock contraction energy}\label{subsec:af-fock-energy}

Let us now get back to $E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})}$, the double counting energy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int1}. As said before, its definition is the same as for the normal phase, Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-V-fock-double-counting}, with averaging operation being performed on the AF ground state:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-V-fock-double-counting-int1}
	E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})} \equiv \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\cos \left(
	\delta k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	\delta k_y
	\right)	
	\right]	
	\langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger 
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma}
	\rangle \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger  \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} 
	\rangle
\end{equation}
Proceeding exactly as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-int2} and making changing variables as in Fig.~\ref{fig:Kk-qq'}, we get
\[
E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \sum_\sigma \left[
\cos \left(
\delta q_x
\right)	+ \cos \left(
\delta q_y
\right)	
\right]
\Big[
\underbrace{
	\langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle \langle
	\hat n_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}
	\rangle
}_\text{Diagonal terms}
-
\underbrace{
	\langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\rangle \langle
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}
	\rangle
}_\text{Off-diagonal terms}
\Big]
\]
Apply the decomposition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:coscos-symmetries-decomposition} and use Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-uqsigma-def}, \eqref{eq:af-vqsigma-def},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-V-fock-double-counting-int2}
	E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \frac{V}{2 L_x L_y} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(\gamma)} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*} \left[
	u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} u_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} + v_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} v_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}
	\right]
\end{equation}
Now: it holds
\[
u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} = \langle
\hat n_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
\rangle
\quad\implies\quad
u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} = u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^*
\]
and then
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{1}{(L_x L_y)^2} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(\gamma)} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
	u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} u_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} &=
	\left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(\gamma)} u_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\right] \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*} u_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}^*
	\right] \\
	&= \big|u^{(\gamma)}_\sigma\big|^2
\end{aligned}
\]
At the same time, being $\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}\sigma} = \hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^\dagger$,
\[
v_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} = i\langle
\hat \psi_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
\rangle
\quad\implies\quad
v_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}\sigma} = - v_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}^*
\]
Since the form factors satisfy $\varphi_{\mathbf{q}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)} = -\varphi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(\gamma)}$, we get:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{1}{(L_x L_y)^2} \sum_{\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{q}'} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(\gamma)} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*}
	v_{\mathbf{q}\sigma} v_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma} &=
	\left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}}^{(\gamma)} v_{\mathbf{q}\sigma}
	\right] \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{q}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{q}'}^{(\gamma)*} v_{\mathbf{q}'\sigma}
	\right] \\
	&= v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}}^{(\gamma)*} v_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
	\right] \\
	&= v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \left[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} \varphi_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\gamma)*} v_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^*
	\right] \\
	&= \big| v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \big|^2
\end{aligned}
\]
where in the second passage we employed the change of variables $\mathbf{q}'=\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}$ and used BZ periodicity to map the original sum onto a new sum for $\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}$. Inserting above results in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-V-fock-double-counting-int2}, the latter becomes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-V-fock-double-counting-int3}
	E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = L_x L_y \times \frac{V}{2} \sum_{\gamma,\sigma} \left[
	\big|u^{(\gamma)}_\sigma\big|^2 + \big|v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma\big|^2
	\right]
\end{equation}
The new HFPs amplitudes contribute positively to contraction energy. The consequences of this fact will be discussed in Sec.~\ref{subsec:af-energy-constraints}.

\section{Full MFT hamiltonian and gap complexification}\label{sec:af-full-hamiltonian}

Finally, it is time to collect everything in a single hamiltonian operator. The approximated hamiltonian is:
\begin{multline}
	\hat H - \mu \hat N \simeq \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \left[
	\hat n_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \hat n_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}
	\right]
	- \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
	\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^* \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger
	\right] \\
	- \tilde{\mu} \hat N - \left[
	E_{\mathrm{H}/U}^{(\mathrm{AF})} + 
	E_{\mathrm{H}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})} + E_{\mathrm{F}/V}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
	\right] \label{eq:af-full-h}
\end{multline}
where renormalized bands are given by:
\[
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + V \sum_{\gamma\in\lbrace s^*,d \rbrace} u^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\gamma)*}
\]
and full gap function by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-full-gap}
	\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - iV \sum_\gamma v^{(\gamma)}_\sigma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)*}
\end{equation}

The gap correction is purely imaginary, compared to HM gap $\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = (-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}} mU$. Since there is no other contribution to the $x$ pseudo-spin projections other than the pure HM ones, spin antisymmetry is preserved in this direction
\[
\left\langle \hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^x \right\rangle = -\left\langle \hat s_{\mathbf{k}\overline{\sigma}}^x \right\rangle
\]
This property confirms that Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-m-self-consistency} actually holds. Indeed, we anticipated that the self-consistency equation of the magnetisation should have been identical for the HM and the EHM. The fact that the $x$ component EV of the pseudospin maintains this antisymmetry relation is a confirmation of that, since the proof that lead us to Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-hm-m-self-consistency} for the HM was based on this property.

One of the net effects of $\hat H_V$ on the AF phase, with respect to the conventional HM, is ``gap complexification''. This is expressed by:
\[
\big|
\Im\lbrace\Delta_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace
\big| \ge 0
\quad\implies\quad
\big|
\ev{s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y}
\big| \ge 0
\]
while for HM the $y$ pseudo-spin component was identically null.

\subsection{The relative phase in the zero temperature half-filled ground state}\label{subsec:af-gs}

In Sec.~\ref{appsubsec:af-T=0-gs}, we derive analytically the AF ground state on conventional HM at zero temperature, as a simple Bloch's transform of a polarized (tilted) pseudo-spins collective pure state. Eq.~\eqref{appeq:af-hm-gs-3} reports the final form of said ground-state. Said quantum state is in general pretty similar to conventional BCS state.

At half-filling and zero temperature we can extend that state to the new complexified gap. The HF approximation of the true ground-state is, in the AF sector,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-hm-gs}
	\ket{\Psi} = \bigotimes_\sigma \bigotimes_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\sin\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger + e^{2i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} \cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}+\bm{\pi}\sigma}^\dagger
	\right] \ket{\Omega_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
\end{equation}
The proof is identical to the one provided in Sec.~\ref{appsubsec:af-T=0-gs} for the HM, apart from renormalisations. Let $\ket{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \equiv \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \ket{\Omega_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}$. Then we can write
\[
\ket{\Psi} = \bigotimes_\sigma \bigotimes_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
\sin\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + e^{2i\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} \cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\right] \ket{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\]
having we used $\ket{\Omega_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} = \lbrace \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}, \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \rbrace \ket{\Omega_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}$.

The angles appearing in above equations are the same of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-(~theta)}, \eqref{eq:af-ehm-(~zeta)}. The relative phase $2 \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}$ is the gap function angle in complex plane, since
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\mel{\Psi}{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^x}{\Psi} &= \mel{\Psi}{\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} + \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger}{\Psi} \\
	&= \sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \left(
	e^{-2i \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} + e^{2i \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
	\right) \\
	&= \sin 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \cos 2\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \\[1em]
	\mel{\Psi}{\hat s_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^y}{\Psi} &= i \mel{\Psi}{\hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} - \hat \psi_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger}{\Psi} \\
	&= i \sin \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \cos \tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \left(
	e^{-2i \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}} - e^{2i \tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}}
	\right) \\
	&= \sin 2\tilde{\theta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \sin 2\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\end{aligned}
\]
These equations are coherent with Eqns.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-sx}, \eqref{eq:af-ehm-sy}, thus the involved angles (apart from irrelevant $2\pi$ shifts) coincide.

\subsection{AF phase flavours and spin degeneracy}\label{subsec:eaf-hm-flavours}

In this analysis, antiferromagnetism is realised without breaking inversion symmetry. This translates in the requirement that:
\[
\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \stackrel{!}{=} \tilde{E}_{-\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\]
We know that:
\[
\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \sqrt{
	\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^2 + \Re^2\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace+ \Im^2\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace
}
\]
Renormalized bare bands as well as the real part of the gap satisfy said inversion symmetry requirement,
\[
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} = \tilde{\epsilon}_{-\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\qquad
\Re\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace = \Re\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{-\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace
\]
hence inversion symmetry is respected only if
\[
\Im\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace = \pm\Im\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{-\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace
\]
which is: either the imaginary part of the gap is inversion symmetric, or is antisymmetric. Based on this consideration, we are able two discard two of the four self-consistency equations for the $v$-related HFPs listed in Tab.~\ref{tab:af-ehm-vgamma-self-consistencies}: $s^*$ and $d$-wave symmetries HFPs are nonzero if $\Im\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace$ is momentum-even; $p_x$ and $p_y$-wave symmetries HFPs are nonzero if $\Im\lbrace \tilde{\Delta}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\rbrace$ is momentum-odd. The two options cannot coexist, and separate simulations can be implemented in the two symmetry sectors.

We name the two possible symmetries ``sAF'' (symmetric antiferromagnet) and ``aAF'' (antisymmetric antiferromagnet). The set of HFPs then restricts to these AF structures, and is reported for both cases in Tab.~\ref{tab:af-saf-aaf-hfps}. The presence itself of two ``AF flavours'' is not-so-physical, given that up to now we have no clear physical interpretation for $\Im\lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace$. As we will discuss in Sec.~\ref{sec:af-constraints}, it is possible to prove that almost all these new HFPs actually vanish -- a fact that is not directly inferrable right now, but can be seen only delving into the discussion of the non-linear map that defines the algorithm. For now, let us proceed integrally with all the HFPs we found and derive the results.

\section{AF free energy density}\label{sec:af-free-energy}

In order to derive the free energy density expression for AF phase, we proceed precisely as in Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-free-energy}. The only notable difference is in the expression of entropy density: Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-entropy-density} is written for two opposite bands on MBZ
\[
s = s^{(+)} + s^{(-)}
\]
with
\begin{multline}
	s^{(\pm)} \equiv -\frac{k_B}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \Big[
	\left(
	1- f( \pm\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\right) \ln \left(
	1- f( \pm\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\right) \\
	+ f(
	\pm\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\ln f( \pm\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\Big] \label{eq:af-density-entropy}
\end{multline}
Consider also that we are computing free energy on the gran-canonical hamiltonian
\[
\hat H - \mu \hat N \simeq \sum_\sigma \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
(\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} - \tilde{\mu}) \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)\dagger} \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(+)} - (\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} + \tilde{\mu}) \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)\dagger} \hat \gamma_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^{(-)}
\right] + E_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{AF})}
\]
being $E_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{AF})}$ the total AF contraction energy. Note that on the left-hand side we have $\mu$ and on the right-hand side its RMP counterpart $\tilde{\mu}$.

With identical meaning of the notation with respect to Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-free-energy}, the free energy contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Quasiparticles energy, taking care of spin degeneracy:
	\[
	e_\mathrm{g}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} (\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} - \tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) - \frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} (\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} + \tilde{\mu}) f( -\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\]
	\item Contraction energy:
	\[
	e_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{AF})} = U(m^2 - n^2) + V \left[
	2zn^2 - \sum_{\gamma} \left(
	\big|u^{(\gamma)}\big|^2 + \big|v^{(\gamma)}\big|^2
	\right)
	\right]
	\]
	\item Entropic energy
	\begin{multline*}
		e_s^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \ln \left(
		1- f( \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) - \frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}-\tilde{\mu}) f( \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \\
		+ \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \ln \left(
		1- f( -\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) + \frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} (\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}+\tilde{\mu}) f( -\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu})
	\end{multline*}
	\item Chemical potential correction
	\[
	e_n^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \mu n
	\]
\end{enumerate}
Compose everything
\[
f^{(\mathrm{AF})} \equiv e_\mathrm{g}^{(\mathrm{AF})} + e_\mathrm{c}^{(\mathrm{AF})} + e_s^{(\mathrm{AF})} + e_n^{(\mathrm{AF})}
\]
and we get
\begin{multline*}
	f^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\ln \left(
	1- f( \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right) + \ln \left(
	1- f( -\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) \right)
	\right] \\
	+ U m^2 - V \sum_{\gamma} \left[
	\big|u^{(\gamma)}\big|^2 + \big|v^{(\gamma)}\big|^2
	\right] + n \left[
	\mu + n(2zV-U)
	\right]
\end{multline*}
As was for the normal phase, contraction energy and chemical potential energy corrections build up the RMP $\tilde{\mu}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-nl-mu},
\begin{multline}
	f^{(\mathrm{AF})} = \frac{2}{\beta L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \left[
	\ln \left(
	1- f( \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) 
	\right) + \ln \left(
	1- f( -\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}; \beta,\tilde{\mu}) 
	\right)
	\right] \\
	+ U m^2 - V \sum_{\gamma} \left[
	\big|u^{(\gamma)}\big|^2 + \big|v^{(\gamma)}\big|^2
	\right] + \tilde{\mu} n 
	\label{eq:af-fMFT}
\end{multline}
In this expression, we see that $u^{(\gamma)}$s, $v^{(\gamma)}$s contribution is negative, while magnetisation contributes positively. At a first glance, this suggests that a system with no magnetisation and non-zero $u^{(\gamma)}$s, $v^{(\gamma)}$s would be energetically favoured. In that situation we would have a purely imaginary gap for a non-magnetised phase, which would be not so intuitive to explain physically. In next section we explain how the above expression for the free energy density can be used to give a constraint that proves that said situation is impossible. HFPs are interrelated and the physical solution cannot admit arbitrary values.

\subsection{Energetic constraints on the HFPs}\label{subsec:af-energy-constraints}

Let us derive a constraint from the free energy density expression. Collecting the terms directly related to the gap function, the following identity holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-ehm-gap-energy}
	Um^2 - V \sum_{\gamma} \big| v^{(\gamma)} \big|^2 = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\big|
		\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}
		\big|^2}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}]
\end{equation}
The proof is simple and immediate: expanding right-hand side,
\begin{multline*}
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\big|
		\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}
		\big|^2}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] = \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Re^2 \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \\
	+ \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Im^2 \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}]
\end{multline*}
Now:
\[
\Re \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace = Um
\]
which implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Re^2 \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] &= Um \times
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Re \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \\
	&= Um^2
\end{aligned}
\]
having we recognised the expression for the magnetisation from Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-m-self-consistency}. Equivalently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\Im \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace &= - V \sum_\gamma v^{(\gamma)} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)*} \\
	&= - V \sum_\gamma v^{(\gamma)*} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)}
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-ivvphi-imag} to exchange the conjugation operation in the last passage. This implies:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \frac{\Im^2 \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] &= - V \sum_\gamma v^{(\gamma)*} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{MBZ}} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)} \frac{\Im \lbrace\tilde{\Delta}_\mathbf{k}\rbrace}{\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}} \tilde{\mathrm{Th}}^{(-)}[\beta,\tilde{\mu}] \\
	&= - V \sum_\gamma v^{(\gamma)*} \times v^{(\gamma)} \\
	&= - V \sum_\gamma \big| v^{(\gamma)*} \big|^2
\end{aligned}
\]
where in second passage we recognised $v^{(\gamma)}$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-vgamma-chain}. Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-gap-energy} is verified. 

A consequence of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-gap-energy} is that, being the right-hand side positive, for $V>0$ the total amplitude of the ``complexifiers'' vector is maximised,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-vgamma-circle}
	\sum_{\gamma} \big| v^{(\gamma)} \big|^2 \le M
	\qq{being}
	M^{(\mathrm{AF})} \equiv m^2 \frac U V
\end{equation}
The above constraint predicts that:
\begin{itemize}
	\item In the strong coupling limit $U \ll V$, gap complexification is suppressed;
	\item If magnetisation is null, so is the imaginary part of the gap
	\[
	\lim_{m\to0} v^{(\gamma)} = 0
	\]
	which is coherent with the entire derivation and takes us back to the normal phase.
\end{itemize}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-ehm-gap-energy} is also solved by a null magnetisation and thus null complexifiers. This can be interpreted by the fact that when the constraint is impossible to solve self-consistently, the gap must close.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-vgamma-circle} provides a neat graphic interpretation for the constraint on HFPs in both sAF and aAF phases. At any competition ratio $U/V$, the product $m \sqrt{U/V}$ is the radius of a sphere. Inside this sphere must lie the vector defined by remaining $v^{(\gamma)}$ HFPs. For our AF flavours, the sphere is actually a circle, being in both cases the total number of HFPs equal to $3$, counting also $m$. Note that the solution:
\[
\forall \gamma
\quad\colon\quad
v^{(\gamma)} = 0
\]
solves the constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-vgamma-circle}. These considerations do not imply gap complexification is an actual, observed effect.

\subsection{Free energy at half-filling}

Let us discuss the half-filling condition. We start with the identity:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\ln(1 - \frac{1}{e^x + 1}) + \ln(1 - \frac{1}{e^{-x} + 1}) &= \ln\left[ \frac{e^x}{(e^x+1)^2} \right] \\
	&= x - 2 \ln(1+e^x)
\end{aligned}
\]
Let now $x=\beta \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}$. At low temperature for a band with no nodes, we can approximate $x\to +\infty$ on the entire BZ. This gives
\[
x - 2 \ln(1+e^x) \simeq -x
\]
Hence we get the half-filling free energy density substituting ,
\[
f^{(\mathrm{AF})} \simeq -\frac{2}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}} \tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} + U m^2 - V \sum_{\gamma} \left[
\big|u^{(\gamma)}\big|^2 + \big|v^{(\gamma)}\big|^2
\right]
\]
The first term in this sum gives the energy of the fully occupied lower band.

This approximation makes sense when $\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} \neq 0$ everywhere. This is always realised in AF phase: from the constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-vgamma-circle} we know magnetisation is necessary for all gap HFPs; for a magnetised system, whatever the symmetries,
\[
\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k} = \sqrt{\tilde{\epsilon}_\mathbf{k}^2 + (Um)^2 + \big| \Im\lbrace \Delta_\mathbf{k} \rbrace \big|^2} \ge Um
\]
At half filling and low temperature, the system is well approximated by a completely filled electronic band $-\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}$. This gives the aforementioned approximation of the free energy $f^{(\mathrm{AF})}$.

\section{Discussion of numeric results}\label{sec:af-numeric}

In this section we present numeric results. For the AF phase, the HFPs vector, as we thoroughly showed, has two components
\[
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
	m \\ u^{(s^*)}
\end{bmatrix}
\qquad
\mathbf{v} = \mathbb{R}^2
\]
The resulting model RMPs are, as before:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\tilde{t}_{ij} &\equiv \left(
	t - \frac{u^{(s^*)}V}{2}
	\right) \varphi_{ij}^{(s^*)} \\
	\tilde{\mu} &\equiv \mu + n (2zV-U)
\end{aligned}
\]
In the end, the computational scenario is not so different from the normal phase. The only real difference is our choice of parametric boundaries for simulations:
\[
U = 0,~0.05,~0.1,~\dots,~8.0
\qquad
V = 0,~0.05,~0.1,~\dots,~8.0
\qquad
\delta = 0.0
\]
We set $\delta=0.0$. As we anticipated in Sec.~\ref{subsec:af-features} (and discuss in Sec.~\ref{appsubsec:af-instability}), any finite doping disrupts commensurate antiferromagnetism, a property that makes any simulation at $\delta >0$ of low physical significance. Following this consideration, we omit data at finite $\delta \neq 0$.

In Fig.~\ref{fig:af-f-data} the solution free energy is plotted. In the right panel, Fig.~\ref{subfig:af-f-data-A256}, we see that antiferromagnetism becomes more stable at low $V$, high $U$. The opposite limit of weak local repulsion, strong non-local attraction (top-left corner of Fig.~\ref{subfig:af-f-data-C128}) instead increases $f$, reaching zero energy at $U=0$, $V>4.93t$. The latter isz the critical value discussed in Sec.~\ref{subsec:normal-mott-numeric} and computed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal-Vc-half-filling}.

It is then evident how, even if the macroscopic magnetic properties are the same along one isomagnetic line of those plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:af-m-data-C128}, right panel, and thus the realised state exhibits an identical value of $m$, much different is the energetic content of the state itself. While in the $U \gg 1$, fixed $V$ limit free energy gain comes from gap amplification -- which moves down the Bogoliubov bands $\tilde{E}_\mathbf{k}$ --, in the $V \gg 1$, fixed $U$ limit energies of the lower Bogoliubov band (full at low temperature) are shifted upwards via the Mott localisation effect.

The AF phase is always energetically favoured at half-filling, with respect to the normal phase. Let us define the energy difference,
\[
\Delta f \equiv f^{(\mathrm{N})} - f^{(\mathrm{AF})}
\]
shown for a slightly more coarse-grained dataset in Fig.~\ref{fig:af-df-data-A128}, we see immediately how $\Delta f \ge 0$ in all $(U,V)$ plane. The normal phase data of Sec.~\ref{sec:normal-numeric} having been simulated at $U=0$ due to their intrinsic $U$ independence. The AF phase is simulated in the same range $0 \le V \le 8$, but for $0 \le U \le 10$. The comparable normal phase dataset was just a repetition of the $U=0$ data at all $U$s.
%

	\caption{Difference of free energies for the normal phase minus the AF phase.}

As we expect, in the $V \gg 1$ and low $U$ limit the energy difference is null: in this limit the two phases tend to coincide, since at $U=0.0$ no magnetisation is physically possible. Moreover, the $U \gg 1$ limit makes the AF phase energetically favoured. Overall, at half filling the interesting free fermions Mott-localisation effect is, at least when comparing these phases, existent but surpassed by the high energetic gain of establishing an AF structure at infinitesimal $U$. The situation could change at finite doping, a regime we do not investigate limitedly to AF Physics.

At low temperature and half filling, it is not worth plotting entropy density. Half-filled commensurate antiferromagnets are insulators, with two gapped Bogoliubov bands. At low temperature, electronic distribution fills completely the lower band and empties the upper one. Because of this, insulating gapped systems inherently are low entropic.

\subsection{Fixed-point interpretation of HF solution}

As a final remark discussing the mathematical structure of the algorithm, we anticipated in Sec.~\ref{subsec:reject-mix-iterate} how the reject-mix-iterate scheme upon which our HF algorithm is built can be seen as an effective search for the singular fixed point of a nonlinear map. A physical solution is represented as a HFP vector $\mathbf{v}$ such that the algorithm is a local identity,
\[
\mathrm{A}(\mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{v}
\]
To predict for a given initializer $\mathbf{v}_0$ the path it will follow through HFPs space is, in general, highly non-trivial due to the intrinsic nonlinearity of the algorithmic map defined by self-consistency equations for the various HFPs. An example of such nonlinearity is given in the derivation of Secs.~\ref{subsec:af-HFPs=0-sAF}, \ref{subsec:af-HFPs=0-aAF}.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{/home/nepero27178/Thesis/EHM-HF/Project/src/labs/attractor/attractor.pdf}
	\caption{Quiver plot of algorithmic evolution in HFPs space at fixed model parametrization. The black dot $\mathbf{v}$ represents the nonlinear map fixed point, while the colored paths the sequences of HFPs coordinates simulated via iterative HF procedure, starting from different HFPs initializers (colored dots).}
	\label{fig:af-attractor}
\end{figure}

In Fig.~\ref{fig:af-attractor} we sketch this concept in a practical situation. In figure are plotted various algorithmic paths through AF HFPs space, with operational setup at
\[
U = 3
\qquad
V = 6
\qquad
L = 128
\qquad
\beta = 100
\qquad
\delta = 0
\]
At this point, the self-consistent HF ``fixed-point'' solution is given by
\[
m \approx 0.449
\qquad
u^{(s^*)} \approx 0.212
\]
indicated in panels as the black dot. The three panels show different map evolutions varying the mixing parameter within three choices, $g \in \lbrace 0.25, 0.1, 0.05 \rbrace$. We chose various initializers in the set:
\[
\mathbf{v}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} m_0 \\ u^{(s^*)}_0 \end{bmatrix} 
\quad\colon\quad
m_0 \in \lbrace 0.2,~0.3,~...,~0.6 \rbrace
\qq{and}
u^{(s^*)}_0 \in \lbrace 0.0,~0.1,~...,~0.4 \rbrace
\]
In figures, different colours indicate different evolutions, while the tip size indicates the magnitude of the algorithmic step in passing from one iteration to the following.

All paths are able to converge to $\mathbf{v}$, and the quantity of steps necessary to reach the fixed point, a sort of attractor, increases as we lower $g$. To select the correct $g$ turns out to be the real algorithm fine-tuning knob: as we saw for the normal phase, some model parametrisation could lead to unavoidable algorithmic instabilities. The problem is that we do not know beforehand where the instabilities will occur, and how. The correct $g$ is the compromise between keeping low the number of steps (larger mixing) and moving slow enough to not diverge when encountering unstable points (smaller mixing). By recursively fine-tuning $g$ repeating non-converged simulation with more care, we are essentially making the nonlinear map ``softer'' and curvier, as is in the right panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:af-attractor}. It's not always resource convenient to do so, so for most simulations we stick to a rougher map (like the left panel one) by setting $g=0.5$.
