\chapter{Phase transitions and SSB in EHM}\label{chap:ehm-symmetries}

The general idea of this project is to apply HF methods to the EHM, derive analytically the approximated quadratic hamiltonian of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hf-Hhf-k} and then apply the iHF procedure sketched in Sec.~\ref{sec:sketch-ihf-algorithm} to extract the self-consistent values of the various HFPs. One of the advantages of using iHF methods is that we can impose rather simply the symmetries of the HF approximated wavefunction.

This is most useful when dealing with competing phases. A phase transition is basically described by some spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) and the rising to a non-zero value of an order parameter. We will deal with three phases:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item the normal phase, where no symmetry is broken;
	\item the antiferromagnetic phase, where we allow for spin density to be non-uniform across sites;
	\item the superconducting phase, where we do not conserve particle number.
\end{enumerate}
Each phase is discussed in a separate chapter. Many other ``phases'' are possible, based on which symmetries are respected and which are not.

For each phase, we will extract the best approximation to the true ground state wavefunction. In computational terms, we will find three solutions to the general self-consistency problem of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hf-self-consistency-general}. In the end we want to compare the free energy of these three solutions, assessing which one is the most stable. Any HF approximation, being by construction not the true ground state, overestimates energy. Thus, comparing energies of our solutions we get a hint of the features of the true ground state.

\section{The EHM symmetries}

In this section we present the symmetries of the extended Hubbard model introduced in Eq.~\eqref{eq:extended-hubbard-model}
\[
	\hat H_\mathrm{EHM} =
	\underbrace{
		-t \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_\sigma \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
	}_{\hat H_t}
	\underbrace{
		\vphantom{\sum_{\langle ij \rangle}}
		+ U \sum_i \hat n_{i\uparrow} \hat n_{i\downarrow}
	}_{\hat H_U}
	\underbrace{
		- V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \hat n_{i\sigma} \hat n_{j\sigma'}
	}_{\hat H_V}
\]
The symmetries of the model can be divided in two groups: the ones related to the geometry of the square lattice, and those related to the algebraic properties of $\hat H_\mathrm{EHM}$. We start by the lattice symmetries.

\subsection{Lattice symmetries}\label{subsec:lattice-syms}

We study the EHM on the planar square lattice, with one orbital per site. We indicate with $L_x$ the number of sites in direction $x$, and $L_y$ in direction $y$. The hamiltonian of Eq.~\eqref{eq:extended-hubbard-model} respects three fundamental lattice symmetries:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Discrete translations symmetry in both the $x$ and $y$ directions. We refer to the groups of one-site translations as as $\mathrm{T}_1^{(x)}$, $\mathrm{T}_1^{(x)}$. It holds:
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-Tn-Tkn}
		\mathrm{T}_n^{(\ell)} \supset \mathrm{T}_{kn}^{(\ell)}
		\qquad
		k\in\mathbb{N}
	\end{equation}
	A shift in direction $\ell$ by $kn$ sites can be decomposed in $k$ shift by $n$ sites. Invariance by $n$-sites shifts, thus, implies invariance also for $kn$-sites shifts.
	%
	\item Discrete $\pi/2$ rotations symmetry. This symmetry is described by the four-fold cyclic point group $\mathrm{C}_4$, containing rotations by angle $\pi/2, \pi, 3\pi/2$ and identity. A similar property as for the translations holds for cyclic point groups,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-Cn-Cn/k}
		\mathrm{C}_n \supset \mathrm{C}_{n/k}
		\qquad
		k, n/k \in\mathbb{N}
	\end{equation}
	Invariance for rotations of angle $2\pi/n$ implies invariance for rotations of angle $k \times 2\pi/n$.
	%
	\item Inversion symmetry. We define $\mathrm{I}_2$ as the inversion group in two-dimensional space, containing the reflection operator and identity. This point may appear redundant, because
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-I2-C2}
		\mathrm{I}_2 \equiv \mathrm{C}_2
	\end{equation}
	In $2$D space, to rotate by $\pi$ around the origin and to mirror the position of a point are the same operation.
\end{enumerate}

The planar square lattice is bipartite. Let us give a definition. Consider a graph $\mathcal{L}$ and the set of its vertices $V(\mathcal{L}) = \lbrace v_i \rbrace$. Let $E(\mathcal{L})$ be the set of graph edges,
\[
	E(\mathcal{L}) = \left\lbrace (v,w) \mid v,w \in V(\mathcal{L}) \land \text{$v$, $w$ are connected} \right\rbrace
\]
The graph is said to be bipartite if two sub-graphs $\mathcal{S}_1$, $\mathcal{S}_2$ exist such that:
\begin{itemize}
	\item The vertices of $\mathcal{L}$ split in the two sub-graphs,
	\[
		V(\mathcal{L}) = V(\mathcal{S}_1) \cup V(\mathcal{S}_2)
	\]
	\item Vertices of one sub-graph are linked only to vertices of the other sub-graph. In other words
	\[
		v,w \in \mathcal{S}_i
		\quad\implies\quad
		(v,w) \notin E(\mathcal{L})
	\]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Spin and charge symmetries}\label{subsec:spin-charge-syms}

The conventional HM shows a global $\mathrm{U}(2)$ symmetry, extended to $\mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) / \mathbb{Z}_2 = \mathrm{SO}(4)$ symmetry at half-filling on a bipartite lattice \cite{arovas2022hubbard}, such as the square lattice. For the EHM, we limit to prove that we can identify a $\mathrm{U}(2) \subset \mathrm{SO}(4)$ global symmetry.

\paragraph{Gauge symmetry.} The EHM is symmetric under the global transformation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-symmetry}
	\hat c_{i\sigma} \to e^{-i\eta} \hat c_{i\sigma}
	\qquad
	\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \to e^{i\eta} \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger
	\qquad
	\eta \in \mathbb{R}
\end{equation}
To prove this, it suffices to note that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-symmetry-c+c-}
	\hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'}
	\quad\to\quad
	e^{i\eta} \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger e^{-i\eta} \hat c_{j\sigma'} = \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma'}
\end{equation}
As a consequence, any product of fermionic operators with an equal number of annihilations and creations is symmetric. Hence the kinetic part $\hat H_t$ and the interactions $\hat H_U$, $\hat H_V$ are invariant. As a consequence, the total number of particles (charge)
\[
	\hat{N} = \sum_{i\sigma} \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{i\sigma}
\]
is a good quantum number. The symmetry is described by the transformation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge-symmetry}, which is a pure phase shift by $\eta$. The relative group is $\mathrm{U}^\mathrm{c}(1)$, with a superscript ``$\mathrm{c}$'' indicating ``charge''.

\paragraph{Global spin symmetry}

The EHM is described by three operators: NN hopping, independent of $\sigma$; local repulsion between opposite-spin densities; non-local attraction, independent of $\sigma$. If we rotate all spins by an equal amount, the hamiltonian must remain identical. An identical observation holds for the number operator, $\hat N$. Thus $\hat H_\mathrm{EHM}-\mu\hat N$ must be exhibit a global $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ symmetry, related to the rotation in $3$D space of all spins altogether.

Let us prove this rigorously. Let the on-site spinor:
\[
	\hat \Psi_i \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		\hat c_{i\uparrow} \\ \hat c_{i\downarrow}
	\end{bmatrix}
\]
and the Pauli matrices
\[
	\tau^x \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		& 1 \\
		1 &
	\end{bmatrix}
	\qquad
	\tau^y \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		& -i \\
		i &
	\end{bmatrix}
	\qquad
	\tau^z \equiv \begin{bmatrix}
		1 & \\
		& -1
	\end{bmatrix}
\]
We define the local spin operator as:
\[
	\hat S_i^\alpha \equiv \frac{1}{2} \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \tau^\alpha \hat \Psi_i
	\qquad
	\left[ \hat S_j^\alpha, \hat S_j^\beta \right] = i \sum_\gamma \epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma} \hat S_j^\gamma
\]
with $\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ the Levi-Civita tensor. It can be checked by explicit calculation that the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ spin algebra is actually respected. For example, consider $\alpha=x$, $\beta=y$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\left[ \hat S_j^x, \hat S_j^y \right] &= \frac{1}{4} \left[
		\hat \Psi_j^\dagger \begin{bmatrix}
			& 1 \\
			1 &
		\end{bmatrix} \hat \Psi_j, \hat \Psi_j^\dagger \begin{bmatrix}
			& -i \\
			i &
		\end{bmatrix} \hat \Psi_j
	\right] \\
	&= \frac{1}{4} \left[
		\hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow} + \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow}, -i\hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow} + i \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow}
	\right] \\
	&= \frac{i}{4} \left[
		\hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}, \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow}
	\right] - \frac{i}{4} \left[
		\hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow}, \hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}
	\right] \\
	&= \frac{i}{2} \left[
		\hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}, \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow}
	\right] \\
	&= \frac{i}{2} \left(
		\hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow} \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow} - \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow} \hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}
	\right) \\
	&= \frac{i}{2} \left(
		- \hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow} \hat c_{j\uparrow} + \hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow} + \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow} \hat c_{j\downarrow} - \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}
	\right) \\
	&= \frac{i}{2} \left( 
		\hat c_{j\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\uparrow} - \hat c_{j\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{j\downarrow}
	\right) \\
	&= i \hat S_j^z
\end{aligned}
\]
We used Fermi anticommutation rules, $\lbrace \hat c_{i\sigma}, \hat c_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \rbrace = \delta_{ij} \delta_{\sigma\sigma'}$. Identical derivations can be carried out for all other components.

Let the raising and lowering operators:
\[
	\hat S_j^+ \equiv \frac{\hat S_j^x + i\hat S_j^y}{2}
	\qquad
	\hat S_j^- \equiv \frac{\hat S_j^x - i\hat S_j^y}{2}
\]
and the global spin operators
\[
	\hat S^\alpha \equiv \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \hat S_i^\alpha
	\qquad
	\left[ \hat S^\alpha, \hat S^\beta \right] = i \sum_\gamma \epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma} \hat S^\gamma
\]
being $\mathcal{L}$ the lattice. Explicitly,
\[
	\hat S^z \equiv \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \left(
		\hat n_{i\uparrow} - \hat n_{i\downarrow}
	\right)
	\qquad
	\hat S^+ \equiv \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow}
	\qquad
	\hat S^- \equiv \left( \hat S^+ \right)^\dagger
\]
The EHM hamiltonian and the number operator commutate with the global spin operator. For the kinetic part $\hat H_t$ and the local repulsion $\hat H_U$ this is a well known result \cite{arovas2022hubbard,pavarini2015many},
\[
	\comm{\hat H_t + \hat H_U}{\hat{\mathbf{S}}} = 0
	\qquad
	\comm{\hat N}{\hat{\mathbf{S}}} = 0
\]
For the non-local attraction, since it depends solely on total densities on neighbouring sites, spin-rotational invariance must hold. Anyway, let us give a short formal proof following this chain of results:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item The non-local attraction can be written as:
	\begin{align}
		\hat H_V &= V\sum_{\ev{ij}} \sum_{\sigma\sigma'} \hat n_{i\sigma} \hat n_{j\sigma'} \nonumber \\
		&= V\sum_{\ev{ij}} \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat \Psi_i \hat \Psi_j^\dagger \hat \Psi_j \label{eq:ehm-HV-4xPsi}
	\end{align}
	having we used $\hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat \Psi_i = \hat c_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\uparrow} + \hat c_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{i\downarrow}$.
	%
	\item Similarly, the number operator is given by:
	\begin{align}
		\hat N &= \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \sum_{\sigma} \hat n_{i\sigma} \nonumber \\
		&= \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat \Psi_i \label{eq:ehm-N-2xPsi}
	\end{align}
	%
	\item Let now $\hat R(\bm{\theta})$ be any global spin rotation,
	\[
		\hat R (\bm{\theta}) \equiv \exp \left(-i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}\right)
		\qq{where}
		\bm{\theta} = (\theta_x,\theta_y,\theta_z)
	\]
	Spinors transform as:
	\[
		\hat R (\bm{\theta}) \hat \Psi_i \hat R (\bm{\theta})^\dagger = \mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}) \hat \Psi_i
	\]
	with $\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}) \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$ the unitary matrix associated to said rotation,
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-R-exp}
		\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}) = \exp(- \frac{i}{2} \bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau})
		\qq{where}
		\bm{\tau} = (\tau_x,\tau_y,\tau_z)
	\end{equation}
	Let us prove this. Let $\hat A = i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}$ e $\hat B = \hat \Psi_i$. The following identity, a consequence of Baker-Campbell-Housdorff formula, holds:
	\[
		e^{\hat A} \hat B e^{-\hat A} = \hat B + \comm{\hat A}{\hat B} + \frac{1}{2!} \comm{\hat A}{ \comm{\hat A}{\hat B}} + \frac{1}{3!} \comm{\hat A}{\comm{\hat A}{\comm{\hat A}{\hat B}}} + \cdots
	\]
	The first order of the expansion gives:
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		[i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}, \hat \Psi_i] &= i \sum_\alpha \theta_\alpha [\hat S^\alpha, \hat \Psi_i] \\
		&= -\frac{i}{2} \sum_\alpha \theta_\alpha \tau^\alpha \hat{\Psi}_i \\
		&= -\frac{i}{2} \bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau} \hat \Psi_i
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	We omitted some purely algebraic passages. The second order:
	\[
		\begin{aligned}
			\comm{
				i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}
			}{
				\comm{ i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}} }{ \hat{\Psi}_i }
			}
			&= \comm{
				i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}
			}{
				-\frac{i}{2} (\bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau}) \hat \Psi_i
			} \\
			&= -\frac{i}{2} \bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau} \comm{
				i \bm{\theta} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}
			}{
				\Psi_i
			} \\
			&= \frac{1}{4} (\bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau})^2 \hat{\Psi}_i
		\end{aligned}
	\]
	and so on. Collecting all terms,
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\hat R (\bm{\theta}) \hat \Psi_i \hat R (\bm{\theta})^\dagger &= \hat \Psi_i -\frac{i}{2} \bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau} \hat \Psi_i + \frac{1}{2! \times 4} (\bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau})^2 \hat{\Psi}_i + \cdots \\
		&= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k!} \left( - \frac{i}{2} \bm{\theta} \cdot \bm{\tau} \right)^k \hat \Psi_i \\
		&= \mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta}) \hat \Psi_i
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	In last passage, we identified the series as the Taylor expansion of the exponential, recognising the expression for $\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta})$ given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-R-exp}.
	%
	\item Then, the group acts on the product $\hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat \Psi_i$ as
	\[
		\begin{aligned}
			\hat R \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat \Psi_i \hat R^\dagger &= \hat R \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat R^\dagger \hat R \hat \Psi_i \hat R^\dagger \\
			&= \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \mathcal{R}^\dagger \mathcal{R} \hat \Psi_i\\
			&= \hat \Psi_i^\dagger \hat \Psi_i
		\end{aligned}
	\]
	being $\mathcal{R} \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$. Recalling Eqns.~\eqref{eq:ehm-HV-4xPsi}, \eqref{eq:ehm-N-2xPsi}, both $\hat H_V$ and $\hat N$ are $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ symmetric.
\end{enumerate}
The EHM is symmetric under global spin rotations. Note that $\hat H_V$ is actually symmetric under local spin rotations, meaning that we could rotate the spin at each site by a different amount and get the same interaction. The same holds for $\hat H_U$. It is the kinetic operator that makes the EHM globally (and not locally) $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ symmetric.

Note that the $U^\mathrm{c}(1)$ gauge symmetry and the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ spin rotations symmetry can be combined,
\[
	\mathrm{U}^\mathrm{c}(1) \otimes \mathrm{SU}(2) = \mathrm{U}(2)
\]
We will refer to $\mathrm{U}(2)$ symmetry to indicate full charge-spin conservation. Finally, note the group of $3$D rotations contains the subgroup of $2$D rotations around a given axis $z$,
\[
	\mathrm{U}^z(1) \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)
\]
where the $z$ superscript serves to distinguish from the gauge group. The $z$ axis does not need to be identified with the ``real'' $z$ axis, perpendicular with the lattice plane. Any given direction works. Inverting our logic, we can build the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ generators, starting from $\hat S^z$, after we have chosen the direction $z$. In terms of commutation relations, a given operator can respect $\mathrm{U}^z(1)$ but not $\mathrm{SU}(2)$: in that case, it will commutate to zero with $\hat S^z$, and not the other spin operators.

\subsection{Particle-hole symmetry at half-filling}\label{subsec:phs}

The half-filled EHM on the square lattice exhibits particle-hole symmetry (PHS). The presence of this additional symmetry is a consequence of the fact that the square lattice is bipartite. Let us prove this. Consider the generic unitary particle-hole (PH) transformation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phs-P-general}
	\mathrm{P}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\hat c_{i\sigma}
	\quad\to\quad
	e^{i\eta_{i\sigma}} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger
\end{equation}
being in general $\eta_i$ a phase dependent on the fermionic quantum state $(i\sigma)$. Inserting the above transformation in the EHM, we get
\[
	\mathrm{P} \lbrace \hat H \rbrace =
	-t \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_\sigma e^{i(\eta_{i\sigma} + \eta_{j\sigma})} \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma}^\dagger
	+U \sum_i \hat h_{i\uparrow} \hat h_{i\downarrow} \hat h_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\uparrow}^\dagger
	- V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger
\]
For the two interactions $\hat H_U$, $\hat H_V$ the phase factors cancelled out. We aim to find the conditions under which the above hamiltonian is mapped onto the EHM of \eqref{eq:extended-hubbard-model},
\[
	\mathrm{P} \lbrace \hat H \rbrace \stackrel{?}{=}
	-t \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_\sigma \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma}
	+U \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}} \hat h_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\downarrow} \hat h_{i\uparrow}
	- V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma}
\]

Before starting the derivation, which is an extension of the argument carried out for the conventional HM \cite{arovas2022hubbard}, let us discuss some properties of the hole operators. Due to the presence of the phase factors in the PHS operative definition, the Fermi anticommutation rules hold regardlessly of the phase factor:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\lbrace \hat h_{i\sigma}, \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \rbrace &= e^{i(\eta_{i\sigma}-\eta_{j\sigma'})} \lbrace \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger, \hat c_{j\sigma'} \rbrace \\
	&= e^{i(\eta_{i\sigma}-\eta_{j\sigma'})} \delta_{ij} \delta_{\sigma\sigma'} \\
	&= \delta_{ij} \delta_{\sigma\sigma'}
\end{aligned}
\]
The on-site density operator is given by:
\[
	\hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger = \hat n_{i\sigma}
\]
being $\hat n_{i\sigma}$ the fermionic number operator on site $i$ with spin $\sigma$. It follows, for quartic interactions
\begin{align}
	\hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger &= -\hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \nonumber \\
	&= \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger - \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \delta_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\sigma'} \nonumber \\
	&= - \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger + \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger - \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \delta_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\sigma'} \nonumber \\
	&= \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'} - \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma} + \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger - \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \delta_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\sigma'} \nonumber \\
	&= - \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'} - \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma} + \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger - \left(
		\hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger - \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}
	\right) \delta_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\sigma'} \nonumber \\
	&= \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma} + \left(
		\hat n_{i\sigma} + \hat n_{j\sigma'}
	\right) - 1 - \left(
		\hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger - \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}
	\right) \delta_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\sigma'} \label{eq:phs-hhhh-chain}
\end{align}
The first term of the last passage is mirrored with respect to the starting term. Now, let us deal with the kinetic, local and non-local interactions separately.

\paragraph{Kinetic term.}

Consider the kinetic term,
\[
	\hat H_t = -t \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_\sigma \hat c_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{j\sigma}
\]
Apply the PH transform of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phs-P-general}
\[
	\mathrm{P} \lbrace \hat H_t \rbrace = -t \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_\sigma e^{i(\eta_{i\sigma} + \eta_{j\sigma})} \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma}^\dagger
\]
Anti-commutate the two fermionic operators, and exchange site labels $i \leftrightarrow j$. We get:
\[
	\mathrm{P} \lbrace \hat H_t \rbrace = -t \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_\sigma e^{i(\eta_{i\sigma} + \eta_{j\sigma}+\pi)} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma}
\]
A necessary condition on the unitary operation $\mathrm{P}$ is the following,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phs-P-condition}
	\eta_{i\sigma} + \eta_{j\sigma} = (2k-1)\pi
	\qq{with}
	k\in\mathbb{Z}
\end{equation}
valid for NN sites.

\paragraph{Local repulsion.}

Substitute Eq.~\eqref{eq:phs-hhhh-chain} in $\hat H_U$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\mathrm{P} \lbrace \hat H_U \rbrace &= U \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}} \hat h_{i\uparrow} \hat h_{i\downarrow} \hat h_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \\
	&= U \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}} \hat h_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\downarrow}\hat h_{i\uparrow} + U \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}} \left(
		\hat n_{i\uparrow} + \hat n_{i\downarrow}
	\right) - U \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}} (1) \\
	&= U \sum_i \hat h_{i\uparrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\downarrow}^\dagger \hat h_{i\downarrow}\hat h_{i\uparrow} + U (\hat N - L_x L_y)
\end{aligned}
\]
For the half-filled state $\langle \hat N \rangle = L_x L_y$. The unitary map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phs-P-general}, independently of the condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phs-P-condition}, leaves the hamiltonian identical at half-filling.

\paragraph{Non-local attraction.}

Proceed identically for $\hat H_V$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\mathrm{P} \lbrace \hat H_V \rbrace &= - V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma} \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \\
	&= - V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma} - V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \left(
		\hat n_{i\sigma} + \hat n_{j\sigma'}
	\right) + V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} (1) \\
	&= - V \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \sum_{\sigma \sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'}^\dagger \hat h_{j\sigma'} \hat h_{i\sigma} - 2zV (\hat N - L_x L_y)
\end{aligned}
\]
with $z=4$ the lattice coordination number. In the last passage, we used that on the square lattice
\begin{align}
	\sum_{\ev{ij}} (1) &= \sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}/2} z \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{L_x L_y}{2} \times z \label{eq:square-NN-ghost-sum}
\end{align}
As before, the non-local attraction is particle-hole symmetric at half-filling. As for the local repulsion, PHS is given just the operation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phs-P-general}. The condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phs-P-condition} is irrelevant.

Recollecting everything, if $\mathrm{P}$ is defined by the unitary operation
\[
	\mathrm{P}
	\quad\colon\quad
	\begin{cases}
		\hat c_{i\sigma} \to \hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger
		&\qq{for}
		i \in \mathcal{S}_1 \\
		\hat c_{i\sigma} \to -\hat h_{i\sigma}^\dagger
		&\qq{for}
		i \in \mathcal{S}_2
	\end{cases}
\]
and the system is half-filled, PHS is respected.
Any other phase, as long as it alternates by $\pi$ on the two sublattices, is good enough. As for the conventional HM \cite{arovas2022hubbard}, this result can be generalised to any bipartite lattice. In turn, if a lattice is not bipartite, PHS is not present. To summarise, we report in Tab.~\ref{tab:symmetry-groups} all identified symmetries.

\section{Symmetry breaking channels in real space}\label{subsec:ehm-sym-break}

In many-body Physics, a phase transition is described by some order parameter rising from zero to a non-zero value. This is associated with some spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB). In order to simulate different phases, we need a way to distinguish them based on which symmetries they break (or not). We also need a way to work computationally in a given symmetry sector. HF methods, by reducing the hamiltonian to a non-interacting approximation, offer a simple framework to these ends. Indeed, once we identified all symmetries of a given phase, we just impose them on our approximation of the full hamiltonian. The found ground-state approximation will entail these symmetries by construction.

Recall now the discussion of the HF method of Sec.~\ref{sec:sketch-hf-method}. The ending point were Eqns.~\eqref{eq:hf-solution-h+-}, \eqref{eq:hf-solution-h++}, which identify the self-consistent single-particle fields which approximate the original quartic hamiltonian. In order to get there, we applied Wick's theorem to the quartic part of the hamiltonian and derived the most general expressions of $\mathbf{h}$. In next sections here apply Wick's theorem to the quartic operators of $\hat H_\mathrm{EHM}$ and discuss the symmetry properties of the resulting decomposition.

Before starting, let us define precisely the three phases we will investigate in terms of symmetries:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Normal phase: no broken symmetry.
	\item Antiferromagnetic phase: simultaneous $\mathrm{SU}(2) \SSB \mathrm{U}^z(1)$ and $\mathrm{T}_1^{(x)} \otimes \mathrm{T}_1^{(y)} \SSB \mathrm{T}_2^{(x)} \otimes \mathrm{T}_2^{(y)}$. Among all possible realisations of antiferromagnetism, we realise a SDW with alternating distribution of spin densities;
	\item Superconducting phase: simultaneous $\mathrm{U}^c(1) \SSB 0$ and $\mathrm{C}_4 \SSB \mathrm{C}_2$. We investigate anisotropic superconductivity, allowing for the particle number to be not conserved and for nematic effects.
\end{itemize}
{\color{tabred}\textbf{Nematic effects are observed in unconventional superconductors, and this is the reason of our interest in the possibility of rotational SSBs.}} We will theoretically discuss the nematic SSB $\mathrm{C}_4 \to \mathrm{C}_2$ also for normal and AF phases, as if discrete rotational symmetry was broken. As we will see, while it not trivial to exclude it \textit{a priori}, it will actually turn out that the HF ground state preserves $\mathrm{C}_4$ in both cases.

\]
with $x,y \in \mathbb{N}$. The lattice versors are:

as in Fig.~\ref{fig:square-nearest-neighbors}.

\subsection{Local repulsion}

The local repulsion $\hat H_U$ is the quartic operator of the conventional HM,
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_U &= U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \\
	&= U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
\end{aligned}
\]
In order to obtain the self-consistent values of $h_{\alpha\beta}^{+-}$, $h_{\alpha\beta}^{++}$ we need to compute an expectation value of the full hamiltonian. Wick's theorem guarantees that, for the wavefunctions we are using, the expectation value of a quartic fermionic operator is the sum of all fully contracted terms. Recalling the nomenclature of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hf-wick-hfc},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-HU-wick}
	\langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
	\rangle = \underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} 
		\rangle
	}_{\text{Hartree}}
	- 
	\underbrace{
			\langle 
			\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
			\rangle \langle	
			\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} 
			\rangle 
		}_{\text{Fock}}
	+ 
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} 
		\rangle 
	}_{\text{Cooper}}
\end{equation}
where the equal sign holds as long as we perform the averaging operation on the subspace of {\color{tabred}\textbf{gaussian states}}. Now we discuss the above EVs separately, identifying if any can be neglected based on selection rules in a given phase.

\paragraph{Hartree term.} The Hartree part of the above decomposition is a product of density EVs,
\[
	\langle 
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
	\rangle \langle	
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}
	\rangle
\]
Recalling the groups of Tab.~\ref{tab:symmetry-groups}, we see that the density operator preserves all symmetries. Single-site density operators commutate with spin operators and are gauge-invariant. Moreover, shifting or rotating the lattice leaves density operators unchanged. Thus no selection rule applies, and the above EVs are in principle non-zero.

\paragraph{Fock terms.} For the Fock part, notice that
\[
	\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} = \hat S_\mathbf{r}^+
\]
being $S_\mathbf{r}^+$ the local raising operator. From $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ algebra we have:
\[
	\comm{\hat S_\mathbf{r}^+}{\hat S^\alpha} \neq 0
	\qquad
	\alpha \in \lbrace x,y,z \rbrace
\]
If the ground-state is invariant under global spin rotations, these EVs must be zero. Gauge symmetry gives no selection rules, due to Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge-symmetry-c+c-}.

\paragraph{Cooper terms.} The Cooper part can be non-zero only if charge is not conserved. In terms of the gauge symmetry, it suffices to apply the map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gauge-symmetry} to $\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger$ 
\[
	\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger
	\quad\to\quad
	e^{2i\eta} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger
\]
to conclude that this operator is in general not gauge invariant. It is instead $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-symmetric. To prove this, it suffices to compute commutators of $\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger$ with $\hat S^z$, $\hat S^+$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat S^z} &= \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\frac{1}{2} \sum_{\mathbf{r}'\in\mathcal{L}} (\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\uparrow}-\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\downarrow})} \\
	&= \frac{1}{2} \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}-\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} \\
	&= \frac{1}{2} \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger - \frac{1}{2} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}}
\end{aligned}
\]
Since:
\begin{align}
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}} &= \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} - \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \nonumber \\
	&= -\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \left(
	1 - \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \right) \nonumber \\
	&= -\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \label{eq:[c+,n]}
\end{align}
we have:
\[
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat S^z} = -\frac{1}{2} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger + \frac{1}{2} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger = 0
\]

Moreover, considering the raising operator:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat S^+} &= \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\frac{1}{2} \sum_{\mathbf{r}'\in\mathcal{L}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}^\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}^\downarrow}} \\
	&= \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} \\
	&= \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger}{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} - \left( \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \right)^2 \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger}{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}} \\
	&= 0
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used the facts that every operator commutates with itself, and a squared creation operator is null. These commutation relations, together, prove that Cooper EVs are legitimately non-zero for a $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-preserving phase.

\subsection{Non-local attraction}

In order to analyse similarly the non-local attraction $\hat H_V$, it is useful to decompose the operator in spin channels:
\[
	\hat H_V \equiv 
	\underbrace{
		\hat H_V^{\uparrow\uparrow} + \hat H_V^{\downarrow\downarrow}
	}_{\text{Same spin}} +
	\underbrace{
		\hat H_V^{\uparrow\downarrow} + \hat H_V^{\downarrow\uparrow}
	}_{\text{Opposite spin}}
\]
being
\[
	\hat H_V^{\sigma\sigma'} \equiv - V \sum_{\langle \mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}' \rangle} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma'}
\]

Let us start from the opposite spin sector. On the square lattice (as for any bipartite lattice) NNs belong to different sublattices. This implies that any NNs sum can be rewritten as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-NNs-sum(f)}
	\sum_{\langle \mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}' \rangle} f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') = \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}/2} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta})
\end{equation}
with $f$ some function. We are using the notation of Fig.~\ref{fig:square-nearest-neighbors}. The symbol ``$\mathcal{L}/2$'' indicates one of the two sublattices $\mathcal{S}_1$, $\mathcal{S}_2$ represented in Fig.~\ref{fig:square-sublattices}. We can rewrite:
\[
	\hat H_V^{\sigma\sigma'} = - V 
	\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}/2} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \left(\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}-\bm{\delta}\sigma'}\right)
\]
Now, if we choose $\mathcal{L}/2 = \mathcal{S}_1$, this gives:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_V^{\uparrow\downarrow} &= - V 
	\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{S}_1} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \left(\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\downarrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}-\bm{\delta}\downarrow}\right) \\
	\hat H_V^{\downarrow\uparrow} &= - V 
	\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{S}_1} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \left(\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\uparrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}-\bm{\delta}\uparrow}\right)
\end{aligned}
\]
These two operators are ``complementary'': $\sigma=\uparrow$ densities act on $\mathcal{S}_1$ for $\hat H_V^{\uparrow\downarrow}$, on $\mathcal{S}_2$ for $\hat H_V^{\downarrow\uparrow}$; $\sigma=\downarrow$ densities, the opposite. Then their sum can be expressed as:
\begin{align}
	\hat H_V^{(\text{o.s.})} &\equiv \hat H_V^{\uparrow\downarrow}  + \hat H_V^{\downarrow\uparrow} \nonumber \\
	&= - V 
	\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \left(\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\downarrow} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}-\bm{\delta}\downarrow}\right) \label{eq:ehm-HV(os)}
\end{align}
Wick's theorem applied to this quartic operator gives:
\begin{align}
	\langle 
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \hat n_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}
	\rangle &= \langle
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
	\rangle \nonumber \\
	&\simeq
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}
		\rangle
	}_{\text{Hartree}}
	- 
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
		\rangle 
	}_{\text{Fock}}
	+
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow}^\dagger
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}
		\rangle 
	}_{\text{Cooper}}	
	\label{eq:ehm-HV(os)-wick}
\end{align}

For the same-spin sector we define
\begin{align}
	\hat H_V^{(\mathrm{s.s.})} &\equiv \hat H_V^{\uparrow\uparrow}  + \hat H_V^{\downarrow\downarrow} \nonumber \\
	&= -V \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}/2} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \sum_{\sigma \in \lbrace \uparrow, \downarrow \rbrace} \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta} \sigma} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r}-\bm{\delta}\sigma}
	\right) \label{eq:ehm-HV(ss)}
\end{align}
Note that, with respect to the o.s. sector, we are summing on half the sites two times: one for spin $\uparrow$, one for spin $\downarrow$. Applying Wick's theorem:
\begin{align}
	\langle 
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}
	\rangle &= \langle
	\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
	\rangle \nonumber \\
	&\simeq
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}
		\rangle
	}_{\text{Hartree}}
	- 
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\rangle 
	}_{\text{Fock}}
	+
	\underbrace{
		\langle 
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma}^\dagger
		\rangle \langle	
		\hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}
		\rangle 
	}_{\text{Cooper}}	
	\label{eq:ehm-HV(ss)-wick}
\end{align}
As we did for $\hat H_U$, we analyse each separately.

\paragraph{Hartree terms.}

As it was for $\hat H_U$, Hartree EVs of Eqns.~\eqref{eq:ehm-HV(os)}, \eqref{eq:ehm-HV(ss)} are not subject to selection rules for any symmetry of Tab.~\ref{tab:symmetry-groups}. Thus they are allowed in both sectors.

\paragraph{Fock terms.}

The Fock EVs from the two sectors behave differently. Let us compute the following generic commutation relations:
\begin{align}
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'}}{\hat S^z} &= \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'}}{\frac{1}{2} \sum_{\mathbf{r}'\in\mathcal{L}} (\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\uparrow}-\hat n_{\mathbf{r}'\downarrow}) } \nonumber \\
	&= \frac{(-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}}}{2} \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'} + \frac{(-1)^{\delta_{\sigma'=\downarrow}}}{2} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'}}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma}} \nonumber \\
	&= \left(
		-\frac{(-1)^{\delta_{\sigma=\downarrow}}}{2} + \frac{(-1)^{\delta_{\sigma'=\downarrow}}}{2}
	\right)\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'} \label{eq:ehm-HV-wick-int1}
\end{align}
where in last passage we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:[c+,n]} and its conjugate version,
\[
	\comm{\hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'}}{\hat n_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma}} = \hat c_{\mathbf{r}+\bm{\delta}\sigma'}
\]
Now, if $\sigma=\sigma'$ the last passage of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-HV-wick-int1} is zero; otherwise is not. This proves that the o.s. Fock EVs are non-zero only for a phase that completely breaks $\mathrm{SU}(2)$; instead, in the s.s. sector we can have non-zero EVs for a phase that reduces $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ to $\mathrm{U}^z(1)$.

\paragraph{Cooper terms.} For the Cooper terms, identical considerations as for $\hat H_U$ hold. Gauge symmetry must be broken to allow for non zero EVs, while $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ symmetry survives. The proof is analogous to the one given for $\hat H_U$.

By identifying these selection rules, we set grounds for the detailed derivation of each chapter and heavily simplify application of HF model to the EHM. We now move to the last part of this chapter: reciprocal space description of the EHM.

\section{The EHM in reciprocal space}

The EHM is symmetric under discrete lattice translations. We will investigate phases that do not (completely) break translational invariance. It is thus convenient to move to reciprocal space, by Fourier-transforming the hamiltonian. We will use the following convention:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier-convention}
	\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \Feq \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_xL_y}} \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} e^{-i \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
\end{equation}
Throughout the text, $\Feq$ will indicate a passage where a Fourier transform is used. ``BZ'' is shorthand notation for the Brillouin Zone.

It is useful to recall that the following equation for Kronecker's delta holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-delta}
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}} e^{i \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}} = \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{0}}
\end{equation}
On one  sublattice:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ehm-delta/2}
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}/2} e^{i \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}} = \frac{1}{2} \delta_{\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{0}}
\end{equation}
The notation $\mathcal{L}/2$ is shorthand to indicate $\mathcal{S}_1$ or $\mathcal{S}_2$.

\paragraph{Kinetic part.}

Let us transform $\hat H_t$:
\begin{align}
	\hat H_t &= -t \sum_{\langle \mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}' \rangle} \sum_\sigma \left[
	\hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}'\sigma} + \hc
	\right] \nonumber \\
	&\stackrel{\mathcal{F}}{=} -t \sum_{\langle \mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}' \rangle} \sum_\sigma \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_xL_y}} \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_xL_y}} \sum_{\mathbf{k}'\in\mathrm{BZ}} e^{-i\mathbf{k}'\cdot\mathbf{r}'} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma} + \hc \nonumber \\
	&= -2t\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \times \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}/2} e^{i(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}')\cdot\mathbf{r}} \times \sum_{\bm{\delta} \in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} e^{i \mathbf{k}' \cdot \bm{\delta}} \nonumber \\
	&= -2t\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}'} \sum_\sigma \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \left[
		\frac{e^{i k_x}+e^{-i k_x}}{2} + \frac{e^{i k_y}+e^{-i k_y}}{2}
	\right] \\
	&= \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathrm{BZ}} \sum_\sigma \epsilon_\mathbf{k} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma} \label{eq:Ht-Fourier}
\end{align}
where we used Eqns.~\eqref{eq:ehm-NNs-sum(f)}, \eqref{eq:ehm-delta/2}. We defined the bare bands: 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bare-bands}
	\epsilon_\mathbf{k} \equiv -2t \left(
	\cos k_x + \cos k_y
	\right)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Local repulsion.}

The local repulsion transforms as:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat H_U &= U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\uparrow} \\
	&\Feq U \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} \frac{1}{(L_x L_y)^2} \sum_{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\mathbf{k}_3,\mathbf{k}_4} e^{i \mathbf{k}_1 \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1 \uparrow}^\dagger e^{i \mathbf{k}_2 \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2 \downarrow}^\dagger e^{-i \mathbf{k}_3 \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3 \downarrow} e^{-i \mathbf{k}_4 \cdot \mathbf{r}} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4 \uparrow} \\
	&= \frac{U}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\mathbf{k}_3,\mathbf{k}_4} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1 \uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2 \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3 \downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4 \uparrow} \times \frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}} e^{i \left[ (\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2) - (\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4) \right] \cdot \mathbf{r}} \\
	&= \frac{U}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\mathbf{k}_3,\mathbf{k}_4} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1 \uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2 \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3 \downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4 \uparrow} \delta_{\mathbf{k}_1+\mathbf{k}_2 = \mathbf{k}_3+\mathbf{k}_4}
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-delta}. Let $\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2 = \mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4 = \mathbf{K}$, and define $\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{k}'$ such that
\[
	\mathbf{k}_1 \equiv \mathbf{K} + \mathbf{k}
	\qquad
	\mathbf{k}_2 \equiv \mathbf{K} - \mathbf{k}
	\qquad
	\mathbf{k}_3 \equiv \mathbf{K} - \mathbf{k}'
	\qquad
	\mathbf{k}_4 \equiv \mathbf{K} + \mathbf{k}'
\]
Sums over these variables must be intended as over the Brillouin Zone ($\mathrm{BZ}$). Hence:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HU-fourier}
	\hat H_U \Feq \frac{U}{L_x L_y}
	\sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}' \uparrow}
\end{equation}


\paragraph{Non-local attraction.} 

To transform the non-local attraction $\hat H_V$, it is useful to start by defining:
\[
	h_{\mathbf{r}\sigma\sigma'} \equiv - V \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \left(
		\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r} + \bm{\delta} \sigma'} + \hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r} - \bm{\delta} \sigma'}
	\right)
\]
with:
\[
	\hat H_V = \sum_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{L}/2} \sum_{\sigma,\sigma'} h_{\mathbf{r}\sigma\sigma'}
\]
The product of densities on neighbouring sites transforms as:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\hat n_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \hat n_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma'} &= \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{r} \pm \bm{\delta} \sigma'} \hat c_{\mathbf{r}\sigma} \\
	&\Feq \frac{1}{(L_xL_y)^2} \sum_{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\mathbf{k}_3,\mathbf{k}_4}
	e^{i \left[ (\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2) - (\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4) \right] \cdot \mathbf{r}} e^{\pm i(\mathbf{k}_2-\mathbf{k}_3) \cdot \bm{\delta}}  \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1 \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2 \sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3 \sigma'} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4\sigma}
\end{aligned}
\]
Then:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	h_{\mathbf{r}\sigma\sigma'} &\Feq -\frac{V}{(L_xL_y)^2} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \sum_{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\mathbf{k}_3,\mathbf{k}_4} e^{i \left[ (\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2) - (\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4) \right] \cdot \mathbf{r}} \\
	&\hspace{0.3\textwidth} \times \left(
	e^{ i(\mathbf{k}_2-\mathbf{k}_3) \cdot \bm{\delta}} + e^{ -i(\mathbf{k}_2-\mathbf{k}_3) \cdot \bm{\delta}}
	\right)
	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1 \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2 \sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3 \sigma'} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4\sigma} \\
	&= -\frac{2V}{(L_xL_y)^2} \sum_{\bm{\delta}\in \lbrace \underline{\mathbf{x}}, \underline{\mathbf{y}} \rbrace} \sum_{\mathbf{k}_1,\mathbf{k}_2,\mathbf{k}_3,\mathbf{k}_4} e^{i \left[ (\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2) - (\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4) \right] \cdot \mathbf{r}} \cos\left[
	(\mathbf{k}_2-\mathbf{k}_3) \cdot \bm{\delta}
	\right]	\hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1 \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2 \sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3 \sigma'} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4\sigma}
\end{aligned}
\]
The full non-local interaction is given by summing over all sites of $\mathcal{L}/2$ (which is, one sublattice). Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-delta/2} gives back momentum conservation
\[
	\frac{1}{L_xL_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}/2} e^{i \left[ (\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2) - (\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4) \right] \cdot \mathbf{r}} = \frac{1}{2} \delta_{\mathbf{k}_1 + \mathbf{k}_2 = \mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4}
\]
where we used Eq.~\eqref{eq:ehm-delta/2}. Define $\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{k}'$ and $\mathbf{K}$ as we did fo the local repulsion, and
\[
	\delta \mathbf{k} \equiv \mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}'
\]
Hence:
\begin{align}
	\hat H_V &= \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{L}/2} \sum_{\sigma,\sigma'} h_{\mathbf{r}\sigma\sigma'} \nonumber \\
	&\Feq - \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\sigma\sigma'} \sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \cos\left(
	\delta \mathbf{k} \cdot \bm{\delta}
	\right)	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma'} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \nonumber \\
	&= - \frac{V}{L_x L_y} 	\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}
	\sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \left[
	\cos \left(
	\delta k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	\delta k_y
	\right)	
	\right]	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k} \sigma'}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}' \sigma'} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \label{eq:HV-fourier}
\end{align}

Finally, we can separate in same spin and opposite spin channels. This gives:
\begin{multline*}
	\hat H_V \Feq 
	\overbrace{
		- \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\sigma}
		\sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \left[
		\cos \left(
		\delta k_x
		\right)	+ \cos \left(
		\delta k_y
		\right)	
		\right]	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma}
	}^{\mathrm{s.s.}} \\
	\underbrace{
		- \frac{V}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\sigma}
		\sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \left[
		\cos \left(
		\delta k_x
		\right)	+ \cos \left(
		\delta k_y
		\right)	
		\right]	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\overline{\sigma}}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\overline{\sigma}} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma}
	}_{\mathrm{o.s.}}
\end{multline*}
The o.s. part can be simplified: since
\[
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\overline{\sigma}}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\overline{\sigma}} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} = \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\overline{\sigma}}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \sigma}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\sigma} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\overline{\sigma}} 
\]
due to fermionic anti-commutation rules, and since the composite transform
\[
	\mathbf{k} \to -\mathbf{k}
	\qquad
	\mathbf{k}' \to -\mathbf{k}'	
\]
leaves the form factor $\cos \left( \delta k_x \right)	+ \cos \left( \delta k_y \right)$ invariant, we reduce the spin sum to two times the $\sigma=\uparrow$ part,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HV-os-fourier}
	\hat H_V^{(\mathrm{o.s.})} = - \frac{2V}{L_x L_y}
	\sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \left[
	\cos \left(
	\delta k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	\delta k_y
	\right)	
	\right]	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\uparrow}
\end{equation}
The $\mathcal{F}$-transform of the s.s. part is given explicitly by:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:HV-ss-fourier}
	\hat H_V^{(\mathrm{s.s.})} = - \frac{V}{L_x L_y}
	\sum_{\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{k}, \mathbf{k}'} \left[
	\cos \left(
	\delta k_x
	\right)	+ \cos \left(
	\delta k_y
	\right)	
	\right]\\
	\times \left[
	\hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\uparrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\uparrow} + \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k} \downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}\downarrow}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{k}'\downarrow} \hat c_{\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k}'\downarrow}
	\right]
\end{multline}

All these expressions are going to be used in the analysis of each phase. In reciprocal space, for a quartic operator $\hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4}$ we distinguish contractions as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Hartree contractions:
	\[
		\wick{
			\c c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \c c_{\mathbf{k}_4}
		}
		\qquad
		\wick{
			\hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \c c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \c c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4}
		}
	\]
	\item Fock contractions:
	\[
		\wick{
			\c c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \c c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4}
		}
		\qquad
		\wick{
			\hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \c c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \c c_{\mathbf{k}_4}
		}
	\]
	\item Cooper contractions:
	\[
		\wick{
			\c c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \c c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_4}
		}
		\qquad
		\wick{
			\hat c_{\mathbf{k}_1}^\dagger \hat c_{\mathbf{k}_2}^\dagger \c c_{\mathbf{k}_3} \c c_{\mathbf{k}_4}
		}
	\]
\end{itemize}
with appropriate sign factors. With the EHM expressed in reciprocal space, there is no general feature left to address. We can move to the detailed discussion of each phase.

We here introduce a set of orthonormal functions that results particularly useful to represent quantities defined on NNs sites on the square lattice. Let us start from a generic function of two variables $f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$. Suppose that, for a given centre $\mathbf{r}$, the function is non-zero only on the site itself ($\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}$) and its NNs $\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}\pm\bm{\delta}$, with $\bm{\delta}\in\lbrace\underline{\mathbf{x}},\underline{\mathbf{y}}\rbrace$. In other words,
\[
	f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \neq 0
	\quad\iff\quad
	\abs{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'} = 0, 1
\]
in units of the lattice step. The following identity holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}) &= f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \delta_{\mathbf{r}'=\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}} \\
	&= f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \left(
		\varphi^{(s^*)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} + \varphi^{(d)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} + \sqrt{2} \varphi^{(p_x)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}
	\right)
\end{aligned}
\]
where we defined the set of orthonormal functions $\varphi_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}^{(\gamma)}$ listed explicitly in Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-real-factors} and sketched in Fig.~\ref{fig:x-wave-real-factors}.

The orthonormality condition reads:
\[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} \varphi_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}^{(\gamma)} \varphi_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}^{(\gamma')*} = \delta_{\gamma\gamma'}
\]
Similarly, also the following relations hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}) &= f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \varphi^{(s)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} \\
	f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}})
	&= f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \left(
	\varphi^{(s^*)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} + \varphi^{(d)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} - \sqrt{2} \varphi^{(p_x)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}
	\right) \\		f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}})
	&= f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \left(
	\varphi^{(s^*)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} - \varphi^{(d)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} + \sqrt{2} \varphi^{(p_y)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}
	\right) \\
	f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}})
	&= f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') \left(
	\varphi^{(s^*)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} - \varphi^{(d)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'} - \sqrt{2} \varphi^{(p_y)}_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}
	\right)
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, defining:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	f^{(s)} &\equiv f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}) \\
	f^{(s^*)} &\equiv f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}) + f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}) + f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}) + f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}) \\
	f^{(p_x)} &\equiv f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}) - f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}) \\
	f^{(p_y)} &\equiv f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}) - f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}}) \\
	f^{(d)} &\equiv f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{x}}) + f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{x}}) - f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}+\underline{\mathbf{y}}) - f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}-\underline{\mathbf{y}})
\end{aligned}
\]
we get the decomposition:
\[
	f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') = \sum_\gamma f^{(\gamma)} \varphi_{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}'}^{(\gamma)}
\]
We decomposed the original function in its harmonics. Moving to reciprocal space, we get the $\mathcal{F}$-transformed functions of Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}, also satisfying the orthonormality condition:
\[
	\frac{1}{L_x L_y} \sum_\mathbf{k} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)} \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma')*} = \delta_{\gamma\gamma'}
\]
The functions of Tab.~\ref{tab:x-wave-reciprocal-factors} are plotted in the BZ in Fig.~\ref{fig:x-wave-reciprocal-factors}.

We will refer to these harmonics as ``symmetry channels'', indicating the various spatial components of the order parameter associated to a given phase transition. As we will see, indeed, we consider order parameters that have specific transformation properties under planar rotations. To distinguish the various spatial harmonics of the order parameter is useful in order to apply symmetry-based selection rules.

Finally, a very useful mathematical identity is given by the following argument. Consider Eq.~\eqref{eq:HV-fourier}, where is present the factor $\cos \left( \delta k_x \right)	+ \cos \left( \delta k_y \right)$ (with $\delta k_x = k_x-k_x'$ and $\delta k_y = k_y-k_y'$). Expanding,
\[
	\cos \left( \delta k_x \right)	+ \cos \left( \delta k_y \right) = c_x c_x' + c_y c_y' + s_x s_x' + s_y s_y'
\]
In last passage, for the sake of readability, we used the notations
\[
	c_\ell \equiv \cos k_\ell
	\qquad
	s_\ell \equiv \sin k_\ell
	\qquad
	c_\ell' \equiv \cos	k_\ell'
	\qquad
	s_\ell' \equiv \sin k_\ell'
\]
Group the four terms,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sym-asym-couplings-gap}
	\underbrace{
		\left(c_x c_x' + c_y c_y' \right) 
	}_\text{Symmetric}
	+ \underbrace{
		\left(s_x s_x' + s_y s_y' \right)
	}_\text{Anti-symmetric}
\end{equation}
All four terms are invariant under simultaneous inversion of both momenta, $(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}') \to (-\mathbf{k},-\mathbf{k}')$. Instead, if we invert just one momentum of the pair, the first two are symmetric for both arguments $\mathbf{k}$, $\mathbf{k}'$; the second two are anti-symmetric. Decoupling the symmetric part, we obtain
\[
	c_x c_x' + c_y c_y' = \frac{1}{2} (c_x + c_y)(c_x' + c_y') + \frac{1}{2} (c_x - c_y)(c_x' - c_y')
\]
which finally gives:
\[
\begin{aligned}
	\cos \left( \delta k_x \right)	+ \cos \left( \delta k_y  \right) &= \frac{1}{2}  (c_x + c_y)(c_x' + c_y') &&\qquad\text{($s^*$-wave)} \\
	&\hphantom{=}+ s_x s_x' &&\qquad\text{($p_x$-wave)} \\
	&\hphantom{=}+ s_y s_y' &&\qquad\text{($p_y$-wave)} \\
	&\hphantom{=}+ \frac{1}{2} (c_x - c_y)(c_x' - c_y') &&\qquad\text{($d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave)}
\end{aligned}
\]
or, in a more compact form	
\begin{align} 
	\cos \left( \delta k_x \right)	+ \cos \left( \delta k_y  \right) &= \frac 1 2 \sum_\gamma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)} \varphi_{\mathbf{k}'}^{(\gamma)*}
	\qq{for $\gamma \in \lbrace s^*, p_x, p_y, d_{x^2-y^2} \rbrace$} \label{eq:coscos-symmetries-decomposition} \\
	&= \frac 1 2 \sum_\gamma \varphi_\mathbf{k}^{(\gamma)*} \varphi_{\mathbf{k}'}^{(\gamma)}
\end{align}
This decomposition will result particularly handy in later calculations.
